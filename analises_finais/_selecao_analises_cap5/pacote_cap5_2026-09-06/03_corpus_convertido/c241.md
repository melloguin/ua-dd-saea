# A Performance Indicator Based Infill Criterion for Expensive Multi-/Many-objective Optimization 

Shufen Qin, Chaoli Sun, _Senior Member, IEEE,_ Qiqi Liu, Yaochu Jin, _Fellow, IEEE_ . . 

**_Abstract_ —In surrogate-assisted multi-/many-objective evolutionary optimization, each solution normally has an approximated value on each objective, resulting in increased difficulties in selecting solutions for expensive objective evaluations due to complicated trade-off between different objectives and accumulated uncertainty in the approximation of the objective functions. Thus, it is highly challenging to design an efficient model management strategy for surrogate-assisted expensive multi-/many-objective optimization. In this paper, a surrogate model is built for each objective function, based on which a set of promising candidate solutions are found. Additionally, a Gaussian process model is constructed to approximate a newly designed performance indicator measuring both convergence and diversity properties of individual solutions. Finally, the solution of the found candidate solutions having the maximum expected improvement in terms of the performance indicator is selected for evaluation using the expensive objective functions. Comparative experiments are conducted on 3-, 5-, and 10-objective DTLZ, WFG, and MaF test functions, as well as two real-world applications. The experimental results show that the proposed method is competitive compared to five state-of-the-art surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms for expensive multi-/many-objective optimization.** 

**_Index Terms_ —Computationally expensive multi-/manyobjective optimization, model management, performance indicator, infill criterion.** 

## I. INTRODUCTION 

N many real-world optimization problems, such as airfoil **I** design [1], furnace optimization [2], design of crude oil distillation units [3], and car cab design [4], [5], multiple objectives, which are normally conflicting with each other, must be optimized simultaneously. In evolutionary optimization, it is usually known as multi-objective optimization when the number of objectives is two or three, and many-objective optimization when the number of objectives is more than three [6], [7]. Different to the single-objective optimization where usually only one global optimal solution exists, in multi-/many-objective case, there is a set of optimal tradeoff solutions. These solutions are called the Pareto optimal set (PS) in the decision space, and their projection in the objective

Over the past decades, it has been shown that meta-heuristic algorithms, including evolutionary algorithms (EAs) and swarm intelligence algorithms, are well suited for solving multi-/manyobjective optimization problems because those population based metaheuristic algorithms are able to obtain a set of diverse and well-converged solutions in one single optimization run [9], [10]. Many multi-/many-objective optimization algorithms can be found in the literature, which can be roughly classified into three categories, namely dominance-based EAs, decomposition-based EAs, and indicator-based EAs. Representative dominance-based EAs include the fast elitist nondominated sorting genetic algorithm NSGA-II [11], the improved strength Pareto evolutionary algorithm SPEA2 [12] and the _ϵ_ -dominance based evolutionary algorithm [13]. The decomposition-based approach transforms a multi-/manyobjective problem into a set of sub-problems with weight vectors or reference vectors, and the resulting sub-problems will be simultaneously optimized. MOEA/D [14], MOEA/DM2M [15], MPSO/DD [16], and RVEA [17] are popular decomposition-based EAs, among others. The indicator-based methods rely on a performance indicator for environmental selection. Some commonly used indicator-based optimization algorithms include the hypervolume-based search algorithm (HyPE) [18], the R2-indicator based evolutionary multi-objective algorithm (R2-EMOA) [19], the indicator based multi-objective evolutionary algorithm with reference point adaptation (AR-MOEA) [20], and the convergence and diversity indicators based many-objective algorithm (NSPIEMO) [21]. Note that some multi-/many-objective algorithms may not fall into any of the above three categories. For example, in [4], [22] and [23], both the dominance-based and decomposition-based methods are adopted. Regardless of which category it belongs to, an EA often requires a large number of objective evaluations to find a set of diverse and well-converged non-dominated solutions for solving multi/many-objective optimization problems, thus preventing EAs from being applied to solve expensive optimization problems that are usually evaluated at the cost of a large amount of computational time. To address the above problem, surrogate models have been proposed for assisting EAs to solve expensive optimization problems [24], [25], since the computational complexity to train a surrogate model is usually much smaller than that for evaluating the expensive objective functions. Commonly used surrogate models include polynomial regression (PR) [26], Gaussian process (GP) [27], [28], radial basis function (RBF) [29], support vector machines (SVM) [30], and artificial neural networks (ANN) [31]. - 1) The surrogate models are trained for approximating the original expensive objective functions. Most existing work on the expensive multi-/many-objective optimization belongs to this category. Emmerich et al. In [34], a surrogate is constructed for each objective to estimate the hypervolume, based on which expected improvement (EI) is maximized to find a promising solution for expensive evaluation. Ponweister et al. [35] employed a similar idea as in [34] except that the solution for expensive objective evaluation is found by optimizing the _S_ -metric, which is calculated on the lower confidence bound of a GP model for each objective function. In [36], all evaluated solutions are grouped into a number of clusters using the fuzzy C-means method, and a surrogate model is trained for each objective using samples from each cluster. The models of a solution’s closest fuzzy cluster will be used to approximate the objective values. The EI of the aggregation objective functions will then be maximized to find a solution in each cluster for expensive evaluation. Zhan et al. [37] also proposed to construct a GP model for each objective and optimize the scalarizing EI matrix (EIM) to select the best solution for expensive function evaluations. Different to using a single-objective optimization to find an infilling sample, Jeong et al. [38] suggested to optimize the EI of each objective based on a GP model and then the non-dominated solutions sorted according to the EI value of each objective will be selected for expensive evaluations. Recently, new model management strategies extended from infill criteria have been proposed. Chugh et al. [28] trained a GP model for each objective and selected individuals for expensive objective evaluations based on the diversity and convergence of the current population. Wang et al. [39] trained GP models for expensive multi-objective functions and put forward an adaptive sampling selection criterion switching between an angle based distance and an angle-penalized distance for selecting _µ_ solutions to be evaluated using the expensive objective functions. Li et al. [40] proposed to train an RBF model for each objective function, and two infill criteria are utilized in a collaborative manner for selecting promising solutions for expensive evaluation after each round of model-based optimization. Guo et al. [41] proposed a heterogeneous surrogate ensemble for each objective to replace the Gaussian process for

[42] trained four surrogate models for each objective function and chose the one with the minimum root mean square error to approximate the value of each objective function. The interior-point algorithm is adopted to generate high-quality solutions for expensive evaluations. By contrast, Zhao et al. [26] used the average output of three models to approximate each objective and proposed a new method to measure the uncertainty of the approximation, which will be used as the infill criterion when the population convergence is not good. 

- 2) The surrogate models are trained for predicting the scalarizing functions of the original expensive multiobjective optimization problems. To the best of our knowledge, not many methods fall into this category. Knowles [43] first proposed to train a GP model for the aggregated Tchebycheff function using a set of random weights, then maximize the EI to select one solution for expensive objective evaluations, which can also be seen as an extension of Bayesian optimization to multiobjective optimization. Lim et al. [44] adopted a polynomial and an ensemble to simultaneously approximate the local landscapes of the weighting sum-based aggregated objective functions, and the optimal solutions found by the local search on the two surrogate models will be evaluated using the expensive objective functions. 

- 3) The surrogate is used to predict the dominance relationship between the solutions. In [45], an SVM based surrogate is used to predict non-dominated ranking of the offspring solutions, and those on the first front will be selected for expensive evaluations. Pan et al. [31] proposed to use a feedforward neural network to predict the dominance relationship between the candidates and a group of reference solutions, then select solutions based on the uncertainty information together with the dominance relationship for expensive evaluations. 

Infill criteria have been the most popular and most effective model management strategies in surrogate-assisted multiobjective optimization. Note, however, that infill criteria were meant for single-objective optimization, and when they are extended to multi-objective optimization, the performance may degrade even if some additional measures for promoting diversity are introduced. Roughly speaking, two main categories of infill criteria are used in GP-assisted multi-objective evolutionary optimization. The first category adopts an infill criterion for single-objective optimization after converting a multi-objective optimization problem into a single-objective one using a scalarizing function [34], [43], [46], [47]. However, these infill criteria designed for single-objective optimization aim to balance exploration and exploitation during the search without explicitly considering the diversity of the solution set, although it is true that the diversity is taken into account in the definition of the subproblems. Furthermore, the performance of the GP model for estimating the uncertainty level may become unreliable when the amount of training data is highly limited [48]. This problem may become even worse as the number of objectives increases in many-objective optimization since the error in estimating the uncertainty information and the objective values may accumulate. 

The infill criteria in the second category normally choose solutions for expensive evaluations from the candidate solutions found on the basis of the surrogate models [28], [38], [39]. However, the infill criteria used in these algorithms are based also on the uncertainty information and the approximated objective value of each objective. Thus, errors in estimating the uncertainty information and the objective values may mislead the selection of the right solutions for expensive evaluations. 

In this work, a new performance indicator that takes both the diversity and convergence of the population into account, is proposed and integrated into the EI infill criterion to select an individual for expensive objective function evaluations. 

The empirical studies indicate that the surrogate management strategy using the performance indicator as the infill criterion is effective in handling trade-offs between exploration and exploitation, and convergence and diversity as well. The main contributions of this work are summarized below. 

- 1) A new surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm composed of three stages for expensive multi-/manyobjective optimization, called EMMOEA, is proposed. In the first stage, which is termed model-based optimization, a GP surrogate model is built for each objective function, based on which a population of promising solutions is found. In the second stage, a separate GP surrogate is trained to approximate a new performance indicator. At last, an infill criterion for selecting a solution from the set of solutions obtained in the modelbased optimization for expensive objective evaluation. This way, surrogate management can be directly carried out without being influenced by the propagation of the errors in approximating multiple objective functions and in estimating the uncertainty levels of the approximated objective values. 

- 2) A new performance indicator measuring the convergence and diversity of individual solutions in the objective space is proposed. The convergence is measured according to a solution’s Euclidean distance to the ideal point in the normalized objective space. Meanwhile, the diversity of the solution is measured by the crowdedness of this solution in the normalized objective space, which is defined to be the minimum Euclidean distance to its neighbors. 

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II provides a detailed description of the proposed method. The parameter settings, experimental results and discussions of the results are presented in Section III. Finally, a conclusion is drawn and future work is outlined in Section IV. 

## II. PROPOSED ALGORITHM 

## _A. Overall Framework_ 

As previously discussed, existing methods for adapting the infill criteria to multi-/many-objective optimization may suffer from performance degradation when the number of objectives increases. In this work, we propose a new performance indicator to more effectively balance convergence and diversity. Then we build a GP surrogate model to approximate the proposed performance indicator for choosing one solution for expensive evaluation. Instead of directly optimizing the infill criterion, a population of candidate solutions will be found by searching for non-dominated solutions on the basis of the surrogate models, each approximating one objective function. The separate search for promising solutions aims to find solutions located in a promising sub-space of the multi/many-objective optimization problem so that a set of highquality non-dominated solutions can be found given a limited computational budget.

## _B. Sensitivity analysis of gmax_ 

Model-based optimization is used to find a set of promising solutions for model management by searching for optimal solutions of the surrogate models. Here, we need to specify the maximum number of generations ( _gmax_ ) to be run before the solutions are used for model management. The solutions in the optimal candidate population found based on the model are expected to have a good convergence performance for the computationally expensive problem. However, it should be mentioned that surrogate models may mislead the search due to their approximation errors. Therefore, the number of iterations for the model-based search plays an important role in expensive multi-/many-objective optimization. To set a proper maximum number of iterations for the model-based optimization, we investigate the performance of the proposed algorithm on the MaF test problems [53] using different values of _gmax_ . Note that MaF8 and MaF9 are limited only to two-dimensional problems, while MaF14 and MaF15 are large-scale problems, which, therefore, are not used in the experiments. 

Table I gives the statistical results in terms of IGD on the MaF test instances when _gmax_ = 1 (denoted as MO-g1), _gmax_ = 5 (denoted as MO-g5), _gmax_ = 10 (denoted as MO-g10), _gmax_ = 15 (denoted as MO-g15), _gmax_ = 20 (denoted as MO-g20), _gmax_ = 30 (denoted as MO-g30), and _gmax_ = 50 (denoted as MO-g50). The resulting ranks of the performances with different _gmax_ values are also listed in Table I and the mean ranks are listed at the bottom of the table. From these results, we can see that the performance of EMMOEA with _gmax_ = 30 ranks best. Note, however, the performance is relatively insensitive when _gmax_ changes from 10 to 50. In the following experiments, we adopt _gmax_ = 10 for the model-based optimization to reduce computation time as much as possible. 

## _C. Contribution of model-based optimization_ 

In the model-based optimization of EMMOEA, a surrogate model will be trained for each objective, and the optimal solutions of the surrogate models will be searched by an evolutionary algorithm. Note that only the objective functions are considered in the model-based optimization in EMMOEA. Thus, the adopted model-based optimization are expected to find a set of solutions that locate close at the true nondominated front, in which one solution with better convergence and diversity will be selected to be evaluated using the expensive objective function. As an ablation study, we modified the objective function of the model-based optimization so that only the acquisition function is optimized, and the optimal solution of this acquisition is evaluated for expensive functions. This variant is denoted as EMMOEA-IP. The proposed performance 

indicator in the model management can measure the convergence and diversity of a solution. Thus, we also train a GP surrogate model for the performance indicator _IP_ as done in model management, and the EI [61] based acquisition function is optimized for choosing a solution to be evaluated using the expensive objective function. A number of experiments are conducted on MaF test problems with 10 objectives by EMMOEA-IP and EMMOEA, respectively, and the HV and IGD statistical results are given in Table II. From Table II, we can see that the proposed EMMOEA can significantly perform better on eight and six of 11 MaF functions than EMMOEA-IP with regard to HV and IGD results, respectively. The results show that, compared to optimizing the EI value of the performance indicator, searching for a set of optimal solutions of the surrogate models approximating the objective functions is more effective for model management. 

## _D. Contribution of Performance Indicator Based Model Management_ 

In this work, we build a separate GP model to approximate the proposed performance indicator measuring both convergence and diversity of each solution. To demonstrate its benefit, we compare it with a common approach that directly performs model management based on the GP models of the objective functions. There are different approaches to applying the infill criterion based model management based on the GP models of the objective functions. Zhan et al. [37] proposed an EI matrix (EIM) for managing surrogates in multi-objective optimization, where multiple EI values are provided on each objective based on the non-dominated solutions in the training set. In order to evaluate the efficiency of the proposed model management strategy, we conduct a number of experiments on the MaF test problems that compares the results to a variant of EMMOEA, in which the EIM is adopted for selecting solutions from the candidate solutions found by the modelbased optimization. Table III presents the comparative results in terms of IGD and HV of the EMMOEA and its variant, denoted as EMMOEA-EIM, on the 10-objective MaF test problems. From Table III, we can see that the proposed EMMOEA significantly outperforms its EIM variant on seven and six of 11 functions in terms of HV and IGD, respectively. These results suggest that separating the search of the candidate solutions from model management is beneficial in surrogate-assisted multi-/many-objective optimization. In the Supplementary material, we also make a comparison with SMS-EGO [35] on 3-objective DTLZ1-7 test problems. Table I of the Supplementary material presents the statistical results (including IGD<sup>+</sup> , HV, and IGD) obtained by our proposed method and SMS-EGO. From Table I of the Supplementary material, we can see that the proposed method outperforms SMS-EGO.

From Table VII, we can see that EMMOEA performs better than CSEA, K-RVEA, HSMEA, MOEA/DEGO, and EIM on 56, 51, 49, 63 and 46 out of 69 problems, while it is inferior to them only on 2, 4, 10, 4 and 10 problems, respectively. Thus, we can draw the conclusion that the proposed method is very competitive for expensive multi/many-objective problems. To better understand this, we took a closer look at the performance of the algorithm on problems with different characteristics for the Pareto front. From these results, we find that the proposed method is more efficient for solving problems with a concave PF, such as DTLZ2-4, MaF2, WFG4-9 and WFG13. However, the advantage of the proposed algorithm will become weaker on problems with a convex Pareto front. For example, EMMOEA obtains better results on one out of three instances of the MaF3 and WFG1 problems that have a convex Pareto front. This might be attributed to the fact that an MOP with a concave front may benefit more from the preferences over solutions located in the center of the front and close to the ideal point.

The model-based optimization aims to search for a set of promising solutions, from which the model management will choose one to be evaluated using the expensive objective function. Instead of using the surrogates for the objective functions, a GP surrogate model is built to approximate a scalar performance indicator account for both the diversity and convergence, so that an infill criterion can be applied. The experimental results on DTLZ, MaF and WFG test suites and two real-world applications confirm that our proposed EMMOEA is highly competitive for expensive multi-/manyobjective optimization problems. 

Note, however, that EMMOEA needs to build a large number ( _m_ +1 for _m_ -objective problems) of Gaussian process models, making it less suited for solving high-dimensional or large-scale multi-/many-objective optimization problems due to the cubic computational complexity of the Gaussian process models. An analysis of the computational time required by the algorithms under comparison is given in the Supplementary material. Our future work will be dedicated to developing computationally more scalable Gaussian process modelling 

14 

techniques. Furthermore, other computationally more efficient surrogate models will be considered in combination of advanced machine learning such as semi-supervised learning and transfer learning to achieve high-quality approximation of complex objective functions when the data is sparse. Experimental results have shown EMMOEA is competitive for solving problems with concave Pareto fronts but relatively weak in solving problems with convex, degenerated or disconnected Pareto fronts. To address this limitation, our future work will consider using the distance to the nadir point in calculating the performance indicator and adapting the reference vectors during the optimization. The performance of EMMOEA may also deteriorate as the number of decision variables increases, and the computational complexity will increase when the amount of training samples becomes large. These may be alleviated by replacing the Gaussian process with a computationally more scalable model such as a Bayesian neural network. Finally, the proposed algorithm is developed for continuous optimization problems and cannot directly be extended to discrete optimization problems. Thus, methods for solving large-scale and discrete optimization problems will be considered in our future work.

A Performance Indicator Based Infill Criterion for
Expensive Multi-/Many-objective Optimization
Shufen Qin, Chaoli Sun, Senior Member, IEEE, Qiqi Liu, Yaochu Jin, Fellow, IEEE . .
Abstract—In surrogate-assisted multi-/many-objective evolu-
tionary optimization, each solution normally has an approxi-
mated value on each objective, resulting in increased difficulties in
selecting solutions for expensive objective evaluations due to com-
plicated trade-off between different objectives and accumulated
uncertainty in the approximation of the objective functions. Thus,
it is highly challenging to design an efficient model management
strategy for surrogate-assisted expensive multi-/many-objective
optimization. In this paper, a surrogate model is built for each
objective function, based on which a set of promising candidate
solutions are found. Additionally, a Gaussian process model
is constructed to approximate a newly designed performance
indicator measuring both convergence and diversity properties of
individual solutions. Finally, the solution of the found candidate
solutions having the maximum expected improvement in terms
of the performance indicator is selected for evaluation using
the expensive objective functions. Comparative experiments are
conducted on 3-, 5-, and 10-objective DTLZ, WFG, and MaF
test functions, as well as two real-world applications. The ex-
perimental results show that the proposed method is competitive
compared to five state-of-the-art surrogate-assisted evolutionary
algorithms for expensive multi-/many-objective optimization.

multi-/many-objective case, there is a set of optimal trade-

objective optimization problems because those population
based metaheuristic algorithms are able to obtain a set of
diverse and well-converged solutions in one single optimiza-
tion run [9], [10]. Many multi-/many-objective optimization
algorithms can be found in the literature, which can be roughly
classified into three categories, namely dominance-based EAs,
decomposition-based EAs, and indicator-based EAs. Repre-
sentative dominance-based EAs include the fast elitist non-
dominated sorting genetic algorithm NSGA-II [11], the im-
proved strength Pareto evolutionary algorithm SPEA2 [12]
and the ϵ-dominance based evolutionary algorithm [13].
The decomposition-based approach transforms a multi-/many-
objective problem into a set of sub-problems with weight
vectors or reference vectors, and the resulting sub-problems
will be simultaneously optimized. MOEA/D [14], MOEA/D-
M2M [15], MPSO/DD [16], and RVEA [17] are popular
decomposition-based EAs, among others. The indicator-based
methods rely on a performance indicator for environmen-
tal selection. Some commonly used indicator-based opti-
mization algorithms include the hypervolume-based search
algorithm (HyPE) [18], the R2-indicator based evolution-
ary multi-objective algorithm (R2-EMOA) [19], the indicator
based multi-objective evolutionary algorithm with reference
point adaptation (AR-MOEA) [20], and the convergence and
diversity indicators based many-objective algorithm (NSPI-
EMO) [21]. Note that some multi-/many-objective algorithms
may not fall into any of the above three categories. For
example, in [4], [22] and [23], both the dominance-based
and decomposition-based methods are adopted. Regardless of
which category it belongs to, an EA often requires a large
number of objective evaluations to find a set of diverse and
well-converged non-dominated solutions for solving multi-
/many-objective optimization problems, thus preventing EAs
from being applied to solve expensive optimization problems
that are usually evaluated at the cost of a large amount of
computational time. To address the above problem, surrogate
models have been proposed for assisting EAs to solve expen-
sive optimization problems [24], [25], since the computational
complexity to train a surrogate model is usually much smaller
than that for evaluating the expensive objective functions.
Commonly used surrogate models include polynomial regres-
sion (PR) [26], Gaussian process (GP) [27], [28], radial basis
function (RBF) [29], support vector machines (SVM) [30],
and artificial neural networks (ANN) [31]. Recently devel-

1) The surrogate models are trained for approximating the

promising estimated lower bounds for evaluation using
the expensive objective functions. In [34], a surrogate

maximized to find a promising solution for expensive
evaluation. Ponweister et al. [35] employed a similar
idea as in [34] except that the solution for expensive
objective evaluation is found by optimizing the S-metric,
which is calculated on the lower confidence bound of
a GP model for each objective function. In [36], all
evaluated solutions are grouped into a number of clusters
using the fuzzy C-means method, and a surrogate model
is trained for each objective using samples from each
cluster. The models of a solution’s closest fuzzy cluster
will be used to approximate the objective values. The
EI of the aggregation objective functions will then
be maximized to find a solution in each cluster for
expensive evaluation. Zhan et al. [37] also proposed to
construct a GP model for each objective and optimize
the scalarizing EI matrix (EIM) to select the best so-

using a single-objective optimization to find an infilling
sample, Jeong et al. [38] suggested to optimize the
EI of each objective based on a GP model and then
the non-dominated solutions sorted according to the EI
value of each objective will be selected for expensive
evaluations. Recently, new model management strategies
extended from infill criteria have been proposed. Chugh
et al. [28] trained a GP model for each objective and
selected individuals for expensive objective evaluations
based on the diversity and convergence of the current
population. Wang et al. [39] trained GP models for
expensive multi-objective functions and put forward an
adaptive sampling selection criterion switching between
an angle based distance and an angle-penalized distance

expensive objective functions. Li et al. [40] proposed to
train an RBF model for each objective function, and two
infill criteria are utilized in a collaborative manner for
selecting promising solutions for expensive evaluation
after each round of model-based optimization. Guo et
al. [41] proposed a heterogeneous surrogate ensemble
for each objective to replace the Gaussian process for
high-dimensional multi-objective optimization problems
and the LCB and EI criteria are used for sampling.
Habib et al. [42] trained four surrogate models for each
objective function and chose the one with the minimum
root mean square error to approximate the value of
each objective function. The interior-point algorithm is
adopted to generate high-quality solutions for expensive
evaluations. By contrast, Zhao et al. [26] used the
average output of three models to approximate each
objective and proposed a new method to measure the
uncertainty of the approximation, which will be used as
the infill criterion when the population convergence is
not good.
2) The surrogate models are trained for predicting the
scalarizing functions of the original expensive multi-
objective optimization problems. To the best of our
knowledge, not many methods fall into this category.
Knowles [43] first proposed to train a GP model for the
aggregated Tchebycheff function using a set of random
weights, then maximize the EI to select one solution
for expensive objective evaluations, which can also be
seen as an extension of Bayesian optimization to multi-
objective optimization. Lim et al. [44] adopted a polyno-
mial and an ensemble to simultaneously approximate the
local landscapes of the weighting sum-based aggregated
objective functions, and the optimal solutions found by
the local search on the two surrogate models will be
evaluated using the expensive objective functions.
3) The surrogate is used to predict the dominance rela-
tionship between the solutions. In [45], an SVM based
surrogate is used to predict non-dominated ranking of
the offspring solutions, and those on the first front will
be selected for expensive evaluations. Pan et al. [31]
proposed to use a feedforward neural network to predict
the dominance relationship between the candidates and
a group of reference solutions, then select solutions
based on the uncertainty information together with the
dominance relationship for expensive evaluations.

Roughly speaking, two main categories of infill criteria are
used in GP-assisted multi-objective evolutionary optimization.
The first category adopts an infill criterion for single-objective
optimization after converting a multi-objective optimization
problem into a single-objective one using a scalarizing func-
tion [34], [43], [46], [47]. However, these infill criteria de-
signed for single-objective optimization aim to balance ex-
ploration and exploitation during the search without explicitly
considering the diversity of the solution set, although it is true
that the diversity is taken into account in the definition of the
subproblems. Furthermore, the performance of the GP model
for estimating the uncertainty level may become unreliable
when the amount of training data is highly limited [48]. This
problem may become even worse as the number of objectives
increases in many-objective optimization since the error in
estimating the uncertainty information and the objective values
may accumulate.
The infill criteria in the second category normally choose so-
lutions for expensive evaluations from the candidate solutions
found on the basis of the surrogate models [28], [38], [39].
However, the infill criteria used in these algorithms are based
also on the uncertainty information and the approximated
objective value of each objective. Thus, errors in estimating the
uncertainty information and the objective values may mislead
the selection of the right solutions for expensive evaluations.
In this work, a new performance indicator that takes both the
diversity and convergence of the population into account, is
proposed and integrated into the EI infill criterion to select
an individual for expensive objective function evaluations.
The empirical studies indicate that the surrogate management
strategy using the performance indicator as the infill criterion
is effective in handling trade-offs between exploration and
exploitation, and convergence and diversity as well. The main
contributions of this work are summarized below.
1) A new surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm com-
posed of three stages for expensive multi-/many-
objective optimization, called EMMOEA, is proposed.
In the first stage, which is termed model-based optimiza-
tion, a GP surrogate model is built for each objective
function, based on which a population of promising
solutions is found. In the second stage, a separate GP
surrogate is trained to approximate a new performance
indicator. At last, an infill criterion for selecting a
solution from the set of solutions obtained in the model-
based optimization for expensive objective evaluation.
This way, surrogate management can be directly carried
out without being influenced by the propagation of the
errors in approximating multiple objective functions and
in estimating the uncertainty levels of the approximated
objective values.
2) A new performance indicator measuring the convergence
and diversity of individual solutions in the objective
space is proposed. The convergence is measured ac-
cording to a solution’s Euclidean distance to the ideal
point in the normalized objective space. Meanwhile, the
diversity of the solution is measured by the crowdedness
of this solution in the normalized objective space, which
is defined to be the minimum Euclidean distance to its
neighbors.

The parameter settings, experimental results and discussions
of the results are presented in Section III. Finally, a conclusion
is drawn and future work is outlined in Section IV.
II. PROPOSED ALGORITHM
A. Overall Framework
As previously discussed, existing methods for adapting the
infill criteria to multi-/many-objective optimization may suffer
from performance degradation when the number of objectives
increases. In this work, we propose a new performance indi-
cator to more effectively balance convergence and diversity.
Then we build a GP surrogate model to approximate the
proposed performance indicator for choosing one solution
for expensive evaluation. Instead of directly optimizing the
infill criterion, a population of candidate solutions will be
found by searching for non-dominated solutions on the basis
of the surrogate models, each approximating one objective
function. The separate search for promising solutions aims to
find solutions located in a promising sub-space of the multi-
/many-objective optimization problem so that a set of high-
quality non-dominated solutions can be found given a limited
computational budget.

B. Sensitivity analysis of gmax
Model-based optimization is used to find a set of promising
solutions for model management by searching for optimal
solutions of the surrogate models. Here, we need to specify the
maximum number of generations (gmax) to be run before the
solutions are used for model management. The solutions in
the optimal candidate population found based on the model
are expected to have a good convergence performance for
the computationally expensive problem. However, it should
be mentioned that surrogate models may mislead the search
due to their approximation errors. Therefore, the number
of iterations for the model-based search plays an important
role in expensive multi-/many-objective optimization. To set
a proper maximum number of iterations for the model-based
optimization, we investigate the performance of the proposed
algorithm on the MaF test problems [53] using different
values of gmax. Note that MaF8 and MaF9 are limited only
to two-dimensional problems, while MaF14 and MaF15 are
large-scale problems, which, therefore, are not used in the
experiments.
Table I gives the statistical results in terms of IGD on the
MaF test instances when gmax = 1 (denoted as MO-g1),
gmax = 5 (denoted as MO-g5), gmax = 10 (denoted as
MO-g10), gmax = 15 (denoted as MO-g15), gmax = 20
(denoted as MO-g20), gmax = 30 (denoted as MO-g30), and
gmax = 50 (denoted as MO-g50). The resulting ranks of the
performances with different gmax values are also listed in
Table I and the mean ranks are listed at the bottom of the
table. From these results, we can see that the performance of
EMMOEA with gmax = 30 ranks best. Note, however, the
performance is relatively insensitive when gmax changes from
10 to 50. In the following experiments, we adopt gmax = 10
for the model-based optimization to reduce computation time
as much as possible.
C. Contribution of model-based optimization
In the model-based optimization of EMMOEA, a surrogate
model will be trained for each objective, and the optimal
solutions of the surrogate models will be searched by an
evolutionary algorithm. Note that only the objective functions
are considered in the model-based optimization in EMMOEA.
Thus, the adopted model-based optimization are expected to
find a set of solutions that locate close at the true non-
dominated front, in which one solution with better convergence
and diversity will be selected to be evaluated using the expen-
sive objective function. As an ablation study, we modified the
objective function of the model-based optimization so that only
the acquisition function is optimized, and the optimal solution
of this acquisition is evaluated for expensive functions. This
variant is denoted as EMMOEA-IP. The proposed performance
indicator in the model management can measure the conver-
gence and diversity of a solution. Thus, we also train a GP
surrogate model for the performance indicator IP as done in
model management, and the EI [61] based acquisition function
is optimized for choosing a solution to be evaluated using
the expensive objective function. A number of experiments
are conducted on MaF test problems with 10 objectives by
EMMOEA-IP and EMMOEA, respectively, and the HV and
IGD statistical results are given in Table II. From Table II,
we can see that the proposed EMMOEA can significantly
perform better on eight and six of 11 MaF functions than
EMMOEA-IP with regard to HV and IGD results, respectively.
The results show that, compared to optimizing the EI value
of the performance indicator, searching for a set of optimal
solutions of the surrogate models approximating the objective
functions is more effective for model management.
D. Contribution of Performance Indicator Based Model Man-
agement
In this work, we build a separate GP model to approx-
imate the proposed performance indicator measuring both
convergence and diversity of each solution. To demonstrate its
benefit, we compare it with a common approach that directly
performs model management based on the GP models of the
objective functions. There are different approaches to applying
the infill criterion based model management based on the GP
models of the objective functions. Zhan et al. [37] proposed
an EI matrix (EIM) for managing surrogates in multi-objective
optimization, where multiple EI values are provided on each
objective based on the non-dominated solutions in the training
set. In order to evaluate the efficiency of the proposed model
management strategy, we conduct a number of experiments on
the MaF test problems that compares the results to a variant
of EMMOEA, in which the EIM is adopted for selecting
solutions from the candidate solutions found by the model-
based optimization. Table III presents the comparative results
in terms of IGD and HV of the EMMOEA and its variant,
denoted as EMMOEA-EIM, on the 10-objective MaF test
problems. From Table III, we can see that the proposed
EMMOEA significantly outperforms its EIM variant on seven
and six of 11 functions in terms of HV and IGD, respec-
tively. These results suggest that separating the search of the
candidate solutions from model management is beneficial in
surrogate-assisted multi-/many-objective optimization. In the
Supplementary material, we also make a comparison with
SMS-EGO [35] on 3-objective DTLZ1-7 test problems. Table
I of the Supplementary material presents the statistical results
(including IGD+, HV, and IGD) obtained by our proposed
method and SMS-EGO. From Table I of the Supplementary
material, we can see that the proposed method outperforms
SMS-EGO.

quality non-dominated solutions within a limited computation
budget. The model-based optimization aims to search for a set
of promising solutions, from which the model management
will choose one to be evaluated using the expensive objective
function. Instead of using the surrogates for the objective
functions, a GP surrogate model is built to approximate a
scalar performance indicator account for both the diversity
and convergence, so that an infill criterion can be applied.
The experimental results on DTLZ, MaF and WFG test suites
and two real-world applications confirm that our proposed
EMMOEA is highly competitive for expensive multi-/many-
objective optimization problems.
Note, however, that EMMOEA needs to build a large
number (m+1 for m-objective problems) of Gaussian process
models, making it less suited for solving high-dimensional or
large-scale multi-/many-objective optimization problems due
to the cubic computational complexity of the Gaussian process
models. An analysis of the computational time required by the
algorithms under comparison is given in the Supplementary
material. Our future work will be dedicated to developing
computationally more scalable Gaussian process modelling

techniques. Furthermore, other computationally more efficient
surrogate models will be considered in combination of ad-
vanced machine learning such as semi-supervised learning
and transfer learning to achieve high-quality approximation of
complex objective functions when the data is sparse. Experi-
mental results have shown EMMOEA is competitive for solv-
ing problems with concave Pareto fronts but relatively weak
in solving problems with convex, degenerated or disconnected
Pareto fronts. To address this limitation, our future work will
consider using the distance to the nadir point in calculating
the performance indicator and adapting the reference vec-
tors during the optimization. The performance of EMMOEA
may also deteriorate as the number of decision variables
increases, and the computational complexity will increase
when the amount of training samples becomes large. These
may be alleviated by replacing the Gaussian process with
a computationally more scalable model such as a Bayesian
neural network. Finally, the proposed algorithm is developed
for continuous optimization problems and cannot directly be
extended to discrete optimization problems. Thus, methods for
solving large-scale and discrete optimization problems will be
considered in our future work.
