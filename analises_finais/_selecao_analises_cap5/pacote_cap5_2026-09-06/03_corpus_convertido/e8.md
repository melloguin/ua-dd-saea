Text S1: Description of hierarchical ligand construction approach A series of hierarchical rules were used to combinatorially enumerate a large space of monodentate and bidentate ligands. These consist of i) heterocycle and metal-coordinating atom selection, ii) modification of non-metal-coordinating portions of the ring, iii) (optional) ring fusion to form symmetric or asymmetric bidentate ligands, and iv) sequential functionalization on a site of the heterocycle. Specific rules are as follows: 

- Eight core five- or six-membered heterocycles are first defined (labels: A-D for sixmembered rings, E-H for five-membered rings, as shown in Figure S1), each with O- or N-atom coordination to the metal defined as position 1 on the ring. In the O-atom cases (C, D, G, and H), the oxygen always forms single bonds with neighboring carbon atoms in positions 2 and 6, whereas for the N-atom cases (A, B, E, and F), nitrogen forms one single bond and one double bond (between positions 1 and 2) in all heterocycles. Pairs of rings of the same size and coordinating atom are distinguished by the presence or absence of a double bond at the C( _n −_ 1)-C( _n_ ) position. 

- Five modifications to the core rings (labels: a-e, as shown in Figure S1) are carried out centered on C3 and influence the C2-C3-C4 bonds for both five- and six-membered rings to form two-letter-coded ring structures (i.e., Aa-He). These modifications of C2C3-C4 include: two single bonds with a carbon atom (a), a single (2-3) and double (3-4) bond (b), insertion of an oxygen atom at the C3 site (c), insertion of an amine nitrogen (NH) at the C3 site (d), or insertion of a sulfur atom at the C3 site (e). In some cases, modification with a does not modify the core heterocycle (e.g., Aa is A unchanged). 

- Fb or Hb would correspond to a C center with two double bonds at both 3-4 and 4-5 and are excluded. This yields 38 modified, core heterocycles that can be used as scaffolds for forming functionalized monodentate ligands. 

- Bidentate ligands are also formed by joining the two rings at the 2nd position to form a 2-2’ bond. Both symmetric and asymmetric bidentate ligands are formed through every possible pairwise permutation of the 38 unique core rings for a total of (<sup>38+</sup> 2<sup>2</sup><sup>_−_1</sup> ) = 741 bidentate ligand scaffolds. 

- Both monodentate and bidentate ligands are then functionalized hierarchically at the C4 site for both five- and six-membered rings through four steps described next. 

- Methylene or phenyl groups are added either once or twice at the C4 site (referred to as B1 and B2 for the first and second addition, as shown in Figure S2). In the case of methylene group addition, the two hydrogen atoms can be further replaced with a functional group (i.e., S1 or S2, as shown in Figure S2) either by a single doubly bonded functional group (=CH2, =O) or by any two singly bonded groups (-CH3, -H, -OH, and -NH2). For phenyl group addition, this functionalization step is not carried out (i.e., S1 = H). These changes are referred to as S1 when they occur on the B1 functional group chain or S2 for B2. In total, each B1 or B2 site can have 13 chemical identities: 1 phenyl group, 2 doubly-bonded S1 choices, 4 symmetric singly-bonded S1 choices, and 6 asymmetric singly-bonded S1 choices. Combining all possible choices of B1-S1 with B2-S2, as well as the cases where only B1-S1 are generated, yields 14 _∗_ 13 = 182 functional group cores. 

S5 

- Finally, functional groups are terminated with a singly-bonded functional group modification (i.e., T, as shown in Figure S2) at either the para position on the phenyl group or on a terminal hydrogen not involved in S1 or S2 in the case of the methylene groups. The five terminating functional groups are H, CH3, OH, NH2, and Cl. This would combinatorially produce 182 _×_ 5 = 910 functional groups and 897 unique functional groups (i.e., no B2, T = CH3 is the same as methylene B2 and T = H). 

- In total, 741 bidentate and 38 monodentate ligand scaffolds combined with 897 functional groups produces 698,763 unique ligands. After combination with 4 metal identities, the total design space contains 2,795,052 complexes. 

- A note on stereochemistry: since the representation used for this design exercise is based on the molecular graph, we do not distinguish seteroisomers at a design level. When performing DFT simulations, an isomer is chosen with functional groups placed cis, where applicable.

### Text S2: ANN evaluation and DFT timing 

We provide some performance indication of the time-cost for computing properties and uncertainties with the ANN. In order to apply the ANN to the full design space, we apply it in 140 batches of 20k observations each, evaluated serially on a commodity quad core Intel Core i74820K CPU with 64 GB RAM running Ubuntu 14.04.5 LTS and Tensorflow 1.14 (Tensorflow automatically distributes evaluation of the ANN for each batch across CPUs). We report average timings for evaluating the ANN over these 140 batches as reported from the python time module. Evaluation of the ANN call for 20k candidates takes on average 1.6 seconds, while computation of the latent distances take a further 0.8 seconds. File IO takes an average of 1.3 seconds per batch, for an average batch time of 3.7 seconds and a total run time of approximately 9 minutes. Simple bash forking these embarrassingly-parallel evaluations into 8 workers can further reduce the amount of time needed to compute serial parts of the code as well as file IO, reducing the run time to 3.4 minutes. 

DFT evaluation takes 21 hours of compute time on average per geometry optimization and solvent single points per complex on 1 GPU on our local HPC resources, consisting of a mix of Nvidia GTX 970s, GTX 1080Tis and RTX 2080Tis. Therefore, evaluation of one full design space point requires 63 hours, and scoring all 3 million candidates would require 20k GPUyears, intractable even with large-scale parallelization. The observed walltime for one generation of 100 complexes depends on the number of available GPUs for parallel evaluation and was in our case between seven and ten days with up to 64 GPUs in parallel, depending on load on the HPC.

Text S3: Analysis of DFT simulation diversity 

Combining the initial diversity-based clusters (generation 1) and the randomly-sampled data with the four E[I]-generations results in 1000 requested DFT calculations, from which 316 successful unique ∆ _G_ ox(sol) and logP values were converged (Figure S20). The converged complexes cover all 38 constituent heterocycles and 231 (484 requested) unique core ligands of the 779 total considered here (Table S13). The most commonly requested core ligand is HeHe (i.e. a bidentate with two 1,3-oxathiole rings), requested 36 times with 36 distinct functionalizations in increasing frequency during the E[I] process (twice in random data, four times in generation 3 and sixteen times in generation 5) but only leading one successful complex in generation 3, with an Mn metal center and a methallylamine functionalization (∆ _G_ ox(sol) = 7.92 eV, logP = _−_ 5.0 _×_ 10<sup>_−_2</sup> ), narrowly missing the Pareto front of the generation 3 data). A similar core ligand, HcHe (mixing the previous oxathiole with a 1,3-dioxole ring), is also requested 20 times without any successes. In general, the E[I] algorithm substantially oversamples five-member O-connecting heterocycles He (1,3-oxathiole) and Hd (2,3-dihydro-1,3-oxazole) relative to random sampling, and this region appears to have generally low rates of conversion to successful calculations (Figure S21). 

The second- and third-most commonly requested core ligands correspond to the two most common successfully simulated ligands, DeHe and DbHe with 12 successful examples each. Both are comprised of a six-membered oxygen-connecting heterocycle, with either a sulfur heteroatom at the C3 position or unsaturated carbons at the C3-C4 positions, and the 1,3oxathiole. Mn complexes with DeHe core ligands span a redox potential window of 7.32 to 8.15 eV (7.76 to 8.14 eV for DbHe) and logP values of _−_ 4.99 _×_ 10<sup>_−_2</sup> to _−_ 4.40 _×_ 10<sup>_−_2</sup> ( _−_ 5.52 _×_ 10<sup>_−_2</sup> to _−_ 4.50 _×_ 10<sup>_−_2</sup> for DbHe) across different functionalizations. 

Only 16 monodentate ligands are successfully converged from the 63 requested, with 15 in the random or cluster-based sampling and only one (cobalt with Be, 6H-1,3-thiazine, ligands and aminochloropropanol functionalizations) in the E[I] process from generation 2, showing middling ∆ _G_ ox(sol) and logP values of 6.46 eV and _−_ 4.41 _×_ 10<sup>_−_2</sup> respectively. A small number (3–4) monodentate ligands continue to be requested into generations 4 and 5 but could not be converged, consisting of the same oxygen-coordinating, sulfur containing heterocycles with Mn found in the top-performing bidentate ligands (main text Figure 7). 

A total of 552 unique functionalizations are requested from a total of 897 possibilities, with 450 from the random or diversity oriented clustering (i.e. 450 from 600), and 203 from the E[I]-guided design (of 400), showing a significant reduction in the diversity of functionalizations requested by the E[I] approach. A total of 69 functionalizations spread over 87 complexes are successfully converged from the total 203 E[I]-requested complexes, while 201 functionalizations spread over 209 complexes are successfully converged from the random/cluster requested 450 (Table S14). The successfully-simulated functionalizations mostly consist of polar groups, with ten of thirteen functionalization that converge three times containing oxygen, and six terminating in a chloride group. Compared to the ranges of values sampled with fixed core ligand, the functionalization exerts relatively weak control over redox potential but strongly constrains logP. For example, the functionalization C(N)(O)C(N)(O)Cl is converged for three complexes (all Co complexes requested from E[I]) with redox values ranging from 6.22 eV to 6.65 eV and logP values ranging from _−_ 4.75 _×_ 10<sup>_−_2</sup> to _−_ 4.62 _×_ 10<sup>_−_2</sup> .

Text S4: Details of initial sample of constructed design space from clustering The 698, 763 ligands are combined with four transition metals (i.e., Cr, Mn, Fe, and Co) to produce a design space of 2, 795, 052( _i_ . _e_ ., _∼_ 2.8 million) transition metal complexes. The transition metal complexes are an average of 141 atoms in size (minimum: 55, maximum: 235 atoms) and evenly divided over the four transition metals by definition (Table S20). In order to select a number of initial candidates to simulate, we obtained 300 cluster centroids from the design space using _k_ -medoids clustering in the RAC-155 representation. We employed the clustering large applications (CLARA) algorithm<sup>5</sup> , as implemented in the R package ‘Cluster’<sup>7</sup> . We used Euclidean feature space (i.e., RAC-155) distance as the dissimilarity metric for clustering. The resulting clusters ranged from 738 to 35, 466 complexes in size with an average size of 9, 317 (Supporting Information Figure S24). We judged cluster quality by the isolation ratio, which is the maximum in-cluster dissimilarity divided by the average distance between centroids. Most clusters (i.e., 285 of 300) have good characteristics, with isolation ratios below 2.5, whereas a minority (15 of 300) exhibit large intra-cluster dissimilarity (Supporting Information Figure S24). Silhouette analysis assigns each cluster a score ranging from _−_ 1 to 1 representing how well separated each cluster is, with 1 being perfect and 0.5 being well separated. Silhouette values near zero arise when data is on the border of two or more clusters. When accounting for cluster size, we observe that the majority of data is assigned to a cluster with a score of at least 0.25 (Supporting Information Figure S25). The full design space characteristics are preserved in the clustered set of 300 unique complexes, including an even distribution over metals as well as the range of complex sizes (Supporting Information Table S20).

Text S1: Description of hierarchical ligand construction approach
A series of hierarchical rules were used to combinatorially enumerate a large space of mon-
odentate and bidentate ligands. These consist of i) heterocycle and metal-coordinating atom
selection, ii) modiﬁcation of non-metal-coordinating portions of the ring, iii) (optional) ring fusion
to form symmetric or asymmetric bidentate ligands, and iv) sequential functionalization on a
site of the heterocycle. Speciﬁc rules are as follows:
•
Eight core ﬁve- or six-membered heterocycles are ﬁrst deﬁned (labels: A-D for six-
membered rings, E-H for ﬁve-membered rings, as shown in Figure S1), each with O- or
N-atom coordination to the metal deﬁned as position 1 on the ring. In the O-atom cases
(C, D, G, and H), the oxygen always forms single bonds with neighboring carbon atoms
in positions 2 and 6, whereas for the N-atom cases (A, B, E, and F), nitrogen forms one
single bond and one double bond (between positions 1 and 2) in all heterocycles. Pairs
of rings of the same size and coordinating atom are distinguished by the presence or
absence of a double bond at the C(n −1)-C(n) position.
•
Five modiﬁcations to the core rings (labels: a-e, as shown in Figure S1) are carried
out centered on C3 and inﬂuence the C2-C3-C4 bonds for both ﬁve- and six-membered
rings to form two-letter-coded ring structures (i.e., Aa-He). These modiﬁcations of C2-
C3-C4 include: two single bonds with a carbon atom (a), a single (2-3) and double (3-4)
bond (b), insertion of an oxygen atom at the C3 site (c), insertion of an amine nitrogen
(NH) at the C3 site (d), or insertion of a sulfur atom at the C3 site (e). In some cases,
modiﬁcation with a does not modify the core heterocycle (e.g., Aa is A unchanged).
•
Fb or Hb would correspond to a C center with two double bonds at both 3-4 and 4-5 and
are excluded. This yields 38 modiﬁed, core heterocycles that can be used as scaffolds
for forming functionalized monodentate ligands.
•
Bidentate ligands are also formed by joining the two rings at the 2nd position to form a
2-2’ bond. Both symmetric and asymmetric bidentate ligands are formed through every
possible pairwise permutation of the 38 unique core rings for a total of (38+2−1
2
) = 741
bidentate ligand scaffolds.
•
Both monodentate and bidentate ligands are then functionalized hierarchically at the C4
site for both ﬁve- and six-membered rings through four steps described next.
•
Methylene or phenyl groups are added either once or twice at the C4 site (referred to
as B1 and B2 for the ﬁrst and second addition, as shown in Figure S2). In the case
of methylene group addition, the two hydrogen atoms can be further replaced with a
functional group (i.e., S1 or S2, as shown in Figure S2) either by a single doubly bonded
functional group (=CH2, =O) or by any two singly bonded groups (-CH3, -H, -OH, and
-NH2).
For phenyl group addition, this functionalization step is not carried out (i.e.,
S1 = H). These changes are referred to as S1 when they occur on the B1 functional
group chain or S2 for B2. In total, each B1 or B2 site can have 13 chemical identities:
1 phenyl group, 2 doubly-bonded S1 choices, 4 symmetric singly-bonded S1 choices,
and 6 asymmetric singly-bonded S1 choices. Combining all possible choices of B1-S1
with B2-S2, as well as the cases where only B1-S1 are generated, yields 14 ∗13 = 182
functional group cores.

•
Finally, functional groups are terminated with a singly-bonded functional group modiﬁ-
cation (i.e., T, as shown in Figure S2) at either the para position on the phenyl group
or on a terminal hydrogen not involved in S1 or S2 in the case of the methylene groups.
The ﬁve terminating functional groups are H, CH3, OH, NH2, and Cl. This would com-
binatorially produce 182 × 5 = 910 functional groups and 897 unique functional groups
(i.e., no B2, T = CH3 is the same as methylene B2 and T = H).
•
In total, 741 bidentate and 38 monodentate ligand scaffolds combined with 897 func-
tional groups produces 698,763 unique ligands. After combination with 4 metal identi-
ties, the total design space contains 2,795,052 complexes.
•
A note on stereochemistry: since the representation used for this design exercise is
based on the molecular graph, we do not distinguish seteroisomers at a design level.
When performing DFT simulations, an isomer is chosen with functional groups placed
cis, where applicable.

Text S2: ANN evaluation and DFT timing
We provide some performance indication of the time-cost for computing properties and uncer-
tainties with the ANN. In order to apply the ANN to the full design space, we apply it in 140
batches of 20k observations each, evaluated serially on a commodity quad core Intel Core i7-
4820K CPU with 64 GB RAM running Ubuntu 14.04.5 LTS and Tensorﬂow 1.14 (Tensorﬂow
automatically distributes evaluation of the ANN for each batch across CPUs). We report aver-
age timings for evaluating the ANN over these 140 batches as reported from the python time
module. Evaluation of the ANN call for 20k candidates takes on average 1.6 seconds, while
computation of the latent distances take a further 0.8 seconds. File IO takes an average of 1.3
seconds per batch, for an average batch time of 3.7 seconds and a total run time of approxi-
mately 9 minutes. Simple bash forking these embarrassingly-parallel evaluations into 8 workers
can further reduce the amount of time needed to compute serial parts of the code as well as
ﬁle IO, reducing the run time to 3.4 minutes.
DFT evaluation takes 21 hours of compute time on average per geometry optimization and
solvent single points per complex on 1 GPU on our local HPC resources, consisting of a mix
of Nvidia GTX 970s, GTX 1080Tis and RTX 2080Tis. Therefore, evaluation of one full design
space point requires 63 hours, and scoring all 3 million candidates would require 20k GPU-
years, intractable even with large-scale parallelization. The observed walltime for one gener-
ation of 100 complexes depends on the number of available GPUs for parallel evaluation and
was in our case between seven and ten days with up to 64 GPUs in parallel, depending on load
on the HPC.

Text S3: Analysis of DFT simulation diversity
Combining the initial diversity-based clusters (generation 1) and the randomly-sampled data
with the four E[I]-generations results in 1000 requested DFT calculations, from which 316
successful unique ∆Gox(sol) and logP values were converged (Figure S20). The converged
complexes cover all 38 constituent heterocycles and 231 (484 requested) unique core ligands
of the 779 total considered here (Table S13). The most commonly requested core ligand is
HeHe (i.e. a bidentate with two 1,3-oxathiole rings), requested 36 times with 36 distinct func-
tionalizations in increasing frequency during the E[I] process (twice in random data, four times
in generation 3 and sixteen times in generation 5) but only leading one successful complex in
generation 3, with an Mn metal center and a methallylamine functionalization (∆Gox(sol) = 7.92
eV, logP = −5.0 × 10−2), narrowly missing the Pareto front of the generation 3 data). A similar
core ligand, HcHe (mixing the previous oxathiole with a 1,3-dioxole ring), is also requested
20 times without any successes.
In general, the E[I] algorithm substantially oversamples
ﬁve-member O-connecting heterocycles He (1,3-oxathiole) and Hd (2,3-dihydro-1,3-oxazole)
relative to random sampling, and this region appears to have generally low rates of conversion
to successful calculations (Figure S21).
The second- and third-most commonly requested core ligands correspond to the two most
common successfully simulated ligands, DeHe and DbHe with 12 successful examples each.
Both are comprised of a six-membered oxygen-connecting heterocycle, with either a sulfur
heteroatom at the C3 position or unsaturated carbons at the C3-C4 positions, and the 1,3-
oxathiole. Mn complexes with DeHe core ligands span a redox potential window of 7.32 to 8.15
eV (7.76 to 8.14 eV for DbHe) and logP values of −4.99 × 10−2 to −4.40 × 10−2 (−5.52 × 10−2
to −4.50 × 10−2 for DbHe) across different functionalizations.
Only 16 monodentate ligands are successfully converged from the 63 requested, with 15 in
the random or cluster-based sampling and only one (cobalt with Be, 6H-1,3-thiazine, ligands
and aminochloropropanol functionalizations) in the E[I] process from generation 2, showing
middling ∆Gox(sol) and logP values of 6.46 eV and −4.41 × 10−2 respectively. A small number
(3–4) monodentate ligands continue to be requested into generations 4 and 5 but could not be
converged, consisting of the same oxygen-coordinating, sulfur containing heterocycles with Mn
found in the top-performing bidentate ligands (main text Figure 7).
A total of 552 unique functionalizations are requested from a total of 897 possibilities, with 450
from the random or diversity oriented clustering (i.e. 450 from 600), and 203 from the E[I]-guided
design (of 400), showing a signiﬁcant reduction in the diversity of functionalizations requested
by the E[I] approach. A total of 69 functionalizations spread over 87 complexes are successfully
converged from the total 203 E[I]-requested complexes, while 201 functionalizations spread over
209 complexes are successfully converged from the random/cluster requested 450 (Table S14).
The successfully-simulated functionalizations mostly consist of polar groups, with ten of thirteen
functionalization that converge three times containing oxygen, and six terminating in a chloride
group. Compared to the ranges of values sampled with ﬁxed core ligand, the functionalization
exerts relatively weak control over redox potential but strongly constrains logP. For example,
the functionalization C(N)(O)C(N)(O)Cl is converged for three complexes (all Co complexes
requested from E[I]) with redox values ranging from 6.22 eV to 6.65 eV and logP values ranging
from −4.75 × 10−2 to −4.62 × 10−2.

Text S4: Details of initial sample of constructed design space from clustering
The 698, 763 ligands are combined with four transition metals (i.e., Cr, Mn, Fe, and Co) to pro-
duce a design space of 2, 795, 052(i.e., ∼2.8 million) transition metal complexes. The transition
metal complexes are an average of 141 atoms in size (minimum: 55, maximum: 235 atoms)
and evenly divided over the four transition metals by deﬁnition (Table S20). In order to select
a number of initial candidates to simulate, we obtained 300 cluster centroids from the design
space using k-medoids clustering in the RAC-155 representation. We employed the clustering
large applications (CLARA) algorithm5, as implemented in the R package ‘Cluster’7. We used
Euclidean feature space (i.e., RAC-155) distance as the dissimilarity metric for clustering. The
resulting clusters ranged from 738 to 35, 466 complexes in size with an average size of 9, 317
(Supporting Information Figure S24). We judged cluster quality by the isolation ratio, which is
the maximum in-cluster dissimilarity divided by the average distance between centroids. Most
clusters (i.e., 285 of 300) have good characteristics, with isolation ratios below 2.5, whereas a
minority (15 of 300) exhibit large intra-cluster dissimilarity (Supporting Information Figure S24).
Silhouette analysis assigns each cluster a score ranging from −1 to 1 representing how well
separated each cluster is, with 1 being perfect and 0.5 being well separated. Silhouette values
near zero arise when data is on the border of two or more clusters. When accounting for cluster
size, we observe that the majority of data is assigned to a cluster with a score of at least 0.25
(Supporting Information Figure S25). The full design space characteristics are preserved in the
clustered set of 300 unique complexes, including an even distribution over metals as well as the
range of complex sizes (Supporting Information Table S20).
