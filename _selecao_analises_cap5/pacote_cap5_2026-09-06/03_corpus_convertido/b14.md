#### **1. Introduction** 

It is often that real-world engineering problems have 20 to 50 decision variables and multiple objectives [1]. Typical examples include antenna design [2,3], manufacturing engineering [4], structural optimization [5], and mechanical engineering [6], which are referred to as medium-scale multi-objective optimization problems (MOPs). In recent decades, evolutionary algorithms (EAs) have been widely used as optimization algorithms because of their outstanding advantages [7– 9] for solving MOPs, which are referred to as multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs). However, the performance of MOEAs requires thousands of function evaluations to be guaranteed [10], which is impractical in the real-world applications. For some engineering problems [2,4,6], the cost in terms of time or money of function evaluations is high. To address those expensive MOPs [11], several surrogate-assisted multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (SA-MOEAs) have been proposed [11], which use surrogate models constructed with evaluated data as an effective alternative of real function evaluations [12]. Hitherto, various machine learning models can be employed 

as surrogate models, such as Kriging model [13,14], radial basis function networks (RBFNs) [15], response surface method (RSM) [16], artificial neural networks (ANNs) [17], support vector machines (SVMs) [18]. 

Although surrogate models can be served as the alternative of function evaluations, their approximation errors are unavoidable. The performance of SA-MOEAs is greatly limited by the fact that approximation errors in surrogate models may disrupt the dominance relation of the candidate solutions. In general, the approximation error of surrogate models can be reduced via model building and infill sampling. In the model building process, the data selection and model ensemble techniques have been employed. Typical examples of data selection include KTA2 [19], MOEA/D-EGO [20] and MCEA/D [21]. KTA2 trains point-insensitive Kriging models by removing extreme values from the training data. In MOEA/D-EGO, a fuzzy clusteringbased method is adopted to select training data for building several local models for each objective. As for MCEA/D, it effectively hedges

Indeed, ensemble methods are usually categorized into homogeneous and heterogeneous ensemble methods, the main difference is whether the ensemble model consists of the same type of sub-models. HeEMOEA and SAEMO adopt different types of models for heterogeneous ensemble. The former employs SVR and RBFN as ensemble sub-models, while the latter employs RSM, RBFN and Kriging model as ensemble sub-models. In addition to model building, the accuracy of surrogate models can be further improved by taking the model uncertainty into account in infill sampling. These methods can be divided into two categories based on the frequency of using the model uncertainty in the infill sampling. The first category includes KTA2, MS-MOEA [26], KRVEA [27], and CSEA [28]. KTA2 and MS-MOEA respectively adopt an adaptive sampling strategy which selects a targeted sampling strategy by analyzing the demand for convergence, diversity, and model uncertainty at the current stage. In KRVEA, the solution with the best angle penalized distance is used for infill sampling. However, when the population diversity deteriorates, KRVEA uses the model uncertainty for infill sampling. CSEA selects whether to sample the predicted non-dominated or predicted dominated solutions depending on the credibility of the model for expensive function evaluations. The second category is represented by MOEA/D-EGO, ParEGO [29], NSGAIII-EHVI [30], SASSEA [31], DFC-MOEA [32], and REMO [33]. In MOEA/D-EGO and ParEGO, the expected improvement (EI) [34] is adopted as the indicator for each infill sampling, which takes into consideration both convergence and model uncertainty at the same time. NSGAIII-EHVI adopts the expected improvement of hyper-volume (EHVI) as the indicator for infill sampling, where HV can balance convergence and diversity [35,36], and the expected improvement of HV further takes model uncertainty into consideration [30]. SASSEA divides the infill sampling process into two steps. The first step selects the candidates with good convergence and diversity, and the second step considers the model uncertainty of these candidates for secondary selection. Both of DFC-MOEA and REMO consider the uncertainty of labels predicted by classifiers. The former fuzzes the labels during infill sampling process, whereas the latter assigns fitness to the candidates via labels and model uncertainty jointly. 

For medium-scale expensive MOPs, as the dimension of the search space rises, the approximation errors of surrogate models increase, which cannot be significantly reduced by the existing model building and infill sampling techniques. The main reason is that the impact of approximation errors on the optimization process and model quality has not been clearly estimated. Essentially, the approximation errors and noise have very similar impacts on the algorithm, both of which make environment selection less accurate. Therefore, the approximation errors of surrogate models can be modeled as noise, and the process of the SA-MOEA becomes solving a noisy optimization problem [37]. It is common to adjust the denoising method according to the different noise impacts at different optimization stages [38–41]. The existing noisy optimization algorithms estimate the impact by re-evaluation error [26], and rank changes [42,43]. The re-evaluation error-based method specifies the impact to a particular solution. The rank changebased method converts the impact into the fitness rank changes of the observed solutions. It is easy to find that rank changes can directly show the impact of approximation errors, because the environment selection in SA-MOEAs is based on the fitness ranking of solutions. 

Inspired by the above work, a stage-adaptive surrogate-assisted multi-objective evolutionary algorithm (SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA) is proposed to solve medium-scale expensive multi-objective optimization problems. In SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA, a stage-adaptive infill strategy can detect the current optimization stage and then analyzes the sampling demand of current stage to select the appropriate strategy to sample from candidates. The 

current optimization stage is detected by a noise impact indicator, where the approximation errors of surrogate models are formulated as noise. In addition, these candidates are generated by the hybrid optimizer and the ensemble model. The environment selection of IBEA and NSGA-II are used together in the hybrid optimizer. As for the ensemble model, it consists of the latest model and historical models. Specifically, The main contributions of this work can be summarized as followed: 

- (1) A noise impact indicator is developed and embedded into the infill sampling strategy, which accurately detects the current optimization stage with the noise formulated by approximation errors and the noise estimation method to assist SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA in selecting the appropriate sampling strategy. Therefore, the proposed infill sampling strategy does not only reduce the approximation errors of the surrogate models, but also takes into account the demands for convergence and diversity. 

- (2) For improving the accuracy of model uncertainty quantification in infill sampling, we propose a model uncertainty quantification based on the rank change, which relies on the ensemble model with the latest model to calculate the model uncertainty. 

The rest of this paper is organized as follow. In Section 2, the concept of MOPs, the involved environment selection methods, and the approach of noise impact estimation are provided. Section 3 explains the main components of the proposed algorithm SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA in details. The experimental results and relative analysis are given in Section 4. Ultimately, Section 5 summarizes the article and outlines the future work.

#### _2.3. Noise measurement for multi-objective optimization_ 

The rank change-based noise intensity measurement is designed for SOPs [42,43]. In [38], the methodology has been improved to make it applicable to MOPs. 

#### _2.3.1. Rank changes_ 

The noise may lead to a change in the dominance relation of solutions in multiple evaluations. More specifically, suppose a population contains _𝑛_ solutions, the fitness rank of solution **_𝒙_** is _𝑖_ . Subsequently, after re-evaluating solution **_𝒙_** , its fitness rank changes to _𝑗_ . Finally, the rank changes of solution **_𝒙_** is | _𝑖_ − _𝑗_ |. Therefore, the impact of the noise on solution **_𝒙_** can be quantified by the rank change.

#### **3. Proposed algorithm** 

Based on the above discussions, SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA is proposed for solving medium-scale expensive MOPs. Like common SA-MOEAs, SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA is composed of three main components: model construction, modelassisted hybrid search, and stage-adaptive infill sampling. In the model construction, the ensemble surrogate model consists of the latest model and historical models. In the model-assisted hybrid search phase, SA<sup>2</sup> - MOEA selects candidates by alternating between the environment selection of NSGA-II and IBEA. Finally, the stage-adaptive infill sampling strategy is adopted to sample suitable solutions from candidates for function evaluations. More details on SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA can be found in Fig. 1. According to the framework, the proposed algorithm can be further divided into the below five steps. 

- (1) **Initialization:** The Latin hypercube sampling method [53] is employed to generate an initial population. These solutions in the initial population are evaluated by expensive objective functions and then archived into _𝐴_ . The data in _𝐴_ is used to build the surrogate models and as the final output. 

- (2) **Model Construction:** The evaluated data is used to build the RBFN for each objective as the latest model. In addition, the historical models in the model pool and the latest model are employed together to construct multiply ensemble models. The best ensemble model is used as an alternative to expensive function evaluations. Finally, the model pool is updated by applying the first-in-first-out (FIFO) update criterion. 

- (3) **Model-Assisted Hybrid Search:** The initial parent population is selected from _𝐴_ , and its offspring are generated via genetic operators, such as simulated binary crossover and mutation. Subsequently, the offspring are evaluated by surrogate models. Finally, 

the environmental selection of either NSGA-II or IBEA is used to select the next generation of parental population. During the optimization process, the maximum number of iterations _𝑤𝑚𝑎𝑥_ is set to the same as KRVEA. 

- (4) **Stage-Adaptive Infill Sampling:** A partial solutions in _𝐴_ are used to estimate the impact of the noise formulated by the approximation errors, and then the optimization stage is detected according to the impact. In the early stage, the proposed algorithm makes a probabilistic choice between the convergence sampling strategy and the uncertainty sampling strategy. In the late stage, the probabilistic choice occurs between the uncertainty sampling strategy and the diversity sampling strategy. As for the usage probability, it is controlled with a given period. 

- (5) **Termination Condition:** Repeat Steps (2) to (4) until the computation budget is consumed.

#### _3.1. Model construction_ 

It has been investigated that the performance of RBFN is insensitive to the rising decision dimension of the objective function [54]. RBFN is a three-layer neutral network with an input layer, a hidden layer, and an output layer. In addition, the activation function of the hidden layer used in this work is Gaussian function [55]. To guarantee the performance of RBFN, the generalized least squares method [56] is used to determine the weight values between all hidden neurons and the output neuron. Furthermore, ensemble is one of the most effective techniques for reducing the approximation errors [57]. Therefore, we adopt RBFN to build the ensemble model. #### _3.2. Hybrid optimizer_ 

From the experiment in Section 2, we can notice that NSGA-II and IBEA have their own advantages in terms of approximation errors. IBEA shows better convergence and noise tolerance, while NSGA-II performs better in maintaining population diversity. Thus, both can be used together to construct a hybrid optimizer that balances population convergence and diversity while keeping its resistance to the impact of approximation errors. 

In the hybrid optimizer, the crossover and mutation operations are used to generate offspring. The ensemble models are employed to evaluate the offspring. If the number of iterations _𝑤_ is an odd number, the environmental selection of IBEA is adopted. Conversely, the environmental selection of NSGA-II is adopted. The optimization process is run continuously until _𝑤_ reaches a given maximum number of iterations _𝑤𝑚𝑎𝑥_ . By alternating the environmental selection of NSGAII and IBEA, we combine the advantages of both basic optimizers, which are effective in mitigating the impact of approximation errors on environmental selection while taking into account the convergence and diversity of populations. Thus, the hybrid optimizer is able to provide good candidates for infill sampling process. 

#### _3.3. Stage-adaptive infill sampling_ 

In various optimization stages, the algorithm has different emphases on convergence, diversity, and model accuracy. Therefore, a stage-adaptive infill sampling strategy is proposed to select a targeted sampling strategy based on the demand at the current stage of optimization, which can make full use of the expensive function evaluations. The infill sampling strategies involved are summarized below. 

- The convergence-based sampling strategy adopts _𝐼𝜖_ + to assign fitness for candidates. Subsequently, the solution with the smallest fitness is removed and the fitness of the remaining solutions are updated till remaining the best solution. By this means, the solution with good convergence can be found. 

- The diversity-based sampling strategy first deletes the candidates that are close to the evaluated solutions, and then the k-means method [58] is used to select the best solutions. In this way, the search space close to the PS can be fully exploited. 

- The uncertainty-based sampling strategy selects the solution with the highest model uncertainty. #### _4.1. Experimental settings_ 

In order to comprehensively validate the superiority of the proposed algorithm, we carry out experiments on two- or three-objective DTLZ and WFG [59] series of test problems, and their corresponding decision variables are set to 31, 51 and 30, 50. In addition, the maximum number of expensive function evaluations is fixed at 300. To avoid serendipity, each test problem is run 30 times independently. In the comparative experiment, we compare the performance of SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA with the representatives of SA-MOEAs, including ADSAPSO [60], HeEMOEA [22], MOEA/D-EGO [20], KRVEA [27], KTA2 [19], and PCSAEA [61]. All of them are implemented with the assistance of PlatEMO [62]. The characteristics of the comparison algorithms are summarized below. 

- (1) An adaptive dropout mechanism is used for dimension reduction to reduce the search space in ADSAPSO. Subsequently, RBFN and PSO are used as surrogate model and basic optimizer, respectively. As for the infill criterion, the obtained optimal solutions are repaired by a replacement operator. Then, the repaired solutions are evaluated by expensive objective functions. 

- (2) HeEMOEA employs an ensemble model consisting of RBFN and support vector regression (SVR) as the alternative to each objective function. NSGA-II is used as the basic optimizer in HeEMOEA. Furthermore, a k-means clustering method is adopted to select solutions for real function evaluation. 

- (3) MOEA/D-EGO builds multiple Kriging models as local models by a fuzzy clustering method as the surrogate models. In the MOEA/D-EGO, MOEA/D [63] is adopted as the basic optimizer. For its infill sampling strategy, the solution with the best EI is chosen for expensive evaluation. 

- (4) KRVEA adopts the Kriging model to approximate each objective function. In addition, RVEA [64] is employed as its basic optimizer. As for its sampling strategy, the solution with the best angle penalized distance is used for re-evaluation. However, when the diversity of population deteriorates, the solution with maximum model uncertainty is chosen. 

- (5) KTA2 constructs an influential point-insensitive Kriging model by data selection for each objective. In KTA2, Two_Arch2 [65] is used as the basic optimizer, and an adaptive infill sampling strategy is used for choosing the solutions for re-evaluation. Different from SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA, the adaptive infill sampling strategy of KTA2 requires two archives to assist in detecting the current sampling demand. 

- (6) PCSAEA employs a pairwise comparison based model to filter candidate solutions, which has a similar structure as RBFN. In PCSAEA, simulated binary crossover and polynomial mutation are used to generate offspring. For the infill sampling strategy of PCSAEA, it selects part of the solutions with good predicted label for re-evaluation.

The complexity of the problem causes the approximation ability of the ensemble model in SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA to be drastically affected. In addition to its non-separable characteristic, WFG9 has a deceptive and multi-modal PF. Perhaps, it is because the ensemble model of HeEMOEA consisting of RBFN and SVR can better approximate WFG9. 

WFG7 is both a separable and uni-modal test problem. Therefore, the problem requires the algorithm to focus more on the maintenance of diversity. From the results of WFG7, it appears that SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA and HeEMOEA have similar performance. It is because SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA adopts the uncertainty-based sampling strategy, which is inefficient to the maintenance of diversity. 

WFG8 is a hard non-separable problem. It imposes the greater demand on the approximation ability of the surrogate model. In other words, the ensemble model of SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA has excessive approximation errors on this problem, which impacts the performance of the algorithm. 

#### _4.6.3. Discussions_ 

From the experimental results of DTLZ and WFG problems, we can notice that various SA-MOEAs are skilled at handling different types of test problems. ADSAPSO achieves the best performance on multi-modal characteristics, like DTLZ1 and DTLZ3. It is because the dimension reduction strategy in ADSAPSO can effectively reduce the search space, thus making the algorithm more likely to converge. HeEMOEA works well with some complex problems like WFG2, WFG7, and WFG9. Because the heterogeneous ensemble model of HeEMOEA can combine 

the advantages of both RBFN and SVR. The performance of MOEA/DEGO, KRVEA, and PCSAEA is significantly degraded because of the rising dimension of the search space. In addition, the random sampling used in PCSAEA when the reliability of surrogate models is poor may lead to some function evaluations that cannot be fully utilized [61]. However, on the medium-scale DTLZ6, one of the main advantages of the performance of MOEA/D-EGO is that the local models are effective in reducing approximation errors. As for KTA2, its point-insensitive models and the adaptive sampling strategy can mitigate the approximation errors to a certain extent. Consequently, KTA2 can achieve comparable results in some difficult problems like DTLZ1, WFG1, and WFG8. Overall, SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA shows significant advantages in dealing with different kinds of test problems, such as DTLZ2, DTLZ5, and WFG3. In summary, the main benefits of SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA can be outlined as follows. 

- The ensemble model consisting of latest RBFN and historical RBFN in SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA has good approximation ability and its performance is insensitive to the increase in the dimensionality of the search space. In addition, RBFN has better smoothness and can effectively prevent SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA from falling into local optima for some multi-modal problems. 

- SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA adopts a stage-adaptive infill sampling strategy. It can accurately detect the current optimization stage by the rankchange based impact estimation method and the formulated noise, and then adaptively selects the appropriate sampling strategy for the current stage. In the stage-adaptive infill sampling strategy, the proposed rank change-based method can effectively quantify the model uncertainty. 

- The hybrid optimizer in SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA can balance the convergence and diversity of IBEA and NSGA-II. Not only that, the resistance of IBEA to approximation errors is inherited by the hybrid optimizer.

#### **5. Conclusion** 

In this work, a stage-adaptive surrogate-assisted MOEA (SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA) is proposed for medium-scale expensive MOPs. In SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA, a stageadaptive infill strategy detects the current optimization stage, and then analyzes the sampling demand of current stage to select the appropriate strategy to sample from candidates. The current optimization stage is detected by a noise impact indicator, where the approximation errors of surrogate models are formulated as noise. In addition, these potential candidates are generated by the hybrid optimizer and the ensemble model. The environment selection of IBEA and NSGA-II are used together in the hybrid optimizer. As for the ensemble model, it consists of the latest RBFN and historical RBFN. 

The effectiveness of the ensemble model, hybrid optimizer, stageadaptive infill sampling strategy and uncertainty quantification method in SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA are shown by experiments on the DTLZ and WFG test suits. From the results, we can observe that SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA outperforms other compared algorithms on medium-scale MOPs. However, there are still some challenges in SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA that need to be addressed in the future. In some problems with multiple complex characteristics, the approximation ability of ensemble model containing only RBFN is insufficient. One promising approach is to integrate more other kinds 

of surrogate models, such as dropout networks [67] or SVR for further improvement of the approximation ability of the model. Furthermore, it might be possible for SA<sup>2</sup> -MOEA to further improve the performance of the algorithm on medium-scale optimization problems through some dimension reduction techniques like PCA [68] or feature selection [66]. In addition to the above, the probability control in the stage-adaptive infill sampling strategy relies on the previous sampling results, which means that there might be parts of function evaluations that cannot be appropriately applied at the start of the optimization. One feasible alternative is to select a targeted sampling strategy by analyzing the convergence or diversity of the candidates at the current stage. The algorithm proposed in this work is designed for expensive medium-scale MOPs. Therefore, there are some challenges in solving expensive highdimensional MOPs. For example, how to construct accurate surrogate models [21] and how to design an effective basic optimizer [69], etc. In the future, we will further improve this work so that it can be used to solve expensive high-dimensional MOPs.

A B S T R A C T
Many real multi-objective optimization problems with 20-50 decision variables often have only a small number
of function evaluations available, because of their heavy time/money burden. Therefore, surrogate models are
often utilized as alternatives for expensive function evaluations. However, the approximation error of the
surrogate model is inevitable compared to the real function evaluation. The approximation error has a similar
impact on the algorithm as noise, i.e., different optimization stages suffer from various impacts. Therefore,
the current optimization stage can be indirectly detected via measuring the impact of the noise formulated
by the approximation error. In addition, the rising dimension of the search space leads to an increase in the
approximation errors of the surrogate models, which poses a huge challenge for existing surrogate-assisted
multi-objective evolutionary algorithms. In this work, we propose a stage-adaptive surrogate-assisted multi-
objective evolutionary algorithm to solve the medium-scale optimization problems. In the proposed algorithm,
the ensemble model consisting of the latest and historical models is used as the surrogate model, on the basis
of which a set of potential candidates can be discovered. Then, a stage-adaptive infill sampling strategy selects
the most suitable sampling strategy by analyzing the demand of the current optimization stage on convergence,
diversity, model accuracy to sample from the candidates. As for the current optimization stage, it is detected
by a noise impact indicator, where the approximation errors of surrogate models are formulated as noise. The
experimental results on a series of medium-scale expensive test problems demonstrate the superiority of the
proposed algorithm over six state-of-the-art compared algorithms.

els can be further improved by taking the model uncertainty into
account in infill sampling. These methods can be divided into two
categories based on the frequency of using the model uncertainty in
the infill sampling. The first category includes KTA2, MS-MOEA [26],
KRVEA [27], and CSEA [28]. KTA2 and MS-MOEA respectively adopt
an adaptive sampling strategy which selects a targeted sampling strat-
egy by analyzing the demand for convergence, diversity, and model
uncertainty at the current stage. In KRVEA, the solution with the
best angle penalized distance is used for infill sampling. However,
when the population diversity deteriorates, KRVEA uses the model
uncertainty for infill sampling. CSEA selects whether to sample the
predicted non-dominated or predicted dominated solutions depending
on the credibility of the model for expensive function evaluations.
The second category is represented by MOEA/D-EGO, ParEGO [29],
NSGAIII-EHVI [30], SASSEA [31], DFC-MOEA [32], and REMO [33].
In MOEA/D-EGO and ParEGO, the expected improvement (EI) [34]
is adopted as the indicator for each infill sampling, which takes into
consideration both convergence and model uncertainty at the same
time. NSGAIII-EHVI adopts the expected improvement of hyper-volume
(EHVI) as the indicator for infill sampling, where HV can balance
convergence and diversity [35,36], and the expected improvement of
HV further takes model uncertainty into consideration [30]. SASSEA
divides the infill sampling process into two steps. The first step selects
the candidates with good convergence and diversity, and the second
step considers the model uncertainty of these candidates for secondary
selection. Both of DFC-MOEA and REMO consider the uncertainty of
labels predicted by classifiers. The former fuzzes the labels during infill
sampling process, whereas the latter assigns fitness to the candidates
via labels and model uncertainty jointly.
For medium-scale expensive MOPs, as the dimension of the search
space rises, the approximation errors of surrogate models increase,
which cannot be significantly reduced by the existing model building
and infill sampling techniques. The main reason is that the impact of
approximation errors on the optimization process and model quality
has not been clearly estimated. Essentially, the approximation errors
and noise have very similar impacts on the algorithm, both of which
make environment selection less accurate. Therefore, the approxima-
tion errors of surrogate models can be modeled as noise, and the process
of the SA-MOEA becomes solving a noisy optimization problem [37].
It is common to adjust the denoising method according to the different
noise impacts at different optimization stages [38–41]. The existing
noisy optimization algorithms estimate the impact by re-evaluation
error [26], and rank changes [42,43]. The re-evaluation error-based
method specifies the impact to a particular solution. The rank change-
based method converts the impact into the fitness rank changes of the
observed solutions. It is easy to find that rank changes can directly show
the impact of approximation errors, because the environment selection
in SA-MOEAs is based on the fitness ranking of solutions.
Inspired by the above work, a stage-adaptive surrogate-assisted
multi-objective evolutionary algorithm (SA2-MOEA) is proposed to
solve medium-scale expensive multi-objective optimization problems.
In SA2-MOEA, a stage-adaptive infill strategy can detect the current
optimization stage and then analyzes the sampling demand of current
stage to select the appropriate strategy to sample from candidates. The
current optimization stage is detected by a noise impact indicator,
where the approximation errors of surrogate models are formulated
as noise. In addition, these candidates are generated by the hybrid
optimizer and the ensemble model. The environment selection of IBEA
and NSGA-II are used together in the hybrid optimizer. As for the
ensemble model, it consists of the latest model and historical models.
Specifically, The main contributions of this work can be summarized
as followed:
(1) A noise impact indicator is developed and embedded into the
infill sampling strategy, which accurately detects the current opti-
mization stage with the noise formulated by approximation errors
and the noise estimation method to assist SA2-MOEA in selecting
the appropriate sampling strategy. Therefore, the proposed infill
sampling strategy does not only reduce the approximation errors
of the surrogate models, but also takes into account the demands
for convergence and diversity.
(2) For improving the accuracy of model uncertainty quantification
in infill sampling, we propose a model uncertainty quantification
based on the rank change, which relies on the ensemble model
with the latest model to calculate the model uncertainty.
The rest of this paper is organized as follow. In Section 2, the
concept of MOPs, the involved environment selection methods, and the
approach of noise impact estimation are provided. Section 3 explains
the main components of the proposed algorithm SA2-MOEA in details.
The experimental results and relative analysis are given in Section 4.
Ultimately, Section 5 summarizes the article and outlines the future
work.

3. Proposed algorithm
Based on the above discussions, SA2-MOEA is proposed for solving
medium-scale expensive MOPs. Like common SA-MOEAs, SA2-MOEA
is composed of three main components: model construction, model-
assisted hybrid search, and stage-adaptive infill sampling. In the model
construction, the ensemble surrogate model consists of the latest model
and historical models. In the model-assisted hybrid search phase, SA2-
MOEA selects candidates by alternating between the environment se-
lection of NSGA-II and IBEA. Finally, the stage-adaptive infill sampling
strategy is adopted to sample suitable solutions from candidates for
function evaluations. More details on SA2-MOEA can be found in Fig. 1.
According to the framework, the proposed algorithm can be further
divided into the below five steps.
(1) Initialization: The Latin hypercube sampling method [53] is
employed to generate an initial population. These solutions in the
initial population are evaluated by expensive objective functions
and then archived into 𝐴. The data in 𝐴is used to build the
surrogate models and as the final output.
(2) Model Construction: The evaluated data is used to build the
RBFN for each objective as the latest model. In addition, the
historical models in the model pool and the latest model are em-
ployed together to construct multiply ensemble models. The best
ensemble model is used as an alternative to expensive function
evaluations. Finally, the model pool is updated by applying the
first-in-first-out (FIFO) update criterion.
(3) Model-Assisted Hybrid Search: The initial parent population is
selected from 𝐴, and its offspring are generated via genetic op-
erators, such as simulated binary crossover and mutation. Subse-
quently, the offspring are evaluated by surrogate models. Finally,
the environmental selection of either NSGA-II or IBEA is used
to select the next generation of parental population. During the
optimization process, the maximum number of iterations 𝑤𝑚𝑎𝑥is
set to the same as KRVEA.
(4) Stage-Adaptive Infill Sampling: A partial solutions in 𝐴are used
to estimate the impact of the noise formulated by the approxima-
tion errors, and then the optimization stage is detected according
to the impact. In the early stage, the proposed algorithm makes
a probabilistic choice between the convergence sampling strategy
and the uncertainty sampling strategy. In the late stage, the prob-
abilistic choice occurs between the uncertainty sampling strategy
and the diversity sampling strategy. As for the usage probability,
it is controlled with a given period.
(5) Termination Condition: Repeat Steps (2) to (4) until the compu-
tation budget is consumed.

3.2. Hybrid optimizer
From the experiment in Section 2, we can notice that NSGA-II and
IBEA have their own advantages in terms of approximation errors.
IBEA shows better convergence and noise tolerance, while NSGA-II
performs better in maintaining population diversity. Thus, both can be
used together to construct a hybrid optimizer that balances population
convergence and diversity while keeping its resistance to the impact of
approximation errors.
In the hybrid optimizer, the crossover and mutation operations
are used to generate offspring. The ensemble models are employed
to evaluate the offspring. If the number of iterations 𝑤is an odd
number, the environmental selection of IBEA is adopted. Conversely,
the environmental selection of NSGA-II is adopted. The optimization
process is run continuously until 𝑤reaches a given maximum number
of iterations 𝑤𝑚𝑎𝑥. By alternating the environmental selection of NSGA-
II and IBEA, we combine the advantages of both basic optimizers,
which are effective in mitigating the impact of approximation errors on
environmental selection while taking into account the convergence and
diversity of populations. Thus, the hybrid optimizer is able to provide
good candidates for infill sampling process.
3.3. Stage-adaptive infill sampling
In various optimization stages, the algorithm has different em-
phases on convergence, diversity, and model accuracy. Therefore, a
stage-adaptive infill sampling strategy is proposed to select a targeted
sampling strategy based on the demand at the current stage of optimiza-
tion, which can make full use of the expensive function evaluations. The
infill sampling strategies involved are summarized below.
∙The convergence-based sampling strategy adopts 𝐼𝜖+ to assign
fitness for candidates. Subsequently, the solution with the small-
est fitness is removed and the fitness of the remaining solutions
are updated till remaining the best solution. By this means, the
solution with good convergence can be found.
∙The diversity-based sampling strategy first deletes the candidates
that are close to the evaluated solutions, and then the k-means
method [58] is used to select the best solutions. In this way, the
search space close to the PS can be fully exploited.
∙The uncertainty-based sampling strategy selects the solution with
the highest model uncertainty. Quantification of model uncer-

4. Numerical experiments
4.1. Experimental settings
In order to comprehensively validate the superiority of the proposed
algorithm, we carry out experiments on two- or three-objective DTLZ
and WFG [59] series of test problems, and their corresponding decision
variables are set to 31, 51 and 30, 50. In addition, the maximum num-
ber of expensive function evaluations is fixed at 300. To avoid serendip-
ity, each test problem is run 30 times independently. In the comparative
experiment, we compare the performance of SA2-MOEA with the rep-
resentatives of SA-MOEAs, including ADSAPSO [60], HeEMOEA [22],
MOEA/D-EGO [20], KRVEA [27], KTA2 [19], and PCSAEA [61]. All
of them are implemented with the assistance of PlatEMO [62]. The
characteristics of the comparison algorithms are summarized below.
(1) An adaptive dropout mechanism is used for dimension reduction
to reduce the search space in ADSAPSO. Subsequently, RBFN
and PSO are used as surrogate model and basic optimizer, re-
spectively. As for the infill criterion, the obtained optimal solu-
tions are repaired by a replacement operator. Then, the repaired
solutions are evaluated by expensive objective functions.
(2) HeEMOEA employs an ensemble model consisting of RBFN and
support vector regression (SVR) as the alternative to each objec-
tive function. NSGA-II is used as the basic optimizer in HeEMOEA.
Furthermore, a k-means clustering method is adopted to select
solutions for real function evaluation.
(3) MOEA/D-EGO builds multiple Kriging models as local models
by a fuzzy clustering method as the surrogate models. In the
MOEA/D-EGO, MOEA/D [63] is adopted as the basic optimizer.
For its infill sampling strategy, the solution with the best EI is
chosen for expensive evaluation.
(4) KRVEA adopts the Kriging model to approximate each objec-
tive function. In addition, RVEA [64] is employed as its basic
optimizer. As for its sampling strategy, the solution with the
best angle penalized distance is used for re-evaluation. However,
when the diversity of population deteriorates, the solution with
maximum model uncertainty is chosen.
(5) KTA2 constructs an influential point-insensitive Kriging model by
data selection for each objective. In KTA2, Two_Arch2 [65] is
used as the basic optimizer, and an adaptive infill sampling strat-
egy is used for choosing the solutions for re-evaluation. Different
from SA2-MOEA, the adaptive infill sampling strategy of KTA2
requires two archives to assist in detecting the current sampling
demand.
(6) PCSAEA employs a pairwise comparison based model to filter
candidate solutions, which has a similar structure as RBFN. In
PCSAEA, simulated binary crossover and polynomial mutation
are used to generate offspring. For the infill sampling strategy of
PCSAEA, it selects part of the solutions with good predicted label
for re-evaluation.
To assess the performance of all algorithms, we adopt the inverted
generational distance (IGD) [49] as the metric for all experiments. IGD
calculates the mean value of the shortest Euclidean distance from the
sampled points on the PF to the non-dominated solutions found by
the algorithm. Therefore, it is a comprehensive metric that considers
both the convergence and the diversity of non-dominated solutions.
As for the experimental results of SA2-MOEA and other comparison
algorithms, we adopt Wilcoxon rank-sum (WRS) [51] test to calibrate
them at a significance level of 0.05, where the result of SA2-MOEA is
significantly superior, significantly inferior, and statistically similar to
the compared algorithms are indicated by symbols ‘‘+ ’’, ‘‘−’’, and ‘‘≈’’,
respectively.
To guarantee the fairness of the experiment, the parameters of the
compared algorithms are set according to the recommendations in the
original literature. The details of the parameter settings for SA2-MOEA
are shown below.
(1) The population size is set to 𝑁= 100.
(2) The maximum capacity of the model pool is set to 𝑆𝑚𝑎𝑥= 5.
(3) The hyperparameters of RBFN are set as in [66].
(4) The parameters of genetic operators (crossover and mutation) are
set to 𝑝𝑐= 1, 𝑝𝑚= 1, 𝜂𝑐= 20, 𝜂𝑚= 20.
(5) The noisy threshold is set to 𝜃= 0.2 as in [42].
(6) The scaling factor is set to 𝑘= 0.05 as in [47].
(7) The period of probability control is set to 𝑇= 30.
4.2. Sensitivity analysis of parameters
In SA2-MOEA, the two new parameters: model pool capacity 𝑆𝑚𝑎𝑥
and probability control period 𝑇are directly related to its performance.
To investigate the influence of different 𝑆𝑚𝑎𝑥and 𝑇on SA2-MOEA, we
conduct the parameter experiments on DTLZ1 and WFG3 followed by
analysis of the sensitivity of parameters. DTLZ1 is a hard-to-converge
test problem with multi-modal landscape characteristic. WFG3 is a non-
separable reductions test problem. Both have three objectives and 30
decision variables.
For evaluating the effect of different 𝑆𝑚𝑎𝑥, a range of values 𝑆𝑚𝑎𝑥∈
{1, 3, 5, 7, 9} are tested. As for the parameter 𝑇, it is set to 20. The
experimental results of 30 independent runs of SA2-MOEA on two test
problems can be found in Fig. 2. From the experimental results, we
can discover that 𝑆𝑚𝑎𝑥can affect the performance of the proposed
algorithm and that the proposed algorithm performs best when 𝑆𝑚𝑎𝑥
is equal to 5. As 𝑆𝑚𝑎𝑥is set too small, the model pool contains fewer
historical models, which can result in the approximation capability of
the ensemble model being negatively affected. However, 𝑆𝑚𝑎𝑥is set too
large, the model pool contains too many outdated historical models.
It does not only affect the continuous updating ability of the model
pool, but also leads to a decrease in the approximation capability of the
ensemble model. Combined with above analysis, the maximum capacity
of the model pool is set to 5.
Subsequently, to determine the most appropriate value for the prob-
ability control period 𝑇, we test a series of values 𝑇∈{10, 20, 30, 40, 50}
when 𝑆𝑚𝑎𝑥= 5. As we can observe from the experimental results in
Fig. 2, the effect of the performance of SA2-MOEA varies for different
control periods. Furthermore, the best results are archived on both
test problems at 𝑇= 30. It is mainly because when 𝑇is too small,
it is difficult to analyze the correct demand for the optimization stage
from the limited historical samplings. However, when 𝑇is set overly
large as a direct result of the low probability control frequency, it
causes the major time gap between the optimization demand and the
probability control, which affects the performance of the proposed
algorithm. Therefore, we set the probability control period 𝑇into 30.

4.6. Comparative experiments
To further discuss the performance of SA2-MOEA, we have experi-
mented it with ADSAPSO, HeEMOEA, MOEA/D-EGO, KRVEA, PCSAEA
and KTA2 on a total of 64 test problems in the DTLZ and WFG test suites
with different numbers of objectives (2 and 3) and decision variables
(31, 51 and 30, 50). For fairly evaluating the experimental results,
each experiment is run 30 times independently and IGD is used as the
performance metric of the algorithms.
4.6.1. Analysis of DTLZ results
The IGD results of experiments for all compared algorithms and
SA2-MOEA are shown in Table 5. We perform the WRS test with the
significance level 0.05, where the best results for each test problems
are highlighted. From the results, it is easy to notice that SA2-MOEA
achieves the best results on 14 of all 28 test problems, ranking first,
followed by ADSAPSO, KTA2, MOEA/D-EGO, HeEMOEA, PCSAEA, and
KRVEA in descending order. Next, we analyze the experimental results
in detail by considering the characteristics of the test problems.
DTLZ1 and DTLZ3 are two hard-to-converge problems because
of their multi-modal landscapes, and DTLZ3 is an advanced version
of DTLZ1. In this type of test problem, ADSAPSO has the best per-
formance, followed by SA2-MOEA, KTA2, MOEA/D-EGO, PCSAEA,
KRVEA, and HeEMOEA. The main reason why ADSAPSO achieves the
best performance on them may be that dimension reduction strategy
reduces the search space, which makes it easy to find solutions with
better convergence. Among the other SA-MOEAs without the dimension
reduction strategy, SA2-MOEA ranks first, which benefits from the
inherent smoothing of the surrogate model RBFN [26], which effec-
tively prevents SA2-MOEA from falling into the local optima. Similarly,
the point-insensitive model of KTA2 can also serve this purpose. In
addition, both of their infill sampling strategies can accurately analyze
the demand of convergence and adopt the targeted sampling strategies.
Once stuck in the local optimum, SA2-MOEA and KTA2 can jump out
of it by employing the uncertainty-based sampling strategy. Besides,
MOEA/D-EGO uses EI as the indicator for infill sampling in some extant
to avoid the algorithm falling into the local optimum.
DTLZ2 is an easy-to-converge test problem with uni-modal charac-
teristic. In DTLZ2, SA2-MOEA achieves the best results, followed by
HeEMOEA, ADSAPSO, KTA2, MOEA/D-EGO, PCSAEA, and KRVEA re-
spectively. For such test problems, it is critical to maintain the diversity
of the population for excellent performance. The hybrid optimizer in
SA2-MOEA is better at maintaining the diversity of the population.
In addition, the stage-adaptive infill sampling strategy can meet the
demand of algorithm for diversity in the late stage of optimization.
The fact that HeEMOEA ranks second also demonstrates the importance
of maintaining the diversity of population and the effectiveness of
diversity-based sampling strategy. The results of ADSAPSO on DTLZ2
reflect the weakness of the dimension reduction strategy, which can
cause some difficulties in fully exploring decision space. As for the
unsatisfactory performance of other compared algorithms on such test
problems, the main reason may be that the increasing number of
decision variables causes it be unable to accurately determine the
current optimization demand.
DTLZ4 is a test problem with a preferred region obtained via
modifying DTLZ2. As shown in Table 5, SA2-MOEA performs the best on
DTLZ4, followed by ADSAPSO, HeEMOEA, KTA2, PCSAEA, MOEA/D-
EGO, and KRVEA respectively. The preferred trait of the problem makes
the performance of the algorithm very sensitive to the initial data.
Therefore, to improve the likelihood of finding the preferred region,
the algorithm is required to enable the maintenance of good population
diversity. Both the hybrid optimizer and the stage-adaptive infill sam-
pling strategy can satisfy the requirement of SA2-MOEA for population
diversity. Like DTLZ2, ADSAPSO may be negatively affected by the
dimension reduction strategy. Because of the preferred characteristic
of DTLZ4, the predicted performance of pairwise comparison model
in PCSAEA becomes poor. As for KTA2 and KRVEA, the main reason
for under-performance of the diversity maintenance method is the
deterioration of the approximation ability of surrogate model due to
the enlarged search space.
DTLZ5, DTLZ6, and DTLZ7 belong to test problems with irregular
PFs. The PFs of both DTLZ5 and DTLZ6 are degenerated curves, while
the PF of DTLZ7 is discontinuous. The irregular PFs make it challenging
to maintain diversity in solving this type of optimization problems. On
both DTLZ5 and DTLZ7, SA2-MOEA outperforms all compared algo-
rithms. It does not only prove the effectiveness of the ensemble model
consisting of RBFN in handling medium-scale MOPs but also demon-
strates that the hybrid optimizer and stage-adaptive infill sampling can

4.6.2. Analysis of WFG results
To further comprehensively evaluate the performance of the pro-
posed algorithm, the WFG test suite is used for the experiment. From
the experimental results in Table 6, it is easy to see SA2-MOEA shows
the best performance in these problems, followed by KTA2, HeEMOEA,
ADSAPSO, KRVEA, and MOEA/D-EGO respectively. In the following,
we analyze the results for each problem in detail, considering the
characteristics of each problem.
WFG1 has the most transformation functions in this series of test
problems. Therefore, it is a major challenge to address this problem
by maintaining good diversity. The complexity of the problem causes
the approximation ability of the ensemble model in SA2-MOEA to
be drastically affected. It is the main reason why the performance
of SA2-MOEA is inferior to that of KTA2. However, as the number

the ensemble model approximates this problem better, but also that the
stage-adaptive infill sampling makes full use of limited function evalua-
tion. However, HeEMOEA works the best in WFG9, next to SA2-MOEA,
ADSAPSO, PCSAEA, KRVEA, KTA2 and MOEA/D-EGO. In addition to
its non-separable characteristic, WFG9 has a deceptive and multi-modal
PF. Perhaps, it is because the ensemble model of HeEMOEA consisting
of RBFN and SVR can better approximate WFG9.
WFG7 is both a separable and uni-modal test problem. Therefore,
the problem requires the algorithm to focus more on the maintenance
of diversity. From the results of WFG7, it appears that SA2-MOEA and
HeEMOEA have similar performance. It is because SA2-MOEA adopts
the uncertainty-based sampling strategy, which is inefficient to the
maintenance of diversity.
WFG8 is a hard non-separable problem. It imposes the greater

better approximate the objective function of WFG8. In other words, the
ensemble model of SA2-MOEA has excessive approximation errors on
this problem, which impacts the performance of the algorithm.
4.6.3. Discussions
From the experimental results of DTLZ and WFG problems, we can
notice that various SA-MOEAs are skilled at handling different types of
test problems. ADSAPSO achieves the best performance on multi-modal
characteristics, like DTLZ1 and DTLZ3. It is because the dimension
reduction strategy in ADSAPSO can effectively reduce the search space,
thus making the algorithm more likely to converge. HeEMOEA works
well with some complex problems like WFG2, WFG7, and WFG9.
Because the heterogeneous ensemble model of HeEMOEA can combine
the advantages of both RBFN and SVR. The performance of MOEA/D-
EGO, KRVEA, and PCSAEA is significantly degraded because of the
rising dimension of the search space. In addition, the random sampling
used in PCSAEA when the reliability of surrogate models is poor may
lead to some function evaluations that cannot be fully utilized [61].
However, on the medium-scale DTLZ6, one of the main advantages of
the performance of MOEA/D-EGO is that the local models are effective
in reducing approximation errors. As for KTA2, its point-insensitive
models and the adaptive sampling strategy can mitigate the approx-
imation errors to a certain extent. Consequently, KTA2 can achieve
comparable results in some difficult problems like DTLZ1, WFG1, and
WFG8. Overall, SA2-MOEA shows significant advantages in dealing
with different kinds of test problems, such as DTLZ2, DTLZ5, and
WFG3. In summary, the main benefits of SA2-MOEA can be outlined
as follows.
∙The ensemble model consisting of latest RBFN and historical
RBFN in SA2-MOEA has good approximation ability and its per-
formance is insensitive to the increase in the dimensionality of
the search space. In addition, RBFN has better smoothness and
can effectively prevent SA2-MOEA from falling into local optima
for some multi-modal problems.
∙SA2-MOEA adopts a stage-adaptive infill sampling strategy. It
can accurately detect the current optimization stage by the rank-
change based impact estimation method and the formulated
noise, and then adaptively selects the appropriate sampling strat-
egy for the current stage. In the stage-adaptive infill sampling
strategy, the proposed rank change-based method can effectively
quantify the model uncertainty.
∙The hybrid optimizer in SA2-MOEA can balance the convergence
and diversity of IBEA and NSGA-II. Not only that, the resistance of
IBEA to approximation errors is inherited by the hybrid optimizer.

former has a space complexity of 𝑂(𝑀𝑁2
𝑠), the latter has a space com-
plexity of 𝑂(𝑆𝑚𝑎𝑥𝑀𝑁𝑠𝐷). However, the space consumption incurred by
the stored historical models is much smaller than computing the RBFN
weights. Therefore, the latter can be ignored. As for the surrogate-
assisted search and infill sampling of SA2-MOEA, both consume space
in assigning the 𝐼𝜖+-based fitness for all candidate solutions. Thus, the
space consumption of SA2-MOEA is 𝑂(𝑀𝑁2
𝑠+ 𝑁2 + 𝑁2). All compared
algorithms need to store the population information with the space
complexity of 𝑂((𝑀+ 𝐷)𝑁). However, the space consumption of the

Therefore, the space consumption caused by the stored population can
be ignored. Notably, KTA2 has multiple populations and each objective
has multiple surrogate models. Finally, we can conclude that KTA2
has the highest space complexity, follow by SA2-MOEA and ADSAPSO,
respectively.
5. Conclusion
In this work, a stage-adaptive surrogate-assisted MOEA (SA2-MOEA)
is proposed for medium-scale expensive MOPs. In SA2-MOEA, a stage-
adaptive infill strategy detects the current optimization stage, and then
analyzes the sampling demand of current stage to select the appropriate
strategy to sample from candidates. The current optimization stage
is detected by a noise impact indicator, where the approximation
errors of surrogate models are formulated as noise. In addition, these
potential candidates are generated by the hybrid optimizer and the
ensemble model. The environment selection of IBEA and NSGA-II are
used together in the hybrid optimizer. As for the ensemble model, it
consists of the latest RBFN and historical RBFN.
The effectiveness of the ensemble model, hybrid optimizer, stage-
adaptive infill sampling strategy and uncertainty quantification method
in SA2-MOEA are shown by experiments on the DTLZ and WFG test
suits. From the results, we can observe that SA2-MOEA outperforms
other compared algorithms on medium-scale MOPs. However, there
are still some challenges in SA2-MOEA that need to be addressed in
the future. In some problems with multiple complex characteristics,
the approximation ability of ensemble model containing only RBFN is
insufficient. One promising approach is to integrate more other kinds
of surrogate models, such as dropout networks [67] or SVR for further
improvement of the approximation ability of the model. Furthermore,
it might be possible for SA2-MOEA to further improve the performance
of the algorithm on medium-scale optimization problems through some
dimension reduction techniques like PCA [68] or feature selection [66].
In addition to the above, the probability control in the stage-adaptive
infill sampling strategy relies on the previous sampling results, which
means that there might be parts of function evaluations that cannot
be appropriately applied at the start of the optimization. One feasible
alternative is to select a targeted sampling strategy by analyzing the
convergence or diversity of the candidates at the current stage. The al-
gorithm proposed in this work is designed for expensive medium-scale
MOPs. Therefore, there are some challenges in solving expensive high-
dimensional MOPs. For example, how to construct accurate surrogate
models [21] and how to design an effective basic optimizer [69], etc.
In the future, we will further improve this work so that it can be used
to solve expensive high-dimensional MOPs.
