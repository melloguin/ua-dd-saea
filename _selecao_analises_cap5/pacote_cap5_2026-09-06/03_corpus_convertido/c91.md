#### **1. Introduction** 

Diverterless supersonic inlet (DSI), also referred as bump inlet for the supersonic compression surface is a 3-Dimensional bump, was developed by Lockheed Martin LMT in the early 1990s on the basis of the pioneer NASA work in the 1950s [1]. The DSI does not have boundary-layer splitter plates, bleed system and movable compression ramps. Therefore, the integrated design of compression surface and forward fuselage can be realized, which makes it perform better in weight and stealth reduction. Compared with “conventional” supersonic ramp inlet, DSI has higher total pressure recovery, lower distortion and better compatibility between supersonic and subsonic flight conditions [2]. The concept of 3-D compression bump should account for excellent characteristics of the DSI as mentioned above. In [3], Kim parameterized bump geometry of a supersonic external-compression bump inlet with variables of height, length and width to study the effect of bump shape pa-

The shape optimization design of an inlet based on CFD simulation requires a large number of time-consuming objective function evaluations, which raises a significant computational challenge for repetitive aerodynamic performance analysis during optimization loop. Most of research works in this field primarily focus on obtaining a fine balance between computational effort and convergence ability. Therefore, the gradient-based optimization algorithms are always nice choices. However, calculating derivative information of objective functions in aerodynamic optimization is a painful procedure, and the gradient-based optimization methods are always poor in global search ability. Moreover, these traditional algorithms have many other difficulties in solving multi-objective optimization problems, such as the objectives must be converted into a single objective that extremely relies on preferences of decision maker. The new population-based intelligent optimization algorithm (such as PSO and EA) prevails over these traditional algorithms in all aspects except that the optimization needs numerous function evaluations. Direct coupling meta-heuristic algorithms with expensive functions is unadvisable. But combining them with surrogate model is another feasible method to solve these expensive optimization problems [7,9,10], where the expensive black-box functions are replaced by approximation method for reducing the call number of objective function. For single-objective optimization problem, a lot of efficient optimization algorithms on the basis of the surrogate model have been published [16–19]. The most famous surrogate-based global optimization method was proposed by Jones [16], named as efficient global optimization (EGO), which updates surrogate model by maximum expected improvement (EI) samples with mean value and variance of Kriging model prediction. Knowles [20] extended the EGO to solve multi-objective problems. Zhang et al. [21] incorporated EI into MOEA/D to reduce the overhead for modeling and to improve the prediction quality for dealing with expensive multi-objective optimization. In [22], a Kriging-based multi-objective genetic algorithm (K-MOGA) was presented, in which some design points were evaluated on-line using Kriging model instead of real mathematical model. Jie et al. [45] referred to the thought of EGO and proposed a generalized EI based on multiple Kriging model for expensive black-box multiobjective optimization. 

Besides, Emmerich et al. [23,24] proposed a new expected hyper-volume improvement (EHVI) updating criterion for Kriging model on the basis of non-dominated solutions in multi-objective optimization, which updates surrogate model using a single criterion rather than giving many EIs as the objective functions. Research shows that the new sample updating method is effective on optimizing large number of objective functions problems [26–28]. Literature [29] developed a surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm (EA) that is grounded on the cooperation between evolutionary algorithm and Kriging-based algorithm featuring the EHVI criterion. The new algorithm has been compared with some other Kriging-assisted algorithms over several test functions, and the 

simulation results demonstrated that this algorithm can achieve high performance. 

In surrogate-assisted multi-objective optimization, the EHVI is frequently called during the loop of optimization. To decrease time consumption of calling EHVI operator, Couckuyt et al. [30] proposed an exact EHVI calculation method, which is much faster than original calculation technology presented in [24]. More recently, Emmerich et al. [31] provided an asymptotically optimal algorithm for the bi-objective case and Yang et al. [32] presented a new integration scheme to calculate EHVI in three-objective case, respectively. The fast computing algorithms mentioned above focus on decreasing computation complexity of hyper-volume integration. We propose a new dynamic EHVI calculation method, which focuses on reducing the number of non-dominated solutions to engage in partitioning hyper-volume grid in order to improve computation efficiency of EHVI in macroscopic. 

This paper employs the dynamic EHVI to update Kringing model adaptively and blends the surrogate model with the crowding distance-based multi-objective PSO (MOPSO-CD) [13] to build an efficient multi-objective optimization framework. The new surrogate-based algorithm is named as EHVIMOPSO. The simulation results on several benchmark functions show that the proposed algorithm can effectively reduce the call number of objective functions and preserve search precision. Based on the proposed algorithm, the multi-objective aerodynamic optimization for a DSI inlet is performed. Due to the fact that the low-fidelity analysis technologies on basis of Euler equations or velocity potential equations can not capture the shock wave and flow separation precisely and have a large amount of analytical errors, high-fidelity CFD simulations based on RANS equations and hexahedral mesh are adopted to understand effects of forward fuselage and compression bump integration in the flow field during our DSI optimization. The surrogate-based optimal DSI shape design study is executed to improve aerodynamic performances in two design points.

#### _3.1.2. MOPSO-CD_ 

The multiple start point search of PSO makes it particularly suitable for solving multi-objective problems. Coello et al. proposed the first multi-objective optimization algorithm (MOPSO) in 2002 [12]. This algorithm uses an external archive to store nondominated solutions in the optimization process, and selects global best solution for each particle from the external archive. After that, the remarkable performance of MOPSO attracts many researchers to pay more and more attention to study it. 

In our study, we choose a simple multi-objective particle swarm optimization as the main algorithm, which utilizes crowding distance to select global best for guiding the particles moving and maintaining the external archive, so named it as crowding distance MOPSO (MOPSO-CD). #### _3.2.2. Expected hyper-volume improvement (EHVI)_ 

For a multi-objective optimization problem, two or more objective functions need to be considered. So it is inappropriate to use the EI function directly to measure the improvement probability of unobserved points over a Pareto front. Therefore, Emmerich [23,24] proposed the EHVI sampling criterion to handle multiobjective optimization problems, which is developed on the basis of the theory of hyper-volume indicator [25]. The hyper-volume metric is widely used to assess the quality of Pareto front given by multi-objective optimization algorithm. The EHVI is a unary indicator, which measures the expected value of how much the hyper-volume indicator may be improved with a new unobserved points predicted by Kriging surrogate model.

In order to calculate the EHVI value, multiple integrals need to be conducted over a hyper-volume integration region. As the shape of integration region is often irregular, the area must be partitioned into a series of rectangular cells (cells of hyper-volume grid) by non-dominated solutions. Analytically identify these cells and integrate EI values over them are rather complex for a higher number of Pareto solutions or many objective functions. Some researches [24,30–32] have been reported to improve its efficiency focusing on decreasing the calculate complexity of hyper-volume integration. Instead, the authors propose to reduce the number of non-dominated solutions to engage in partitioning hyper-volume grid in order to lower the computation cost.

   - solutions of _REP_ and sort them in ascending order, choose _N_ particles from top part of the sorted particles as new _SWARM_ . 

   - j. Increment the iteration counter _t_ ; Construct Kriging model via updated sample set. 

- 6) End. 

Notes that, the symbol _IFD_ is an approximate value of infeasibility degree, which is calculated on the basis of the mean values predicted by Kriging model, it is different from the real infeasibility degree _IFD_ . For simplicity, name the proposed algorithm as EHVIMOPSO, which is an abbreviation of expected hyper-volume improvement multi-objective particle swarm optimization. 

The details of EHVIMOPSO show that the number of call exactcostly functions is 3 (2 of temporary external archive plus 1 of feasible particles archive) in each iteration besides the part is to evaluate the initial samples by LHD method before the main loop. In addition, the proposed algorithm does not rely on the number of initial samples to build an extremely accurate Kriging model, because we adopt the EHVI function for selecting some promising unobserved points to update the sample set adaptively. Overall, it is a small number of call real objective functions to finish the optimization process.

#### **5. Conclusions** 

The use of surrogate model within population-based optimization algorithm is beneficial for dealing with expensive function evaluations optimization. In this study, an efficient multi-objective aerodynamic optimization on DSI inlet has been performed by surrogated-based MOPSO coupled with 3D CFD simulation. The major contribution of this work is listed as follows: 

Firstly, an efficient EHVIMOPSO algorithm is developed, which hybrids the MOPSO method with self-adaptive Kriging surrogate model. By virtue of EHVI criterion, some promising particles of whole population are evaluated, the external archive of MOPSO and Kriging model are updated. The call number of real objective function can be reduced greatly and the optimization accuracy is preserved. 

Next, for lightening the computation complexity of EHVI operator, a dynamic sample criterion is proposed, which cuts the hypervolume grid with the different subset of Pareto optimal front. The dynamic operator is faster than the original one. 

Finally, based on the proposed optimization algorithm, FFD method and CFD simulation, an automated multi-objective optimization system for optimizing aerodynamic performance by adjusting compression bump shape of DSI inlet has been built. Its computation burden is light and design efficiency is high. A Pareto front with 14 solutions is obtained after 130 costly CFD calls. The simulation of selected solution shows that the total pressure distortions of DSI are reduced by 1.08% and 6.5% from that of the initial configuration in two design points.

This paper presents an eﬃcient multi-objective optimization method, focusing on aerodynamic optimi-
zation of a diverterless supersonic inlet (DSI) in transonic and supersonic ﬂight conditions. The DSI 
inlet, through scrutinizing the Bump shape has potential to attain greater aerodynamic performance on 
exit plane of inlet. However, the high cost of computational ﬂuid dynamic (CFD) simulations raises a 
signiﬁcant challenge in the DSI optimization process. In order to obtain solution set in few numbers of 
objective function calls, a meta-model multi-objective particle swarm optimization (MOPSO) method is 
proposed based on a self-adaptive Kriging surrogate model, and applied to solve this kind of costly black-
box optimization problem. The Kriging model is updated by using a dynamic expected hyper-volume 
improvement (EHVI) sample metric, which is developed by analyzing disadvantages of the original sample 
criterion. With the help of the dynamic sample metric, simulation results show that the surrogate-based 
MOPSO algorithm can obtain plenty enough non-dominated solutions and achieve high precision in the 
approximation of the Pareto front. In terms of DSI inlet optimization, the bump shape is parameterized 
by free form deformation (FFD) method, and the total pressure distortions of inlet exit plane are treated 
as two minimization objectives under transonic and supersonic ﬂight conditions. A well distributed non-
dominated solution set is generated by the proposed algorithm within the context of a small call number 
of cost evaluations, and optimized inlet conﬁgurated by the selected solution has better aerodynamic 
characteristics compared with the initial inlet.

1. Introduction
Diverterless supersonic inlet (DSI), also referred as bump inlet 
for the supersonic compression surface is a 3-Dimensional bump, 
was developed by Lockheed Martin LMT in the early 1990s on the 
basis of the pioneer NASA work in the 1950s [1]. The DSI does 
not have boundary-layer splitter plates, bleed system and movable 
compression ramps. Therefore, the integrated design of compres-
sion surface and forward fuselage can be realized, which makes 
it perform better in weight and stealth reduction. Compared with 
“conventional” supersonic ramp inlet, DSI has higher total pressure 
recovery, lower distortion and better compatibility between super-
sonic and subsonic ﬂight conditions [2]. The concept of 3-D com-
pression bump should account for excellent characteristics of the 
DSI as mentioned above. In [3], Kim parameterized bump geome-
try of a supersonic external-compression bump inlet with variables 
of height, length and width to study the effect of bump shape pa-

with expensive functions is unadvisable. But combining them with

most famous surrogate-based global optimization method was pro-
posed by Jones [16], named as eﬃcient global optimization (EGO), 
which updates surrogate model by maximum expected improve-
ment (EI) samples with mean value and variance of Kriging model 
prediction. Knowles [20] extended the EGO to solve multi-objective 
problems. Zhang et al. [21] incorporated EI into MOEA/D to reduce 
the overhead for modeling and to improve the prediction quality 
for dealing with expensive multi-objective optimization. In [22], 
a Kriging-based multi-objective genetic algorithm (K-MOGA) was 
presented, in which some design points were evaluated on-line us-
ing Kriging model instead of real mathematical model. Jie et al. 
[45] referred to the thought of EGO and proposed a generalized 
EI based on multiple Kriging model for expensive black-box multi-
objective optimization.

hyper-volume improvement (EHVI) updating criterion for Kriging 
model on the basis of non-dominated solutions in multi-objective 
optimization, which updates surrogate model using a single cri-

search shows that the new sample updating method is effective on 
optimizing large number of objective functions problems [26–28].

tionary algorithm and Kriging-based algorithm featuring the EHVI 
criterion. The new algorithm has been compared with some other 
Kriging-assisted algorithms over several test functions, and the 
simulation results demonstrated that this algorithm can achieve 
high performance.

frequently called during the loop of optimization. To decrease time 
consumption of calling EHVI operator, Couckuyt et al. [30] pro-
posed an exact EHVI calculation method, which is much faster 
than original calculation technology presented in [24]. More re-
cently, Emmerich et al. [31] provided an asymptotically optimal 
algorithm for the bi-objective case and Yang et al. [32] presented a 
new integration scheme to calculate EHVI in three-objective case, 
respectively. The fast computing algorithms mentioned above focus 
on decreasing computation complexity of hyper-volume integra-
tion. We propose a new dynamic EHVI calculation method, which 
focuses on reducing the number of non-dominated solutions to 
engage in partitioning hyper-volume grid in order to improve com-
putation eﬃciency of EHVI in macroscopic.
This paper employs the dynamic EHVI to update Kringing 
model adaptively and blends the surrogate model with the crowd-
ing distance-based multi-objective PSO (MOPSO-CD) [13] to build 
an eﬃcient multi-objective optimization framework. The new 
surrogate-based algorithm is named as EHVIMOPSO. The simula-
tion results on several benchmark functions show that the pro-
posed algorithm can effectively reduce the call number of objective 
functions and preserve search precision. Based on the proposed al-
gorithm, the multi-objective aerodynamic optimization for a DSI 
inlet is performed. Due to the fact that the low-ﬁdelity analysis 
technologies on basis of Euler equations or velocity potential equa-
tions can not capture the shock wave and ﬂow separation precisely 
and have a large amount of analytical errors, high-ﬁdelity CFD 
simulations based on RANS equations and hexahedral mesh are 
adopted to understand effects of forward fuselage and compression 
bump integration in the ﬂow ﬁeld during our DSI optimization. The 
surrogate-based optimal DSI shape design study is executed to im-
prove aerodynamic performances in two design points.

3.1.2. MOPSO-CD
The multiple start point search of PSO makes it particularly 
suitable for solving multi-objective problems. Coello et al. pro-
posed the ﬁrst multi-objective optimization algorithm (MOPSO) in 
2002 [12]. This algorithm uses an external archive to store non-
dominated solutions in the optimization process, and selects global 
best solution for each particle from the external archive. After that, 
the remarkable performance of MOPSO attracts many researchers 
to pay more and more attention to study it.
In our study, we choose a simple multi-objective particle swarm 
optimization as the main algorithm, which utilizes crowding dis-
tance to select global best for guiding the particles moving and 
maintaining the external archive, so named it as crowding dis-
tance MOPSO (MOPSO-CD). MOPSO-CD was reported by Raquel et 
al. [13] in 2005, and this algorithm can be easily implemented in 
simple ﬂow, which has remarkable robustness [45]. The procedure 
of MOPSO-CD is described as follows:
Step 1. Set population size N, external archive size NREP, muta-
tion rate pMut, max iteration number Max_Iter; Initialize external 
archive A = φ and iterative counter t = 0.
Step 2. For each particle: randomly generate position; initialize ve-
locity as zero; evaluate objective values; initialize personal best.
Step 3. Perform Pareto dominance compare and store non-domi-
nated solutions in A.
Step 4. Repeat procedures as follows until t > Max_Iter:
Step 4.1: Computes the crowding distance values of each solution 
in A, and sort the non-dominated solutions in descending crowd-
ing distance values;
Step 4.2: For each particle in population:
(a) Randomly select the global best guide pg from 10% top part 
of sorted solutions;
(b) Update velocity and position according to Eq. (2) and Eq. (3);
(c) If particle goes beyond the boundary, the value of position 
takes the value of the corresponding boundary and its velocity 
is multiplied by (−1) so that it ﬂies in the opposite direction;
(d) Perform mutation on particle if t < Max_Iter∗pMut;
(e) Evaluate objective values of particle and update personal best 
according to Eq. (4);
Step 4.3: Set t = t + 1; identify non-dominated solutions in pop-
ulation and add them into A; remove any dominated solutions 
from A;
Step 4.4: If the number of solutions in A is greater than external 
archive size, delete redundant solutions taking advantage of crowd-
ing distance values.
Step 5. Output and end.

3.2.3. Dynamic expected hyper-volume improvement
In order to calculate the EHVI value, multiple integrals need 
to be conducted over a hyper-volume integration region. As the 
shape of integration region is often irregular, the area must be 
partitioned into a series of rectangular cells (cells of hyper-volume 
grid) by non-dominated solutions. Analytically identify these cells 
and integrate EI values over them are rather complex for a higher 
number of Pareto solutions or many objective functions. Some re-
searches [24,30–32] have been reported to improve its eﬃciency 
focusing on decreasing the calculate complexity of hyper-volume 
integration. Instead, the authors propose to reduce the number of 
non-dominated solutions to engage in partitioning hyper-volume 
grid in order to lower the computation cost.
As mentioned above, the multivariate response of the Kriging 
model is Y j(x) ∼N(
⌢y j(x), 
⌢σ j2(x)), j = 1, ..., k, and the probability 
density function of the uni-dimensional Gaussian distribution often 
is represented as a bell-shaped curve. So, its probability density 
is mainly distributed in the relatively concentrated region deter-
mined by mean value and standard deviation. The probability den-
sity of position, which is far away from the mean value become 
smaller and smaller, is zero to inﬁnity. This distribution charac-
teristic determines that the expected hyper-volume improvement 
of multivariate response predicted by Kriging model at an unob-
served position over the current Pareto solution set is concentrated 
in some grid cells, while the others are zero or negligible. Take a 
bi-objective case as an example. The probability density distribu-
tion obeying normal distribution in a bi-objective space is shown 
in Fig. 2. The expected improvement contribution of the predicted 
vector μ to the Pareto solution set is mainly concentrated in the 
cells delimited by the dashed line. In this case, we can simply 
take advantage of point 3 to point 7 counts from left to right to 
generate a hyper-volume grid. The number of the integration cells 
is only 16; more than 80% hyper-volume grid cells have been re-
duced.
We can learn from the theory of the self-adaptive meta-model 
that the precision of the Kriging model is low at the beginning

consideration. Therefore, the further research coupling supersonic 
compression surface with S-duct will be studied in future works.
5. Conclusions
The use of surrogate model within population-based optimiza-
tion algorithm is beneﬁcial for dealing with expensive function 
evaluations optimization. In this study, an eﬃcient multi-objective 
aerodynamic optimization on DSI inlet has been performed by 
surrogated-based MOPSO coupled with 3D CFD simulation. The 
major contribution of this work is listed as follows:
Firstly, an eﬃcient EHVIMOPSO algorithm is developed, which 
hybrids the MOPSO method with self-adaptive Kriging surrogate 
model. By virtue of EHVI criterion, some promising particles of 
whole population are evaluated, the external archive of MOPSO 
and Kriging model are updated. The call number of real objective 
function can be reduced greatly and the optimization accuracy is 
preserved.
Next, for lightening the computation complexity of EHVI opera-
tor, a dynamic sample criterion is proposed, which cuts the hyper-
volume grid with the different subset of Pareto optimal front. The 
dynamic operator is faster than the original one.
Finally, based on the proposed optimization algorithm, FFD 
method and CFD simulation, an automated multi-objective opti-
mization system for optimizing aerodynamic performance by ad-
justing compression bump shape of DSI inlet has been built. Its 
computation burden is light and design eﬃciency is high. A Pareto 
front with 14 solutions is obtained after 130 costly CFD calls. The 
simulation of selected solution shows that the total pressure dis-
tortions of DSI are reduced by 1.08% and 6.5% from that of the 
initial conﬁguration in two design points.
