# An improved bagging ensemble surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm for expensive many-objective optimization 

Qinghua Gu<sup>1,2</sup> & Xiaoyue Zhang<sup>1,2</sup> & Lu Chen<sup>1,2</sup> & Naixue Xiong<sup>3</sup> 

Accepted: 22 July 2021 # The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer Science+Business Media, LLC, part of Springer Nature 2021 

#### Abstract 

When the surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm is used to solve expensive many-objective optimization problems, the surrogate is used to approximate the expensive fitness functions. However, with the increase of the number of objectives, the approximate error of the surrogate will accumulate gradually, and the computational cost will also increase sharply. This paper proposes an improved bagging ensemble surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm (IBE-CSEA) to solve these problems. An ensemble classifier is used to classify the offspring instead of building the surrogate to approximate the fitness function of each objective. Firstly, a group of classification boundary individuals are selected one by one from the individuals evaluated by the expensive fitness function. All the individuals evaluated by the expensive fitness function are divided into two categories; Secondly, the individuals in these two categories are divided into the training set and the test set. The training set is used to train an improved bagging ensemble classifier. The test set is used to calculate the reliability of the classification; Finally, the classification results and the reliability are used to select the promising individuals for expensive fitness function evaluation. Compared with the current popular surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm, IBE-CSEA algorithm is more competitive.

Solving many-objective optimization problems (MaOPs) that involve four or more objectives has been the main research field in evolutionary multi-objective optimization [1]. Most of the existing evolutionary algorithms (EAs) for solving MaOPs are based on a default assumption. That is, the objective conditions and constraints for evaluating candidate solutions are simple and cheap [2, 3]. However, when solving the practical optimization problems [4, 5], such as aviation design optimization [6], drug design [7] and vehicle crashworthiness optimization design [8], the calculation cost of fitness evaluation (FE) is very high. These problems are defined as expensive many-objective optimization problems. The traditional EAs for MaOPs are unsuited for the expensive MaOPs because the computational cost will increase gradually with the objective number growthing. A common way to solve expensive optimization problems is to introduce a surrogate model into evolutionary algorithm [9]. It is called surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm (SAEA) [4]. In the iterative process of SAEA, the surrogate model with small amount of calculation is used to replace the expensive fitness function evaluation or

pre-screen a large number of individuals, so as to reduce the amount of calculation. According to the different functions of the surrogate models, we divide the existing SAEAs into two categories. 

For the first and the most common type of SAEA, the surrogate model is used to approximate the expensive fitness function [10, 11]. Up to now, a large number of studies are devoted to finding better methods to establish these surrogate models, such as the polynomial regression [12], Kriging [13] and artificial neural network(ANN) [14]. For example, K- RVEA [15] uses the Kriging to approximate each objective, which effectively reduced the cost of computation. 

For the second kind of SAEAs, the surrogate model is regarded as a classifier, which is used to filter the bad candidate solutions to reduce the amount of computation. In the evolutionary algorithms, the fitness of individuals represents the quality of individuals. Generally, individuals with higher fitness are chosen to enter the subsequent generation, and individuals with lower fitness will be eliminated directly. In the selection process of the evolutionary algorithm, what works is the relative fitness of individuals. In recent years, some researchers have pointed out that the SAEA does not need the accurate surrogate model for the objective function because the selection problem in evolutionary algorithm is essentially a classification problem [16]. That means when the new individual is predicted by this kind of surrogate, the value obtained is no longer a specific fitness value but a simple label to judge whether the new individual is better or worse than the reference point. Then, a classifier is trained by these sample points and used to classify the newly offspring solutions. 

When the first kind of SAEAs are used for expensive many-objective optimization, surrogate models are usually established for all objectives [15]. However, when the second kind of SAEAs based on classification are adopted, we only need to build one model to judge the dominant and nondominant relationship of the solutions, which dramatically reduces the computational complexity of the algorithm and makes the algorithm more efficient. So far, the research on SAEAs based on classification is relatively few. In 2015, a classification and Pareto domination based multi-objective evolutionary algorithm (CPS-MOEA) was proposed [16], the solutions are divided into two classes, one positive class and one negative class. Then, the classification regression tree is used to predict the class of new generation offspring. In 2018, a classification-based surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm (CSEA) was proposed [17], which uses the artificial neural network to judge the dominant relationship between the candidate solutions and a group. Simultaneously, considering the uncertain information and dominating relationship in the prediction. 

In addition to the above classification regression tree and artificial neural network, there are also classification models in 

the machine learning, such as multinomial logistic regression [18], support vector machines [19], k nearest neighbor [20], ensemble [21] and so on. When the SAEA based on classification is used to solve expensive MaOPs, the main challenge is to train a surrogate model with high quality using the limited training sample. It is difficult to choose a most suitable classifier to assist the evolutionary algorithm to find a set of optimal solutions. Therefore, a natural idea is to combine multiple base classifiers to form a strong classifier. According to [22], ensemble constructs a strong learner by combining multiple base learners, which has proved to be superior to a single learner in terms of accuracy and robustness. Bagging method in ensemble can make full use of the limited samples to construct a large model pool by using the bootstrap technology. In this paper, we propose an improved bagging ensemble surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm to solve the expensive MaOPs. Firstly, a set of classification boundary individuals are selected to construct a Pareto classification boundary by the one-by-one selection strategy [23]. The population is divided into two external archives, which are called group A and group B. Group A contains the non-dominated individuals, and group B contains the dominant individuals. To improve the accuracy of the surrogate model, an improved bagging ensemble is proposed as the surrogate. According to the reliability of classification [17], the model is updated from the promising solutions of offspring solutions. The main innovations of this paper are as follows: 

- (1) The algorithm adopts the one-by-one selection strategy to pick out a set of classification boundary solutions. These solutions are evaluated by the expensive fitness function, which is used to divide the solutions in archives into two categories. 

- (2) An improved bagging ensemble classifier is proposed as the surrogate, and a model selection strategy is adopted before the model combination, which effectively improves the classification accuracy of the surrogate. 

The rest of the paper are prepared as follows. Related techniques are reviewed in the second section. The third section introduces the improved bagging ensemble surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm. In the fourth section, the experiments of IBE-CSEA are introduced and compared with the latest surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms. The fifth section is the conclusion and future work.

### 2.1 Ensemble` 

Ensemble is a machine learning method, which can combine a group of base learners into a strong learner. In terms of accuracy and robustness, ensemble learning has been proved to be more advantageous than a single learner. Boosting [24] and bagging [25] are two popular methods to generate the ensemble model. Boosting uses the idea of serialization, which increases the weight of the samples with prediction errors of the previous base learner, so that the subsequent base learners can pay more attention to the samples with prediction errors to correct these errors as much as possible until the total number of base learners reaches the preset T, and finally combines the T base learners. Bagging uses parallel thought. The training of base learners is not sequential. They can carried out at the same time. Given an original data set containing m samples, a sample is randomly chosen from the original data set. Then the extracted sample is put back into the original data set, so that this sample can still chosen in the subsequent sampling. Thus, after m random sampling operations, a sample set containing m samples can be obtained. In this way, we can sample T samples with m training samples, train the base learner based on each sample set, and then combine these base learners. The bagging process is shown in Fig. 1.

### 3.3 The improved bagging ensemble classifier 

Bagging and boosting methods in ensemble learning can both effectively improve the accuracy of classification. Among them, bagging can save a lot of time through parallel training. At the same time, it uses bootstrap technology to sample, which can change the distribution of training samples of each base learner, then further enhance the generalization ability of the model. It is suitable for the case of small training samples. Therefore, the algorithm uses bagging based ensemble method. We divided group A and group B into a training set and a test set in a 3:1 proportion, respectively. The training set is adopted to train the improved bagging ensemble classifier, and the test set is adopted to test the accuracy. Ensemble method combines multiple models which can obtain more accurate classification results than a single model. In the past ensemble learning methods, after generating the base learners, it is usually a combination of all the base learners. Although this method had achieved good results, some researchers such 

as Zhou et al. [28] proposed that the model “many is better than all”, which means that selecting some base learners can have the same or better performance than using all base learners. So far, theoretical and empirical studies show that better classification accuracy and generalization performance can be achieved by using a subset of the ensemble instead of the whole ensemble [29–31]. When solving the expensive MaOPs, due to the scarcity of training samples, the accuracy of each base learner trained is not high. We propose an improved bagging ensemble to classify the candidate solutions. The construction of the improved bagging ensemble is shown in algorithm 3. The improved bagging ensemble pruned the base learners with a model selection strategy before combining the base learners, and the based learners with the highest classification accuracy are selected as the final. This method improves the performance of the ensemble by removing the redundant and invalid base learners. The process of the improved bagging ensemble is shown in Fig. 3.

### 4.5 Results analysis of the DTLZ problems 

Tables 3 and 4 show the comparison results of IGD and HV of the seven algorithms during 20 independent runs on DTLZ and MaF, among which the best results are filled with gray. 

According to the experimental results, IBE-CSEA performed best in the DTLZ test problems. DTLZ1 and DTLZ3 have a large amount of local PFs, the difficulty of this kind of problem lies in its multimodal characteristics. Thus it is challenging for the solutions obtained by limited real objective functions to converge to the true PFs. IBE-CSEA shows the best results on DTLZ1 and DTLZ3, followed by CSEA. IBECSEA and CSEA use classifiers to explore the unknown space, which has more advantages in dealing with these multimodal problems with multiple locally optimal solutions. Simultaneously, the improved bagging ensemble can effectively increase the accuracy of classification, so as to enhancing the convergence efficiency. However, the HV results obtained in Table 4 show that the most of the HV of the seven algorithms in these two test problems are 0. ## 5 Conclusions 

### 4.7 Vehicle crashworthiness optimization design 

In order to verify the performance of the proposed algorithm in practical problems, we test the algorithms on the manyobjective vehicle crashworthiness optimization design [8]. In the field of vehicle industry, crashworthiness is an important indicator. However, the cost of the simulation experiment is extremely high. Vehicle crashworthiness optimization design is a 9 objectives optimization problem with optimization objectives such as abdomen load, rib deflection and pubic symptom force. The number of decision variable is 11. We use NSGA-III, CPS-MOEA, MOEA/D-EGO, K-RVEA, KTA2, CSEA and the proposed IBE-CSEA to solve this problem. Among them, the maximum FEs is set as 200, and the population size is set as 11d-1. The average value of IGD and HV obtained from 10 runs on the actual problem are used as the evaluation result. Note that when calculating the IGD value, the non-dominated set obtained by all algorithms is used as the reference set. Table 5 shows the IGD and HV values of the seven algorithms in this practical problem. From the table, KTA2 achieves the best results, followed by IBE-CSEA, K- RVEA, CSEA, MOEA/D-EGO, CPS-MOEA and NSGA-III. 

This paper proposed an improved bagging ensemble SAEA called IBE-CSEA to solve the expensive MaOPs. The proposed algorithm uses the one-by-one selection strategy to select a group of classification boundary individuals, which can be used to divide the solutions into two categories. An improved bagging ensemble classifier is proposed as a surrogate to predict the classes of new candidate solutions. The promising solutions are selected by the predict result and reliability configuration, then evaluated by the expensive fitness function evaluation. By using the surrogate, IBE-CSEA can use hundreds of FEs instead of thousands to solve the expensive MaOPs. We compared IBE-CSEA with five excellent SAEAs and one conventional evolutionary algorithm on the DTLZ, MaF test problems and a vehicle crashworthiness optimization design practical problem to prove its effectiveness. The results show that the performance of IBE-CSEA is competitive. 

The current works show that the classification-based SAEAs are very promising in SAEAs, while it needs further research in model management. In addition to the dominance relationship, applying other classification criterion in the classification-based SAEAs should also be studied.

Accepted: 22 July 2021

Abstract
When the surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm is used to solve expensive many-objective optimization problems, the
surrogate is used to approximate the expensive fitness functions. However, with the increase of the number of objectives, the
approximate error of the surrogate will accumulate gradually, and the computational cost will also increase sharply. This paper
proposes an improved bagging ensemble surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm (IBE-CSEA) to solve these problems. An
ensemble classifier is used to classify the offspring instead of building the surrogate to approximate the fitness function of each
objective. Firstly, a group of classification boundary individuals are selected one by one from the individuals evaluated by the
expensive fitness function. All the individuals evaluated by the expensive fitness function are divided into two categories;
Secondly, the individuals in these two categories are divided into the training set and the test set. The training set is used to train
an improved bagging ensemble classifier. The test set is used to calculate the reliability of the classification; Finally, the
classification results and the reliability are used to select the promising individuals for expensive fitness function evaluation.
Compared with the current popular surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm, IBE-CSEA algorithm is more competitive.

1 Introduction
Solving many-objective optimization problems (MaOPs) that
involve four or more objectives has been the main research
field in evolutionary multi-objective optimization [1]. Most of
the existing evolutionary algorithms (EAs) for solving MaOPs
are based on a default assumption. That is, the objective con-
ditions and constraints for evaluating candidate solutions are
simple and cheap [2, 3]. However, when solving the practical
optimization problems [4, 5], such as aviation design optimi-
zation [6], drug design [7] and vehicle crashworthiness opti-
mization design [8], the calculation cost of fitness evaluation
(FE) is very high. These problems are defined as expensive
many-objective optimization problems. The traditional EAs
for MaOPs are unsuited for the expensive MaOPs because
the computational cost will increase gradually with the objec-
tive number growthing. A common way to solve expensive
optimization problems is to introduce a surrogate model into
evolutionary algorithm [9]. It is called surrogate-assisted evo-
lutionary algorithm (SAEA) [4]. In the iterative process of
SAEA, the surrogate model with small amount of calculation
is used to replace the expensive fitness function evaluation or

For the first and the most common type of SAEA, the

devoted to finding better methods to establish these surrogate

For the second kind of SAEAs, the surrogate model is
regarded as a classifier, which is used to filter the bad candi-
date solutions to reduce the amount of computation. In the
evolutionary algorithms, the fitness of individuals represents
the quality of individuals. Generally, individuals with higher
fitness are chosen to enter the subsequent generation, and
individuals with lower fitness will be eliminated directly. In
the selection process of the evolutionary algorithm, what
works is the relative fitness of individuals. In recent years,
some researchers have pointed out that the SAEA does not
need the accurate surrogate model for the objective function
because the selection problem in evolutionary algorithm is
essentially a classification problem [16]. That means when
the new individual is predicted by this kind of surrogate, the
value obtained is no longer a specific fitness value but a sim-
ple label to judge whether the new individual is better or worse
than the reference point. Then, a classifier is trained by these
sample points and used to classify the newly offspring
solutions.
When the first kind of SAEAs are used for expensive
many-objective optimization, surrogate models are usually
established for all objectives [15]. However, when the second
kind of SAEAs based on classification are adopted, we only
need to build one model to judge the dominant and non-
dominant relationship of the solutions, which dramatically
reduces the computational complexity of the algorithm and
makes the algorithm more efficient. So far, the research on
SAEAs based on classification is relatively few. In 2015, a
classification and Pareto domination based multi-objective
evolutionary algorithm (CPS-MOEA) was proposed [16],
the solutions are divided into two classes, one positive class
and one negative class. Then, the classification regression tree
is used to predict the class of new generation offspring. In
2018, a classification-based surrogate-assisted evolutionary
algorithm (CSEA) was proposed [17], which uses the artificial
neural network to judge the dominant relationship between the
candidate solutions and a group. Simultaneously, considering
the uncertain information and dominating relationship in the
prediction.
In addition to the above classification regression tree and
artificial neural network, there are also classification models in
the machine learning, such as multinomial logistic regression
[18], support vector machines [19], k nearest neighbor [20],
ensemble [21] and so on. When the SAEA based on classifi-
cation is used to solve expensive MaOPs, the main challenge
is to train a surrogate model with high quality using the limited
training sample. It is difficult to choose a most suitable clas-
sifier to assist the evolutionary algorithm to find a set of opti-
mal solutions. Therefore, a natural idea is to combine multiple
base classifiers to form a strong classifier. According to [22],
ensemble constructs a strong learner by combining multiple
base learners, which has proved to be superior to a single
learner in terms of accuracy and robustness. Bagging method
in ensemble can make full use of the limited samples to con-
struct a large model pool by using the bootstrap technology. In
this paper, we propose an improved bagging ensemble surro-
gate-assisted evolutionary algorithm to solve the expen-
sive MaOPs. Firstly, a set of classification boundary indi-
viduals are selected to construct a Pareto classification
boundary by the one-by-one selection strategy [23]. The
population is divided into two external archives, which
are called group A and group B. Group A contains the
non-dominated individuals, and group B contains the
dominant individuals. To improve the accuracy of the
surrogate model, an improved bagging ensemble is pro-
posed as the surrogate. According to the reliability of
classification [17], the model is updated from the promis-
ing solutions of offspring solutions. The main innovations
of this paper are as follows:
(1)
The algorithm adopts the one-by-one selection strategy
to pick out a set of classification boundary solutions.
These solutions are evaluated by the expensive fitness
function, which is used to divide the solutions in archives
into two categories.
(2)
An improved bagging ensemble classifier is proposed as
the surrogate, and a model selection strategy is adopted
before the model combination, which effectively im-
proves the classification accuracy of the surrogate.
The rest of the paper are prepared as follows. Related tech-
niques are reviewed in the second section. The third section
introduces the improved bagging ensemble surrogate-assisted
evolutionary algorithm. In the fourth section, the experiments
of IBE-CSEA are introduced and compared with the latest
surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms. The fifth section
is the conclusion and future work.

2.1 Ensemble`
Ensemble is a machine learning method, which can combine a
group of base learners into a strong learner. In terms of accu-
racy and robustness, ensemble learning has been proved to be
more advantageous than a single learner. Boosting [24] and
bagging [25] are two popular methods to generate the ensem-
ble model. Boosting uses the idea of serialization, which in-
creases the weight of the samples with prediction errors of the
previous base learner, so that the subsequent base learners can
pay more attention to the samples with prediction errors to
correct these errors as much as possible until the total number
of base learners reaches the preset T, and finally combines the
T base learners. Bagging uses parallel thought. The training of
base learners is not sequential. They can carried out at the
same time. Given an original data set containing m samples,
a sample is randomly chosen from the original data set. Then
the extracted sample is put back into the original data set, so
that this sample can still chosen in the subsequent sampling.
Thus, after m random sampling operations, a sample set con-
taining m samples can be obtained. In this way, we can sample
T samples with m training samples, train the base learner
based on each sample set, and then combine these base
learners. The bagging process is shown in Fig. 1.

Initialization the population (lines 1–2): The initial pop-

Bagging and boosting methods in ensemble learning can both
effectively improve the accuracy of classification. Among
them, bagging can save a lot of time through parallel training.
At the same time, it uses bootstrap technology to sample,
which can change the distribution of training samples of each
base learner, then further enhance the generalization ability of
the model. It is suitable for the case of small training samples.
Therefore, the algorithm uses bagging based ensemble meth-
od. We divided group A and group B into a training set and a
test set in a 3:1 proportion, respectively. The training set is
adopted to train the improved bagging ensemble classifier,
and the test set is adopted to test the accuracy. Ensemble

accurate classification results than a single model. In the past
ensemble learning methods, after generating the base learners,
it is usually a combination of all the base learners. Although
this method had achieved good results, some researchers such

have the same or better performance than using all base
learners. So far, theoretical and empirical studies show that
better classification accuracy and generalization performance

the whole ensemble [29–31]. When solving the expensive

4.3 Algorithms under comparisons
We choose six algorithms to compare with IBE-CSEA, and
their brief introduction is as follow:
(1)
NSGA-III [1]: NSGA-III is a conventional EA for many-
objective. Although it is not a SAEA, it is additionally
used to prove that SAEAs can achieve the better perfor-
mance with a small amount of FEs compared with the
conventional EAs when solving expensive MaOPs.
(2)
MOEA/D-EGO [36]: MOEA/D-EGO first divides a
MOP into some single objective problems. Then estab-
lishes a surrogate for each sub-problem according to the
evaluated points.
(3)
CPS-MOEA [16]: CPS-MOEA is a classification-based
SAEA. It uses two external groups to train a classifier
and then uses the classification regression tree to filter
the newly generated candidate solutions, only the posi-
tive ones are retained.
(4)
K-RVEA [15]: The Kriging model is adopted to approx-
imate each objective function, which cuts down the cost
of computation.
(5)
KTA2 [37]: An influential point-insensitive model is
used to approximate each objective function.
Moreover, an adaptive infill criterion is proposed to de-
termine an appropriate sampling strategy for re-
evaluations using the expensive fitness function.
(6)
CSEA [17]: CSEA is a SAEA based on classification
which uses the artificial neural network as a surrogate
to predict the dominance between the candidate solutions
and a group solutions.

4.7 Vehicle crashworthiness optimization design
In order to verify the performance of the proposed algorithm
in practical problems, we test the algorithms on the many-
objective vehicle crashworthiness optimization design [8]. In
the field of vehicle industry, crashworthiness is an important
indicator. However, the cost of the simulation experiment is
extremely high. Vehicle crashworthiness optimization design
is a 9 objectives optimization problem with optimization ob-
jectives such as abdomen load, rib deflection and pubic symp-
tom force. The number of decision variable is 11. We use
NSGA-III, CPS-MOEA, MOEA/D-EGO, K-RVEA, KTA2,
CSEA and the proposed IBE-CSEA to solve this problem.
Among them, the maximum FEs is set as 200, and the popu-
lation size is set as 11d-1. The average value of IGD and HV
obtained from 10 runs on the actual problem are used as the
evaluation result. Note that when calculating the IGD value,
the non-dominated set obtained by all algorithms is used as the
reference set. Table 5 shows the IGD and HV values of the
seven algorithms in this practical problem. From the table,
KTA2 achieves the best results, followed by IBE-CSEA, K-
RVEA, CSEA, MOEA/D-EGO, CPS-MOEA and NSGA-III.
Although IBE-CSEA does not perform best in all algorithms,
it also shows strong competitiveness in practical problems.
5 Conclusions
This paper proposed an improved bagging ensemble SAEA
called IBE-CSEA to solve the expensive MaOPs. The pro-
posed algorithm uses the one-by-one selection strategy to se-
lect a group of classification boundary individuals, which can
be used to divide the solutions into two categories. An im-
proved bagging ensemble classifier is proposed as a surrogate
to predict the classes of new candidate solutions. The promis-
ing solutions are selected by the predict result and reliability
configuration, then evaluated by the expensive fitness
function evaluation. By using the surrogate, IBE-CSEA
can use hundreds of FEs instead of thousands to solve
the expensive MaOPs. We compared IBE-CSEA with five
excellent SAEAs and one conventional evolutionary algo-
rithm on the DTLZ, MaF test problems and a vehicle
crashworthiness optimization design practical problem to
prove its effectiveness. The results show that the perfor-
mance of IBE-CSEA is competitive.
The current works show that the classification-based
SAEAs are very promising in SAEAs, while it needs further
research in model management. In addition to the dominance
relationship, applying other classification criterion in the
classification-based SAEAs should also be studied.
