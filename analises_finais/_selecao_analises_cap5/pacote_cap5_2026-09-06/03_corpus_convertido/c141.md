#### **Abstract** 

Numerous surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms are developed for multi-objective expensive problems with low dimensions, but scarce works have paid attention to that with high dimensions, i.e., generally more than 30 decision variables. In this paper, we propose a multi-mode radial basis functions-assisted evolutionary algorithm (MMRAEA) for solving highdimensional expensive multi-objective optimization problems. To improve the reliability, the proposed algorithm uses radial basis functions based on three modes to cooperate to provide the qualities and uncertainty information of candidate solutions. Meanwhile, bi-population based on competitive swarm optimizer and genetic algorithm are applied for better exploration and exploitation in high-dimensional search space. Accordingly, an infill criterion based on multi-mode of radial basis functions that comprehensively considers the quality and uncertainty of candidate solutions is proposed. Experimental results on widelyused benchmark problems with up to 100 decision variables demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposal. Furthermore, the proposed method is applied to the structure optimization of the blended-wing-body underwater glider (BWBUG) and gets impressive solutions. 

**Keywords** Multi-objective optimization problem · High-dimensional · Expensive optimization · Surrogate ensemble · Structure design of BWBUG

Evolutionary algorithms show the capability of parallel search, which means a set of solutions can be obtained in a single run. It is precisely because of this attractive attribute of EAs, a multitude of multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs) have been developed during the past two decades [8–16]. However, it is extremely time-consuming to evaluate the fitness values for the MOPs that involve computationally expensive simulations or experiments, such as computational fluid dynamics (CFD) [18–20] and finite element analysis (FEA) [21, 22]. For example, it may take several minutes, even hours to handle an FEA simulation of a certain structure. Since a large number of real fitness evaluations (FEs) are required before a satisfying solution set is found by MOEAs, it is impractical to solve expensive MOPs. To deal with this challenge, surrogate models are introduced to replace the real fitness functions, and the time of predicting the function values by surrogate models can be neglected when compared with expensive simulations. Commonly used surrogate models include Kriging [23] (or Gaussian process), radial basis functions (RBFs) [24], response surface model (RSM) [25], support vector machines (SVMs) [26] and artificial neural networks (ANNs) [27], etc. The efficient global optimization (EGO) method applies Kriging to approximate the exact 

functions and mean squared errors to reflect the prediction variance [28, 29]. 

On the one hand, numerous surrogate-assisted MOEAs (SAMOEAs) have been proposed to solve expensive MOPs with low dimensions (i.e., the number of decision variables is no more than 30). In the Pareto-based EGO (ParEGO) [30], EGO is applied to a single-objective problem that is aggregated by a randomly selected weight vector in each iteration. For the EGO-assisted decomposition-based MOEA (MOEA/D-EGO) [31], a Kriging model for each subproblem is constructed, and these approximation models are optimized simultaneously by MOEA/D. In [32], the authors combine EA with an ensemble of SVMs with different parameters, and the SVM which has the lowest expected error is employed for a more accurate approximation. A Krigingassisted particle swarm optimizer is proposed in [33], where the re-evaluated individuals are selected according to their expected growth of the hypervolume. In [34], radial basis functions are employed to assist MOEA/D for Expensive MOPs, where a test suite with 10 and 30 decision variables and an airfoil optimization case are applied for experimental studies. In the Kriging-assisted reference vector-guided EA (K-RVEA), the quality and mean square errors of candidate solutions are considered for the balance between exploration and exploitation [35]. Instead of approximating the objective functions, in the classification-based pre-selection MOEA

(CPS-MOEA) [36], individuals are divided into ’good’ and ’bad’ groups to train a classifier, then the low-quality individuals in the newly generated population are filtered out by the classifier. The classification surrogate-based EA (CSEA) [37] applies an ANN to predict the dominance relationship between candidate solutions and the reference solutions. 

On the other hand, many surrogate-assisted EAs have been developed to deal with single-objective problems with high dimensions (i.e., problems with 30-100 decision variables) [23, 38–41]. In the Gaussian process-assisted evolutionary algorithm (GPEME) [23], a local surrogate model is applied to guide the evolutionary search. It also uses dimension reduction techniques to take advantage of the Gaussian process surrogate model in low-dimensional space. In the surrogate-assisted cooperative swarm optimization (SACOSO) and surrogate-assisted hierarchical particle swarm optimization (SHPSO) [38, 39], particle swarm optimizer (PSO) [43] and social learning-based PSO [44] cooperate to search for the global optimum. In the evolutionary sampling assisted optimization (ESAO) [40], global and local RBF models are respectively constructed to guide the selection of new samples, and global and local search alternates if one of them cannot achieve improvement. The surrogateassisted grey wolf optimization (SAGWO) [41] is developed to combine RBF and grey wolf optimization [42] for highdimensional expensive problems. 

However, it has not been paid much attention to expensive high-dimensional MOPs, even though they widely exist in real-world applications. In [45], a framework of MOEA assisted by a heterogeneous ensemble (HeE-MOEA) is proposed, where uncertainty of the approximated fitness is estimated by the outputs of all ensemble members, HeEMOEAhas beentestedonMOPs withupto80dimensions. In the efficient dropout neural network assisted indicator-based MOEA with reference point adaptation (EDN-ARMOEA) [46], an efficient dropout neural network is proposed as a computationally scalable alternative of the Gaussian process model for assisting the solution of expensive highdimensional MOPs, EDN-ARMOEA performs well on the test suites with up to100 decision variables. In the multiple classifiers-assisted evolutionary algorithm based on decomposition (MCEA/D) [17], SVM is built for each scalarization function as a classifier. MCEA/D shows prominent performance MOPs with up to 150 decision variables when compared with state-of-the-art SAEAs. 

High-dimensional MOPs are more difficult to be solved than MOPs with fewer decision variables, the main reasons are as follows. First, although Kriging model is widelyused in SAMOEAs since it can provide estimation errors, it become less practical for high-dimensional MOPs due to its high computational complexity (i.e., cubic in the size of training data). Second, with the increasing of decision variables, the approximation or prediction accuracy of the surrogate 

model based on a limited number of training samples is dropped dramatically. Moreover, because of the curse of dimensionality, it is hard to explore and exploit the searching space efficiently. Most existing surrogate-assisted MOEAs use the optimizer based on the Genetic algorithm (GA), whose efficiency is restricted on high-dimensional problems. 

To address the above issues, a multi-mode RBFs-assisted evolutionary algorithm (MMRAEA) is proposed in this paper for solving high-dimensional expensive MOPs. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows. 

1) Radial basis functions based on multiple modes are adopted to cooperate to estimate the quality and uncertainty of candidate solutions, thus better accuracy of prediction and reliability can be achieved. And sampling strategy based on multi-mode RBFs is formulated to select promising candidate solutions for re-evaluation. 

2) Bi-population based on competitive swarm optimizer (CSO) and GA are used for a better balance between exploration and exploitation of high-dimensional decision space. 

3) Our proposal is compared with state-of-the-art peer algorithms on a set of benchmark MOPs with 20, 40, 60, and 100 decision variables to verify its ability to solve highdimensional expensive MOPs. In addition, it is applied to the structure optimization of the frame of BWBUG to show its effectiveness on practical engineering problems.

## **The proposed method** 

TheproposedMMRAEAdevotestosolvinghigh-dimensional expensive MOPs with up to 100 decision variables. To build surrogate models and prediction efficiently for highdimensional MOPs, RBFs are selected as surrogate models. For accurate prediction of the quality of candidate solutions, three modes of RBFs are applied to cooperate to select the reevaluated individuals. Besides, bi-population based on CSO and GA is used for the purpose of exploring and exploiting the searching space effectively and capturing more promising candidate solutions. The frame of the proposed method is shown in Fig. 1, where the four gray blocks represent the four main components of MMRAEA. Moreover, the pseudo code of MMRAEA is presented in Algorithm 1, which can be divided into five main steps as follows. 

1) Initialization: An initial population _P_ with 11 _d_ −1 individuals is generated using Latin hypercube sampling (LHS) [52], where _d_ denotes the number of decision variables. These individuals are evaluated by the real fitness functions and added to the archive _A_ . 

(2) Construction of surrogate models: First, calculate the fitness values of individuals in _A_ by Eq. 7, and do nondominated sorting on the individuals in _A_ to obtain their front numbers, i.e., numbers represent nondominated levels. Then approximation model is built for each objective, and two types of direct prediction models are trained according to the fitness values and front numbers of candidate solutions, respectively. 

(3) Evolutionary optimization: MOEAs based on CSO and GA are applied to search the approximation models of the expensive functions respectively. After the optimization process is finished, bi-population merge to a total population _Ptot_ for the next stage. 

4) Sampling: Three modes of RBFs (i.e., approximation models, prediction model of fitness value, and prediction model of front number) cooperate to predict the quality (i.e., convergence and diversity) and uncertainty of _Ptot_ , and according to the prediction information, several promising candidate solutions are screened out for expensive evaluation and added to _A_ . 

5) Repeat steps 2) - 4) until the maximum number of FEs _(FEmax )_ is reached. In the remaining parts of the present section, steps 2), 3), and 4) will be elaborated, respectively.

### **Sampling strategy** 

It is crucial to the performance of SAEAs that choose individuals to be evaluated using expensive functions. For expensive MOPs, the quality (i.e., convergence and diversity) and uncertainty of candidate solutions as well as the balance between exploration and exploitation should be considered comprehensively. In the proposed method, fitness estimations of multi-mode RBFs cooperate to select individuals with respect to their quality or uncertainty for re-evaluation, thereby achieving a better balance between exploration and exploitation in high-dimensional space. The details of the sampling strategy are presented in Algorithm 3, where the 

final population _Ptot_ from evolutionary optimization undergoes three main steps to choose the new samples as follows. 

First is pre-selection, the intersection set _P_ between the _Ptot_ and the nondominated solution set of the union set of _Ptot_ and _A_ are obtained to guarantee the convergence of new samples. Note that the nondominated sorting is based on the predicted objective values of the approximation models ( _ARBFs_ ). Then three groups of fitness values of _P_ will be obtained from the approximation models, the fitness prediction model and the front number prediction model, respectively. ## **Application on BWBUG frame design** 

BWBUG has aroused huge attention during the past few years since its high water-dynamic performance [57], which is widely used for long-term ocean observation. Through adjusting its buoyancy and attitude, it can glide through seawater for thousands of kilometers. In this section, the proposed MMRAEA is applied for the optimization of a biobjective BWBUG’s frame design problem with 43 design variables. 

### **Description of the problem** 

Due to the complexity of the ocean environment, the robustness of the frame of BWBUG is of great significance. Meanwhile, the weight of the frame can influence portability and the extra carrying capacity of BWBUG, where a smaller weight is desired. Therefore, a 2-objective MOP is generated, i.e., minimizing the weight _(W )_ and the maximum equivalent stress _(σmax )_ of the frame simultaneously. Since the calculation of _W_ is not time-consuming and the calculation of _σmax_ requires FEA, which leads to different time-consuming degrees of the problem. For convenience, real weight values of the frame are used in the optimization process. 

Figure 10 presents an illustration of the 43 decision variables of the BWBUG frame. In this problem, 9 plane variables ( _l_ 1 _, l_ 2 _, l_ 3 _, l_ 4 _, l_ 5 _, d_ 1 _, d_ 2 _, d_ 3 _, d_ 4) are used to control the plane shape of the BWBUG and its frame, 4 parameters ( _t_ 1 _, t_ 2 _, t_ 3 _, t_ 4) are used to control the thickness of four types of beams. 5 section variables ( _ci_<sup>1</sup><sup>_, c_</sup> _i_<sup>2</sup><sup>_, c_</sup> _i_<sup>3</sup><sup>_, c_</sup> _i_<sup>4</sup><sup>_, c_</sup> _i_<sup>5) based on the</sup> CST method [59] are used to control each section shape, and 6 sections ( _i_ ∈{1 _,_ 2 _,_ 3 _,_ 4 _,_ 5 _,_ 6}) that employ the standard airfoils from NACA0014 to NACA0026 have 30 variables in total. Therefore, the BWBUG frame design problem has 43 decision variables in total, which is a high-dimensional MOP involving time-consuming simulations.

## **Conclusion** 

In this work, a multi-mode RBFs assisted evolutionary algorithm (MMRAEA) with bi-population based on CSO and GA is proposed to solve high-dimensional expensive MOPs. The main idea is to combine different modes of RBFs for better reliability and prediction accuracy in high-dimensional space. And a sampling strategy based on multi-mode RBFs that considers the quality and uncertainty of candidate solutions are developed. Furthermore, bi-population based on CSO and GA are employed in MMRAEA for a better balance between exploration and exploitation in high-dimensional space. 

The proposed MMRAEA is compared with several stateof-the-art SAMOEA that can apply to high-dimensional expensive MOPs on three sets of widely-used benchmark problems with up to 100 decision variables. Experimental results indicate the competitiveness and effectiveness of the proposed method. In addition, MMRAEA is applied to a 2-objective BWBUG frame design problem with 43 decision variables. And the optimization results demonstrate that MMRAEA is able to obtain a satisfactory solution set on the high-dimensional expensive engineering MOP.

However, several weaknesses of the proposed methods are as follows. Firstly, MMRAEA only uses RBFs as surrogate models, which may be caught in a dilemma when the RBF models show low accuracy. Secondly, MMRAEA is not suitable for handling many-objective problems since it uses selection strategies based on the Pareto dominance relation. Lastly, MMRAEA runs with the assumption that fitness evaluation of each objective consumes the same time, which may cause low applicability on practical problems. 

In the future, we would like to modify the proposed method to better handle high-dimensional expensive MOPs with many objectives, with different evaluation times of different objectives, and with multiple constraints. Moreover, MMRAEA will be applied to the optimization and design of the autonomous underwater vehicle (AUV) battery packs [60].

Optimization of high-dimensional expensive multi-objective problems
using multi-mode radial basis functions
Jiangtao Shen1 · Xinjing Wang1 · Ruixuan He1 · Ye Tian2 · Wenxin Wang1 · Peng Wang1 · Zhiwen Wen3
Received: 16 December 2023 / Accepted: 19 August 2024 / Published online: 15 January 2025

Abstract
Numerous surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms are developed for multi-objective expensive problems with low dimen-
sions, but scarce works have paid attention to that with high dimensions, i.e., generally more than 30 decision variables. In
this paper, we propose a multi-mode radial basis functions-assisted evolutionary algorithm (MMRAEA) for solving high-
dimensional expensive multi-objective optimization problems. To improve the reliability, the proposed algorithm uses radial
basis functions based on three modes to cooperate to provide the qualities and uncertainty information of candidate solutions.
Meanwhile, bi-population based on competitive swarm optimizer and genetic algorithm are applied for better exploration and
exploitation in high-dimensional search space. Accordingly, an inﬁll criterion based on multi-mode of radial basis functions
that comprehensively considers the quality and uncertainty of candidate solutions is proposed. Experimental results on widely-
used benchmark problems with up to 100 decision variables demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposal. Furthermore, the
proposed method is applied to the structure optimization of the blended-wing-body underwater glider (BWBUG) and gets
impressive solutions.

Evolutionary algorithms show the capability of parallel
search, which means a set of solutions can be obtained in a
single run. It is precisely because of this attractive attribute of
EAs, a multitude of multi-objective evolutionary algorithms
(MOEAs) have been developed during the past two decades
[8–16]. However, it is extremely time-consuming to evaluate
the ﬁtness values for the MOPs that involve computationally
expensive simulations or experiments, such as computational
ﬂuid dynamics (CFD) [18–20] and ﬁnite element analysis
(FEA) [21, 22]. For example, it may take several minutes,
even hours to handle an FEA simulation of a certain struc-
ture. Since a large number of real ﬁtness evaluations (FEs) are
required before a satisfying solution set is found by MOEAs,
it is impractical to solve expensive MOPs. To deal with this
challenge, surrogate models are introduced to replace the
real ﬁtness functions, and the time of predicting the function
values by surrogate models can be neglected when compared
with expensive simulations. Commonly used surrogate mod-
els include Kriging [23] (or Gaussian process), radial basis
functions (RBFs) [24], response surface model (RSM) [25],
support vector machines (SVMs) [26] and artiﬁcial neural
networks (ANNs) [27], etc. The efﬁcient global optimiza-
tion (EGO) method applies Kriging to approximate the exact
functions and mean squared errors to reﬂect the prediction
variance [28, 29].
On the one hand, numerous surrogate-assisted MOEAs
(SAMOEAs) have been proposed to solve expensive MOPs
with low dimensions (i.e., the number of decision variables
is no more than 30). In the Pareto-based EGO (ParEGO)
[30], EGO is applied to a single-objective problem that is
aggregated by a randomly selected weight vector in each
iteration. For the EGO-assisted decomposition-based MOEA
(MOEA/D-EGO) [31], a Kriging model for each subproblem
is constructed, and these approximation models are opti-
mized simultaneously by MOEA/D. In [32], the authors
combine EA with an ensemble of SVMs with different
parameters, and the SVM which has the lowest expected error
is employed for a more accurate approximation. A Kriging-
assisted particle swarm optimizer is proposed in [33], where
the re-evaluated individuals are selected according to their
expected growth of the hypervolume. In [34], radial basis
functions are employed to assist MOEA/D for Expensive
MOPs, where a test suite with 10 and 30 decision variables
and an airfoil optimization case are applied for experimental
studies. In the Kriging-assisted reference vector-guided EA
(K-RVEA), the quality and mean square errors of candidate
solutions are considered for the balance between exploration
and exploitation [35]. Instead of approximating the objective
functions, in the classiﬁcation-based pre-selection MOEA

computationally scalable alternative of the Gaussian pro-
cess model for assisting the solution of expensive high-
dimensional MOPs, EDN-ARMOEA performs well on the
test suites with up to100 decision variables. In the multiple
classiﬁers-assisted evolutionary algorithm based on decom-
position (MCEA/D) [17], SVM is built for each scalarization
function as a classiﬁer. MCEA/D shows prominent per-
formance MOPs with up to 150 decision variables when
compared with state-of-the-art SAEAs.
High-dimensional MOPs are more difﬁcult to be solved
than MOPs with fewer decision variables, the main reasons
are as follows. First, although Kriging model is widely-
used in SAMOEAs since it can provide estimation errors, it
become less practical for high-dimensional MOPs due to its
high computational complexity (i.e., cubic in the size of train-
ing data). Second, with the increasing of decision variables,
the approximation or prediction accuracy of the surrogate
model based on a limited number of training samples is
dropped dramatically. Moreover, because of the curse of
dimensionality, it is hard to explore and exploit the searching
space efﬁciently. Most existing surrogate-assisted MOEAs
use the optimizer based on the Genetic algorithm (GA),
whose efﬁciency is restricted on high-dimensional problems.
To address the above issues, a multi-mode RBFs-assisted
evolutionary algorithm (MMRAEA) is proposed in this paper
for solving high-dimensional expensive MOPs. The main
contributions of this work are summarized as follows.
1) Radial basis functions based on multiple modes are
adopted to cooperate to estimate the quality and uncertainty
of candidate solutions, thus better accuracy of prediction and
reliability can be achieved. And sampling strategy based on
multi-mode RBFs is formulated to select promising candi-
date solutions for re-evaluation.
2) Bi-population based on competitive swarm optimizer
(CSO) and GA are used for a better balance between explo-
ration and exploitation of high-dimensional decision space.
3) Our proposal is compared with state-of-the-art peer
algorithms on a set of benchmark MOPs with 20, 40, 60,
and 100 decision variables to verify its ability to solve high-
dimensional expensive MOPs. In addition, it is applied to the
structure optimization of the frame of BWBUG to show its
effectiveness on practical engineering problems.

Sampling strategy
It is crucial to the performance of SAEAs that choose individ-
uals to be evaluated using expensive functions. For expensive
MOPs, the quality (i.e., convergence and diversity) and
uncertainty of candidate solutions as well as the balance
between exploration and exploitation should be considered
comprehensively. In the proposed method, ﬁtness estima-
tions of multi-mode RBFs cooperate to select individuals
with respect to their quality or uncertainty for re-evaluation,
thereby achieving a better balance between exploration and
exploitation in high-dimensional space. The details of the
sampling strategy are presented in Algorithm 3, where the
ﬁnal population Ptot from evolutionary optimization under-
goes three main steps to choose the new samples as follows.
First is pre-selection, the intersection set P between
the Ptot and the nondominated solution set of the union
set of Ptot and A are obtained to guarantee the conver-
gence of new samples. Note that the nondominated sorting
is based on the predicted objective values of the approx-
imation models (ARBFs). Then three groups of ﬁtness
values of P will be obtained from the approximation
models, the ﬁtness prediction model and the front num-
ber prediction model, respectively. The ﬁrst ﬁtness values
Fit1 = [Fit1,1, Fit1,1, . . . , Fit1,|P|] are calculated using
Eq. 7 and the approximated objective values. The second
ﬁtness values Fit2 = [Fit2,1, Fit2,1, . . . , Fit2,|P|] are pre-
dicted by Fit RBF, and the third ﬁtness values Fit3 =
[Fit3,1, Fit3,1, . . . , Fit3,|P|] are predicted by F N RBF
which is a set of continuous prediction values that repre-
sent the front number of solutions in P. And nondominated
sorting based on [−Fit1, −Fit2, Fit3] is conducted, only
the solutions in the ﬁrst front are selected for the next

Application on BWBUG frame design
BWBUG has aroused huge attention during the past few
years since its high water-dynamic performance [57], which
is widely used for long-term ocean observation. Through
adjusting its buoyancy and attitude, it can glide through
seawater for thousands of kilometers. In this section, the
proposed MMRAEA is applied for the optimization of a bi-
objective BWBUG’s frame design problem with 43 design
variables.
Description of the problem
Due to the complexity of the ocean environment, the robust-
ness of the frame of BWBUG is of great signiﬁcance.
Meanwhile, the weight of the frame can inﬂuence portability
and the extra carrying capacity of BWBUG, where a smaller
weight is desired. Therefore, a 2-objective MOP is generated,
i.e., minimizing the weight (W) and the maximum equivalent
stress (σmax) of the frame simultaneously. Since the calcu-
lation of W is not time-consuming and the calculation of
σmax requires FEA, which leads to different time-consuming
degrees of the problem. For convenience, real weight values
of the frame are used in the optimization process.
Figure 10 presents an illustration of the 43 decision
variables of the BWBUG frame. In this problem, 9 plane
variables (l1,l2,l3,l4,l5, d1, d2, d3, d4) are used to control
the plane shape of the BWBUG and its frame, 4 parameters
(t1, t2, t3, t4) are used to control the thickness of four types
of beams. 5 section variables (c1
i , c2
i , c3
i , c4
i , c5
i ) based on the
CST method [59] are used to control each section shape, and
6 sections (i ∈{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}) that employ the standard
airfoils from NACA0014 to NACA0026 have 30 variables
in total. Therefore, the BWBUG frame design problem has
43 decision variables in total, which is a high-dimensional
MOP involving time-consuming simulations.

Conclusion
In this work, a multi-mode RBFs assisted evolutionary algo-
rithm (MMRAEA) with bi-population based on CSO and GA
is proposed to solve high-dimensional expensive MOPs. The
main idea is to combine different modes of RBFs for bet-
ter reliability and prediction accuracy in high-dimensional
space. And a sampling strategy based on multi-mode RBFs
that considers the quality and uncertainty of candidate solu-
tions are developed. Furthermore, bi-population based on
CSO and GA are employed in MMRAEA for a better balance
between exploration and exploitation in high-dimensional
space.
The proposed MMRAEA is compared with several state-
of-the-art SAMOEA that can apply to high-dimensional
expensive MOPs on three sets of widely-used benchmark
problems with up to 100 decision variables. Experimental
results indicate the competitiveness and effectiveness of the
proposed method. In addition, MMRAEA is applied to a
2-objective BWBUG frame design problem with 43 deci-
sion variables. And the optimization results demonstrate that
MMRAEA is able to obtain a satisfactory solution set on the
high-dimensional expensive engineering MOP.

However, several weaknesses of the proposed methods
are as follows. Firstly, MMRAEA only uses RBFs as surro-
gate models, which may be caught in a dilemma when the
RBF models show low accuracy. Secondly, MMRAEA is not
suitable for handling many-objective problems since it uses
selection strategies based on the Pareto dominance relation.
Lastly, MMRAEA runs with the assumption that ﬁtness eval-
uation of each objective consumes the same time, which may
cause low applicability on practical problems.
In the future, we would like to modify the proposed
method to better handle high-dimensional expensive MOPs
with many objectives, with different evaluation times of dif-
ferent objectives, and with multiple constraints. Moreover,
MMRAEA will be applied to the optimization and design
of the autonomous underwater vehicle (AUV) battery packs
[60].
