**Abstract:** Many real-world expensive industrial and engineering multi-objective optimization problems (MOPs) are driven by historical, experimental, or simulation data. In such scenarios, due to the expensive cost and time required, we are only left with a small amount of labeled data to perform the optimization. These offline data-driven MOPs are usually solved by multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs) with the help of surrogate models constructed from offline historical data. The key challenge in developing these data-driven MOEAs is that they have to replace multiple conflicting fitness functions by approximating these objective functions, which may produce cumulative approximation errors and misguide the search. In order to build a reliable surrogate model from a small amount of multi-output offline data and solve the DDMOPs, we have proposed an adaptive model selection method with a reliable individual-based model management-driven MOEA. The proposed algorithm dynamically selects between DNN and XGBoost by comparing their k-fold cross-validation MAE error, which can capture the true generalization ability of the surrogates on unseen data. Then, the selected surrogate is updated with a reliable individual selection strategy, where the individual who is closest, both in the decision and objective space, to the most preferred solution among labeled offline data is chosen. As a result, these two strategies guide the underlying MOEA to the Pareto optimal solutions. The empirical results of the ZDT and DTLZ benchmark test suite validate the use of the three state-of-the-art offline DDMOEAs, showing that our algorithm is able to achieve highly competitive results in terms of convergence and diversity for 2–3 objectives. Finally, our algorithm is applied to an offline data-driven multi-objective problem—transonic airfoil (RAE 2822) shape optimization—to validate its efficiency on real-world DDMOPs. 

**Keywords:** data-driven multi-objective optimization; deep neural network; extreme gradient boosting; offline data-driven multi-objective evolutionary algorithm; surrogate models

Using the existing data, surrogates (also known as meta-models) may be constructed to approximately represent the underlying objective functions associated with the phenomena. These surrogates may be used for optimization by incorporating them into multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs), which have been shown to be effective in addressing black-box optimization problems [1]. There are two types of data-driven optimization problems (DDOPs), online and offline. When solving online data-driven optimization problems, it is feasible to collect new data, for example, by doing more expensive function evaluations, which allow the surrogates to be updated [2]. On the other hand, offline data-driven optimization [3] occurs when no new data can be collected during the optimization process. If the offline DDOP involves multiple conflicting objects (MOPs), then it is called as offline DDMOP, which is the major emphasis of this study. 

In the real world, there are a number of data-driven multi-objective optimization problems for which only historical data is accessible. For example, using imperfect and limited historical data, the multi-objective performance optimization of fused magnesium furnaces has been carried out [4]. Furthermore, historical emergency accident data have been used to determine the multi-objective optimum configuration for trauma system design optimization [5]. Additionally, based on a small quantity of experimental real-time data, the decision-makers have optimized a number of components in the hot metal manufacturing process of blast furnaces [6]. Similarly, an air intake ventilation system of a tractor, which consists of three computationally expensive objective functions, was optimized from simulation data [7]. Moreover, the decision-makers have performed multi-scenario airfoil shape optimization from historical computational fluid dynamic simulation data [8]. Ref. [9] shows that in many cases, due to physical restrictions, candidate evaluations cannot be performed during the iterations of an evolutionary algorithm (EA). As a result, we are left with no choice but to use the offline data from such simulations to address the data-driven multi-objective optimization problems. Therefore, we are eager to develop a DDMOEA-based framework that can handle various real-world optimization problems involving multiple conflicting expensive objectives from offline data alone [10]. 

Most data-driven multi-objective optimization problems (DDMOP) are resolved utilizing surrogates or meta-model-assisted evolutionary algorithms (SAEAs) because they can substitute expensive fitness functions based only on data [11,12]. Different machine learning models such as the polynomial regression [13], support vector machine [14], Gaussian process regression [15], artificial neural network [16], random forest [17], radial basis function [18], etc., have been utilized in the literature as surrogates. Multi-surrogate techniques [19] have also been employed in data-driven optimization because they can increase the SAEAs’ resilience. Because SAEAs are data-driven, they are also referred to as DDMOEAs (data-driven multi-objective evolutionary algorithms) in the literature [5]. DDMOEAs can be classified as online and offline DDMOEAs depending on the availability of new data throughout the optimization process, which is analogous to the concept of online and offline DDOPs. Additionally, both online and offline DDMOEAs put an emphasis on developing the appropriate surrogates and model management techniques for directing the evolutionary algorithm effectively toward the Pareto optimal solutions. In Section 2.3, we review some of the many model management techniques suggested for online DDMOEAs. We cannot just use the model management methods of online DDMOEAs for offline DDMOEAs because offline DDMOEAs cannot use real, expensive objective functions to evaluate potential candidates while optimizing. 

That is why offline DDMOPs pose more challenges to DDMOEAs than online DDMOPs. Some offline data-driven evolutionary algorithms have been proposed for

offline single-objective optimization. For example, in data-driven evolutionary optimization using selective surrogate ensembles (DDEA-SE) [20], a large number of weak base learners are built using the bagging ensemble approach prior to optimization, and during optimization, the algorithm adaptively selects a subset of these weak base learners to the ensemble. To create a powerful surrogate, a different algorithm known as data-driven evolutionary algorithm based on tri-training (TT-DDEA) [21] utilizes the tri-training with a high confidence data augmentation technique. Recently, we proposed a robust algorithm, namely active learning with reliability sampling-based evolutionary algorithm (ALeRSaDDEA) [3], which employs active learning and a new reliability sampling methodology to create an excellent surrogate model for single-objective offline DDOPs. A novel cooperative coevolution-based DDEA (CC-DDEA) [22] is proposed to address high-dimensional problems, optimizing on both the global and local subspace levels. Recently, a novel weighted bagging-based data-driven evolutionary algorithm with data generation based on clustering (WDDEA-DBC) [23] was proposed for single objective offline DDOPs. 

However, when using these offline data-driven EAs for MOPs, the approximation errors of the surrogate models would be amplified due to the conflict between multiple objectives [24]. Due to their design for single-objective problems, these surrogate models are not suitable for approximating multiple objectives (MOP) simultaneously, which increases the likelihood of larger errors for each objective. Furthermore, the trade-offs between objectives (e.g., the Pareto front) are sensitive to these errors, leading to compounded inaccuracies in guiding the optimization process. Therefore, offline data-driven MOEAs require high-quality surrogate models and efficient model management techniques that are specifically tailored for MOPs. Despite the accomplishments in online DDMOEAs and some advancement in offline single-objective DDEAs, very little effort has been put into developing more challenging offline DDMOEAs, with few exceptions. The data-driven multi-objective optimization based on the knowledge transfer between surrogates and the generation of final solutions (MS-RV) [25] is aided by coarse and fine surrogates. The DDMOEA utilizes a coarse surrogate to locate a potential subregion in the search area. Afterward, the knowledge gained from the coarse search concerning promising solutions is integrated into the fine search. For each objective, the performance indicator-based adaptive model selection-aided data-driven multi-objective evolutionary algorithm (IBEAMS) [26] constructs both Kriging models and RBFNs based on offline data, and when the uncertainty of the Kriging models exceeds the allowable error, it chooses the RBFNs as surrogate models. However, with IBEA-MS, the number of surrogate models grows in proportion to the number of objectives. 

This paper aims to address the following two major problems in offline data-driven multi-objective optimization when only a limited amount of historical data is accessible: 

1. Firstly, the focus is on designing surrogate models that effectively guide the search using only the available historical data. In offline data-driven optimization, building reliable surrogates is crucial because the surrogates alone guide the optimization, and no new data can be generated to update or validate them. Furthermore, the multiple labels associated with the offline data from MOPs can increase the complexity of the analysis needed to solve them. So, it may require more advanced modeling techniques and evaluation metrics to properly assess the model’s performance. 

2. Secondly, it is important to develop a model management mechanism that enhances the surrogate’s accuracy while ensuring it does not mislead the search process. This is exceptionally trickier in offline DDMOEAs, as the newly sampled data during optimization can only be evaluated by surrogates, and incorporating these approximated multi-labels might lead to cumulative approximation errors and misguide the search.

### _2.2. Surrogate Management in Data-Driven Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms_ 

The three key stages of the DDMOEA process are data gathering and surrogate construction, surrogate maintenance, and optimization. In addition to appropriate surrogate development, effective surrogate model management procedures must be employed in both offline and online DDMOEAs. However, distinct model management approaches have been developed for offline and online DDMOEAs due to the unique needs of the two issues [9]. 

Numerous model management schemes are suggested in the literature, primarily of the following two types: generation-based techniques and individual-based techniques [9,29]. The key concept in generation-based model management schemes for online DDMOEAs is to tune the sampling rate of newly-generated data depending on the actual fitness function [30]. Individual-based approaches, on the contrary, concentrate on choosing the appropriate individuals that need to be assessed in each generation [31]. The most promising individuals, those who have the greatest predicted value based on the surrogate [31], and the most uncertain individuals, for whom the surrogate model is least confident about their labels [32], have often been shown to be effective. As online DDMOEAs rely heavily on both promising and uncertain individuals, a number of selection procedures have hence been employed to achieve an appropriate balance between the two. In Bayesian optimization, these techniques are referred to as the acquisition function or the infill criterion [33]. Expected improvement (EI) [34], lower confidence bound (LCB) [35], and probability of improvement (PI) [36] are some common infill criteria.

In order to effectively solve offline DDMOPs, it uses RVEA as the baseline optimization approach and a reliable individual selection-based model management mechanism to extract meaningful insights from unlabeled data. ## **6. Conclusions** 

This paper suggests a novel adaptive model selection method with a reliable individual-based model management evolutionary algorithm to solve data-driven multiobjective optimization problems using limited offline data. Specifically, utilizing labeled offline data, AdaMoR-DDMOEA builds multi-output DNN and XGBoost regression models using the k-fold cross-validation method. In reality, the labeled dataset for each surrogate is split into k folds, with _k −_ 1 folds being utilized for training and the final fold being used for testing. To choose the best surrogate, this process is repeated k times, and the average and standard deviation of the test set MAE errors for the two surrogates are compared. The results give a clearer indication of the models’ actual generalization ability on unseen data because the models are trained and tested repeatedly on various subsets of the labeled data. When it comes to offline DDMOEAs, only surrogates can be used to evaluate the potential individuals selected from unlabeled data during optimization. For this reason, AdaMoR-DDMOEA uses a novel reliable individual selection technique to identify the most reliable individual for which the deviation in both the decision and objective spaces is minimal compared to the preferred solution. In order to enhance the limited training data, the chosen surrogate labels these trusted individuals as the predicted outputs. As a result, these trusted individuals and their labels are used to update the chosen surrogate. Lastly, the updated surrogate model predicts the labels of the offspring and directs the search to the Pareto optimal solutions. 

We have studied the empirical results of AdaMoR-DDMOEA against four state-ofthe-art offline DDMOEAs, namely IBEA-MS, MS-RV, NSGA-II-DNN, and BDDEA-LDG and three variants of AdaMoR-DDMOEA on teh ZDT and DTLZ benchmark test suites with 10 decision variables and for 2 and 3 objectives. Experimental findings show that among the compared algorithms, AdaMoR-DDMOEA demonstrates the best overall performance. Additionally, we may draw the conclusion from the component analysis that offline

DDMOEAs can benefit from adaptive model selection based on 10-fold cross-validation and reliable individual-based model updating. Finally, the study conducted on a transonic airfoil (RAE 2822) further validates the efficiency of our technique in addressing real-world offline expensive DDMOPs. 

There are several directions for future work. First, it would be interesting to combine local and global surrogates to develop a more advanced model management system for offline DDMOPs. Second, our model can be expanded to address offline data-driven optimization issues that are more challenging and include a higher number ( _≥_ 4) of conflicting objectives. Finally, few-shot learning or advanced reinforcement learning can be incorporated to solve offline DDMOPs.

Abstract: Many real-world expensive industrial and engineering multi-objective optimiza-
tion problems (MOPs) are driven by historical, experimental, or simulation data. In such
scenarios, due to the expensive cost and time required, we are only left with a small amount
of labeled data to perform the optimization. These offline data-driven MOPs are usually
solved by multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs) with the help of surrogate
models constructed from offline historical data. The key challenge in developing these
data-driven MOEAs is that they have to replace multiple conflicting fitness functions by
approximating these objective functions, which may produce cumulative approximation
errors and misguide the search. In order to build a reliable surrogate model from a small
amount of multi-output offline data and solve the DDMOPs, we have proposed an adap-
tive model selection method with a reliable individual-based model management-driven
MOEA. The proposed algorithm dynamically selects between DNN and XGBoost by com-
paring their k-fold cross-validation MAE error, which can capture the true generalization
ability of the surrogates on unseen data. Then, the selected surrogate is updated with
a reliable individual selection strategy, where the individual who is closest, both in the
decision and objective space, to the most preferred solution among labeled offline data is
chosen. As a result, these two strategies guide the underlying MOEA to the Pareto optimal
solutions. The empirical results of the ZDT and DTLZ benchmark test suite validate the use
of the three state-of-the-art offline DDMOEAs, showing that our algorithm is able to achieve
highly competitive results in terms of convergence and diversity for 2–3 objectives. Finally,
our algorithm is applied to an offline data-driven multi-objective problem—transonic airfoil
(RAE 2822) shape optimization—to validate its efficiency on real-world DDMOPs.

simulations, or censors, serve as the foundation for optimization. Using the existing data,
surrogates (also known as meta-models) may be constructed to approximately represent the
underlying objective functions associated with the phenomena. These surrogates may be
used for optimization by incorporating them into multi-objective evolutionary algorithms
(MOEAs), which have been shown to be effective in addressing black-box optimization
problems [1]. There are two types of data-driven optimization problems (DDOPs), online
and offline. When solving online data-driven optimization problems, it is feasible to collect
new data, for example, by doing more expensive function evaluations, which allow the
surrogates to be updated [2]. On the other hand, offline data-driven optimization [3] occurs
when no new data can be collected during the optimization process. If the offline DDOP
involves multiple conflicting objects (MOPs), then it is called as offline DDMOP, which is
the major emphasis of this study.
In the real world, there are a number of data-driven multi-objective optimization
problems for which only historical data is accessible. For example, using imperfect and
limited historical data, the multi-objective performance optimization of fused magnesium
furnaces has been carried out [4]. Furthermore, historical emergency accident data have
been used to determine the multi-objective optimum configuration for trauma system
design optimization [5]. Additionally, based on a small quantity of experimental real-time
data, the decision-makers have optimized a number of components in the hot metal manu-
facturing process of blast furnaces [6]. Similarly, an air intake ventilation system of a tractor,
which consists of three computationally expensive objective functions, was optimized
from simulation data [7]. Moreover, the decision-makers have performed multi-scenario
airfoil shape optimization from historical computational fluid dynamic simulation data [8].
Ref. [9] shows that in many cases, due to physical restrictions, candidate evaluations cannot
be performed during the iterations of an evolutionary algorithm (EA). As a result, we
are left with no choice but to use the offline data from such simulations to address the
data-driven multi-objective optimization problems. Therefore, we are eager to develop a
DDMOEA-based framework that can handle various real-world optimization problems
involving multiple conflicting expensive objectives from offline data alone [10].
Most data-driven multi-objective optimization problems (DDMOP) are resolved uti-
lizing surrogates or meta-model-assisted evolutionary algorithms (SAEAs) because they
can substitute expensive fitness functions based only on data [11,12]. Different machine
learning models such as the polynomial regression [13], support vector machine [14],
Gaussian process regression [15], artificial neural network [16], random forest [17], radial
basis function [18], etc., have been utilized in the literature as surrogates. Multi-surrogate
techniques [19] have also been employed in data-driven optimization because they can
increase the SAEAs’ resilience. Because SAEAs are data-driven, they are also referred to
as DDMOEAs (data-driven multi-objective evolutionary algorithms) in the literature [5].
DDMOEAs can be classified as online and offline DDMOEAs depending on the availabil-
ity of new data throughout the optimization process, which is analogous to the concept
of online and offline DDOPs. Additionally, both online and offline DDMOEAs put an
emphasis on developing the appropriate surrogates and model management techniques
for directing the evolutionary algorithm effectively toward the Pareto optimal solutions.
In Section 2.3, we review some of the many model management techniques suggested
for online DDMOEAs. We cannot just use the model management methods of online
DDMOEAs for offline DDMOEAs because offline DDMOEAs cannot use real, expensive
objective functions to evaluate potential candidates while optimizing.
That is why offline DDMOPs pose more challenges to DDMOEAs than online
DDMOPs. Some offline data-driven evolutionary algorithms have been proposed for

learners are built using the bagging ensemble approach prior to optimization, and during
optimization, the algorithm adaptively selects a subset of these weak base learners to the

weighted bagging-based data-driven evolutionary algorithm with data generation based

However, when using these offline data-driven EAs for MOPs, the approximation
errors of the surrogate models would be amplified due to the conflict between multiple
objectives [24]. Due to their design for single-objective problems, these surrogate models are
not suitable for approximating multiple objectives (MOP) simultaneously, which increases
the likelihood of larger errors for each objective. Furthermore, the trade-offs between
objectives (e.g., the Pareto front) are sensitive to these errors, leading to compounded
inaccuracies in guiding the optimization process. Therefore, offline data-driven MOEAs
require high-quality surrogate models and efficient model management techniques that
are specifically tailored for MOPs. Despite the accomplishments in online DDMOEAs and
some advancement in offline single-objective DDEAs, very little effort has been put into
developing more challenging offline DDMOEAs, with few exceptions. The data-driven
multi-objective optimization based on the knowledge transfer between surrogates and
the generation of final solutions (MS-RV) [25] is aided by coarse and fine surrogates. The
DDMOEA utilizes a coarse surrogate to locate a potential subregion in the search area.
Afterward, the knowledge gained from the coarse search concerning promising solutions
is integrated into the fine search. For each objective, the performance indicator-based
adaptive model selection-aided data-driven multi-objective evolutionary algorithm (IBEA-
MS) [26] constructs both Kriging models and RBFNs based on offline data, and when the
uncertainty of the Kriging models exceeds the allowable error, it chooses the RBFNs as
surrogate models. However, with IBEA-MS, the number of surrogate models grows in
proportion to the number of objectives.
This paper aims to address the following two major problems in offline data-driven
multi-objective optimization when only a limited amount of historical data is accessible:
1.
Firstly, the focus is on designing surrogate models that effectively guide the search
using only the available historical data. In offline data-driven optimization, building
reliable surrogates is crucial because the surrogates alone guide the optimization, and
no new data can be generated to update or validate them. Furthermore, the multiple
labels associated with the offline data from MOPs can increase the complexity of the
analysis needed to solve them. So, it may require more advanced modeling techniques
and evaluation metrics to properly assess the model’s performance.
2.
Secondly, it is important to develop a model management mechanism that enhances
the surrogate’s accuracy while ensuring it does not mislead the search process. This is
exceptionally trickier in offline DDMOEAs, as the newly sampled data during opti-
mization can only be evaluated by surrogates, and incorporating these approximated
multi-labels might lead to cumulative approximation errors and misguide the search.

2.1. Data-Driven Multi-Objective Optimization
There is no functional form or simulation model available in offline data-driven multi-
objective optimization that can be accessed during the optimization process. The results
of the simulations or experiments are the available (historical) data. The method used to
produce the offline data is referred to as the underlying objective functions. We look at the
underlying multi-objective problem, which has the following structure:

subject to X ∈S

X = (x1, x2, . . . , xd)T is the decision vector which satisfies all the constraints. In this study,
we only consider m = 2 and 3 as multi-objective problems.

If a decision vector is Pareto optimal, then the related objective vector is also Pareto
optimal. The Pareto set refers to the collection of all Pareto optimal solutions in the decision
space, while the Pareto front refers to the collection of their projections in the objective
space [28].
2.2. Surrogate Management in Data-Driven Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms
The three key stages of the DDMOEA process are data gathering and surrogate con-
struction, surrogate maintenance, and optimization. In addition to appropriate surrogate
development, effective surrogate model management procedures must be employed in
both offline and online DDMOEAs. However, distinct model management approaches
have been developed for offline and online DDMOEAs due to the unique needs of the two
issues [9].
Numerous model management schemes are suggested in the literature, primarily of the
following two types: generation-based techniques and individual-based techniques [9,29].
The key concept in generation-based model management schemes for online DDMOEAs
is to tune the sampling rate of newly-generated data depending on the actual fitness
function [30]. Individual-based approaches, on the contrary, concentrate on choosing the
appropriate individuals that need to be assessed in each generation [31]. The most promis-
ing individuals, those who have the greatest predicted value based on the surrogate [31],
and the most uncertain individuals, for whom the surrogate model is least confident about
their labels [32], have often been shown to be effective. As online DDMOEAs rely heavily
on both promising and uncertain individuals, a number of selection procedures have hence
been employed to achieve an appropriate balance between the two. In Bayesian optimiza-
tion, these techniques are referred to as the acquisition function or the infill criterion [33].
Expected improvement (EI) [34], lower confidence bound (LCB) [35], and probability of
improvement (PI) [36] are some common infill criteria.

4.3. Offline DDMOEAs for Comparison
For empirical comparisons with AdaMoR-DDMOEA, we take the following three
state-of-the-art offline DDMOEAs into consideration: IBEA-MS, MS-RV, and NSGA-II-
DNN. The methodologies of these comparison algorithms are briefly discussed in the
paragraphs below.
1.
IBEA-MS [26]: It is an offline two surrogate-assisted data-driven MOEA that adopts
IBEAϵ+ as the underlying optimization method. In particular, it employs both the
Kriging model and radial basis function network (RBFN) as surrogate models. Then, it
uses an adaptive model selection technique that selects RBFN as the surrogate model
when the uncertainty of the Kriging model exceeds the acceptable error.
2.
MS-RV [25]: It is an offline two surrogate-assisted data-driven MOEA which employs
polynomial regression as the coarse surrogate (CS) to guide the algorithm to quickly
find a promising subregion in the search space. Meanwhile, the RBFN is used as
a fine surrogate to focus on leveraging good solutions according to the knowledge
transferred from the CS. Moreover, it introduces a reference vector-based final solution
set generation strategy to obtain a solution set for the final iteration.
3.
NSGA-II-DNN [39]: It is an offline surrogate-assisted data-driven MOEA which is
developed on NSGA-II as the underlying optimization method for comparison. The
optimized deep neural network (DNN) is used as the surrogate model.

5.2. Effectiveness of Adaptive Model Selection and Reliable Individual-Based Updation Strategy
By comparing the four AdaMoR-DDMOEA variations, we analyze the efficacy of the
adaptive model selection technique and further investigate the reliable individual selection-
based model management. We examined AdaMoR-DDMOEA against two of its variations
with mono-surrogates, such as DNN and XGBoost, independently, to determine the effi-
cacy of the adaptive selection mechanism. Additionally, AdaMoR-DDMOEA(WO/MU),
a AdaMoR-DDMOEA variation without the model updation phases, has been presented.
They are thus evaluated using offline benchmark MOPs, as shown in Table 1, for 10 dimen-
sions with 2 and 3 goals, and the results are presented in Table 4.
From Table 4, we can see that, out of 19 instances, AdaMoR-DDMOEA produces
statistically significantly superior outcomes than DNN-DDMOEA, XGB-DDMOEA, and
AdaMoR-DDMOEA(WO/MU) in 5, 10, and 15 instances out of 19, respectively. We can
observe that AdaMoR-DDMOEA performs better than both of the variations that employed
either DNN or XGBoost as the mono-surrogate. This is because out of 19 cases with different
features, DNN fitted better and worse than XGBoost in 12 and 7 instances, respectively, as
AdaMoR-DDMOEA used the better-fitting model via the adaptive selection technique to
produce the best outcomes in each instance. Furthermore, AdaMoR-DDMOEA performed
better than AdaMoR-DDMOEA(WO/MU) in every benchmark issue, demonstrating the
effectiveness of reliable individual selection-based model management as a method for
enhancing the performance of the model. The primary explanation for this outcome is
that the model update schema allows the chosen surrogate to learn from unlabeled data
by compiling the most trustworthy individuals with their near-accurate predicted labels.
Hence, both strategies—adaptive surrogate selection and surrogate updating with reliable
individuals—are highly effective.

6. Conclusions
This paper suggests a novel adaptive model selection method with a reliable
individual-based model management evolutionary algorithm to solve data-driven multi-
objective optimization problems using limited offline data. Specifically, utilizing labeled
offline data, AdaMoR-DDMOEA builds multi-output DNN and XGBoost regression models
using the k-fold cross-validation method. In reality, the labeled dataset for each surrogate
is split into k folds, with k −1 folds being utilized for training and the final fold being used
for testing. To choose the best surrogate, this process is repeated k times, and the average
and standard deviation of the test set MAE errors for the two surrogates are compared.
The results give a clearer indication of the models’ actual generalization ability on unseen
data because the models are trained and tested repeatedly on various subsets of the labeled
data. When it comes to offline DDMOEAs, only surrogates can be used to evaluate the
potential individuals selected from unlabeled data during optimization. For this reason,
AdaMoR-DDMOEA uses a novel reliable individual selection technique to identify the
most reliable individual for which the deviation in both the decision and objective spaces is
minimal compared to the preferred solution. In order to enhance the limited training data,
the chosen surrogate labels these trusted individuals as the predicted outputs. As a result,
these trusted individuals and their labels are used to update the chosen surrogate. Lastly,
the updated surrogate model predicts the labels of the offspring and directs the search to
the Pareto optimal solutions.
We have studied the empirical results of AdaMoR-DDMOEA against four state-of-
the-art offline DDMOEAs, namely IBEA-MS, MS-RV, NSGA-II-DNN, and BDDEA-LDG
and three variants of AdaMoR-DDMOEA on teh ZDT and DTLZ benchmark test suites
with 10 decision variables and for 2 and 3 objectives. Experimental findings show that
among the compared algorithms, AdaMoR-DDMOEA demonstrates the best overall perfor-
mance. Additionally, we may draw the conclusion from the component analysis that offline

DDMOEAs can benefit from adaptive model selection based on 10-fold cross-validation
and reliable individual-based model updating. Finally, the study conducted on a transonic
airfoil (RAE 2822) further validates the efficiency of our technique in addressing real-world
offline expensive DDMOPs.
There are several directions for future work. First, it would be interesting to com-
bine local and global surrogates to develop a more advanced model management system
for offline DDMOPs. Second, our model can be expanded to address offline data-driven
optimization issues that are more challenging and include a higher number (≥4) of con-
flicting objectives. Finally, few-shot learning or advanced reinforcement learning can be
incorporated to solve offline DDMOPs.
