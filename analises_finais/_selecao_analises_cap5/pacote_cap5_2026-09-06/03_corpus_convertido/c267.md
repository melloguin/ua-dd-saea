# A Two-Level Model Management-Based Surrogate-Assisted Evolutionary Algorithm for Medium-Scale Expensive Multiobjective Optimization 

Yuanchao Liu , Jinliang Ding , _Senior Member, IEEE_ , Qian Li, Fei Li , _Member, IEEE_ , and Jianchang Liu 

**_Abstract_ — Medium-scale expensive multiobjective optimization problems (EMOPs) present a significant challenge to most existing surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms (SAEAs). Because the algorithms must balance convergence and diversity with a limited number of fitness evaluations (FEs), while managing the uncertainty in surrogate predictions within the medium-scale decision space. Therefore, this work proposes a surrogate-assisted multiobjective evolutionary algorithm based on two-level model management (SAMOEA-TL2M) to effectively address mediumscale EMOPs. In SAMOEA-TL2M, infill solutions are selected using the proposed two-level model management strategy. In the first level, the estimated non-dominated solutions with good shift-based density estimation (SDE) values are selected for the balance of convergence and diversity. In the second level, the estimated non-dominated solutions and high uncertainty solutions are considered. To quantify uncertainty, an inverse distance weighting (IDW) is introduced. Moreover, an accuracy rate indicator (ARI) is proposed for the optimization state assessment, providing guidance for adaptively executing the two model management levels. Extensive experiments on three widely used instances and time-varying ratio error estimation (TREE) problems with up to 120 dimensions demonstrate the superiority of SAMOEA-TL2M over five state-of-the-art SAEAs in solving medium-scale EMOPs.**

## I. INTRODUCTION 

ANY real-world optimization problems are charac- **M** terized by multiple conflicting objectives and costly fitness evaluations (FEs) [1], [2]. For example, in the operational optimization of crude oil distillation units, product profit and energy consumption are two conflicting objectives, with the performance evaluations of each unit relying on time-consuming numerical simulations [3]. Such optimization problems are termed expensive multiobjective optimization problems (EMOPs). Due to the features of EMOPs, the algorithms should find a set of well-converged and well-diversified non-dominated solutions within a limited number of FEs. 

Multiobjective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs) are capable of approximating the Pareto front (PF) in a single run when solving EMOPs [4], [5], [6]. In recent years, several types of MOEAs have been proposed, such as dominance-based MOEAs [7], [8], decomposition-based MOEAs [9], [10], and indicator-based MOEAs [11], [12], among others. However, MOEAs often require a significant number of FEs, typically in the tens of thousands. Therefore, MOEAs are not well-suited for solving EMOPs. 

To effectively deal with EMOPs, significant efforts have been made to incorporate surrogate models into the framework of MOEAs, leading to the development of surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms (SAEAs). In SAEAs, surrogate models are used to approximate the objectives or constraints of optimization problems [13], [14]. Therefore, the costly FEs can be partially replaced by computationally efficient surrogate models in the search process. As a result, SAEAs are able to achieve a set of non-dominated solutions that can well approximate the PF within a limited number of FEs [15]. 

Since ParEGO was proposed by Knowles [16], research on SAEAs for expensive multiobjective optimization has gradually gained attention. For example, Zhang et al. [17] employed the idea of decomposition to divide the EMOP into several subproblems, with each subproblem approximated by a Gaussian process (GP) model. Luo et al. [18] combined the decomposition approach with a multitask GP model to

However, most existing SAEAs mainly focus on low-scale expensive multiobjective optimization [2], [15], [29]. Their performance degenerates dramatically on medium-scale EMOPs [30]. For example, the measured sequential data typically spans a considerable length in the time-varying ratio error estimation (TREE) problems of voltage transformers [33], while FEs require the expensive and time-consuming estimation. Moreover, the optimization process for network weights and architecture involves massive decision variables in neural network architecture search, where evaluating the performance of each network is a time-consuming task [34]. Therefore, recent studies on SAEAs have made some significant efforts in solving mediumscale EMOPs, which can be broadly divided into the following three categories. 

In the first category, efforts focus on enhancing the prediction capability of surrogate models in the medium-scale decision space. For example, Guo et al. [35] utilized heterogeneous ensembles to model each objective, leveraging the superior scalability of the ensembles compared to the GPs. Lin et al. [36] proposed an ensemble surrogate model to provide more accurate predictions for each solution. A computationally efficient dropout neural network was proposed in [37], where dropout is employed during both the training and test phases. In [38], a lightweight grid classification-based surrogate was constructed to predict the grid locations for each solution. To overcome the high computational complexity of GPs in addressing medium-scale EMOPs, conditional neural processes were employed in [28]. Moreover, Wu et al. [3] used sparse GPs to replace GPs as the surrogate model. A radial basis function (RBF) inverse distance weighting 

(IDW) modeling strategy was proposed in [39], which can approximate fitness values and uncertainty information for candidate solutions in the medium-scale decision space. 

The second category aims to increase the search ability of SAEAs. Sonoda and Nakata [40] proposed to combine multiple local surrogate models and the classification-based SAEA to address medium-scale EMOPs. The idea of co-evolutionary was introduced to enhance the scalability of Bayesian evolutionary optimization algorithms in the decision space [41]. Moreover, a simplified helper task was introduced to speed up the convergence of the target task [29]. 

In the third category, dimensionality reduction techniques are introduced into SAEAs. For example, an adaptive dropout mechanism was proposed in [42], leveraging the statistical differences among various solution sets within the decision space. This mechanism can guide the selection of key decision variables, thereby facilitating the dimensionality reduction of the decision space. Additionally, a weighted optimization framework was employed to transfer a medium-scale MOP into a low-dimensional search space defined by weight variables [43]. In recent years, Si et al. [44] employed a pair of solutions to construct linear subspaces. According to the contrasts between well-converged and poorly converged solutions, an adaptive decision variable grouping was proposed to dynamically divide the decision variables into a set of groups [45]. Moreover, Pan et al. [30] reformulated an EMOP into a low-dimensional expensive single-objective optimization problem, which can benefit the convergence. 

Although recent efforts have made progress in addressing medium-scale EMOPs, some limitations still exist. First, most existing SAEAs simultaneously consider convergence, diversity, and uncertainty in one iteration [3], [35], [39]. However, due to the curse of dimensionality, approximating the true PF within a limited number of FEs poses significant challenges when solving medium-scale EMOPs. As a result, convergencediversity balancing becomes the most critical and difficult issue for medium-scale EMOPs [30]. Meanwhile, uncertainty in surrogate models should only be addressed when it hinders overall improvement in algorithm performance [45]. Therefore, the emphasis on convergence-diversity balancing or uncertainty management should be determined based on the optimization state assessment. Second, most existing SAEAs overlook the importance of assessing the optimization state, leading to a lack of guidance in adapting model management during the optimization process. Third, research on addressing medium-scale EMOPs is still in its infancy, leaving considerable room for improving the performance of existing SAEAs. 

Based on the above considerations, a novel SAEA, termed surrogate-assisted multiobjective evolutionary algorithm based on two-level model management (SAMOEA-TL2M), is proposed to effectively address medium-scale EMOPs. Compared with the existing SAEAs, the main contributions of SAMOEATL2M are summarized as follows. 

- 1) A two-level model management strategy is proposed for selecting infill solutions, which separates convergence-diversity balancing and uncertainty management into distinct levels. The first level mainly

## II. PRELIMINARIES AND MOTIVATION 

In this section, the concept of medium-scale EMOPs is first given. Then, the RBF model and the shift-based density estimation (SDE) are introduced. Lastly, the motivation of this work is presented. 

## _B. Radial Basis Function_ 

The RBF model is a commonly utilized surrogate model in SAEAs [39], [45], [49]. This is due to its high efficiency and accuracy in approximating irregular surfaces, while maintaining robust performance even when the dimensionality of problems increases. In addition, various types of kernel functions are employed within RBF models, such as linear, thin plate, cubic, multiquadratic, and Gaussian. In this work, the RBF model with the cubic kernel function is employed as the surrogate model to approximate each objective. Further details on the RBF model can be found in Section I of the supplementary materials due to the page limit. 

## _C. Shift-Based Density Estimation_ 

SDE is an effective diversity maintenance mechanism proposed in [50]. The primary idea of SDE is to shift the positions of solutions based on their relative distance from the PF. Therefore, unlike traditional density estimation methods that focus solely on the distribution of solutions within the population, SDE takes into account both the distribution and convergence properties of the solutions. 

SDE is widely used for multiobjective optimization due to its negligible computational cost, seamless integration without additional parameters, and simplicity. Moreover, the effectiveness of SDE for multiobjective optimization with a large number of decision variables has been verified in [51]. Therefore, this work employs SDE to maintain the diversity of solutions. The details of SDE can refer to Section II of the supplementary materials due to the page limit.

## _D. Motivation_ 

When solving EMOPs, convergence-diversity balancing and uncertainty management are two key challenges for SAEAs [1]. A common approach to tackle these challenges is to simultaneously consider convergence, diversity, and uncertainty in one iteration in the infill solution selection [3], [17], [23], [35], [39]. However, this method faces difficulties when addressing medium-scale EMOPs. It is particularly challenging to approximate the true PF within a limited number of FEs when the dimension of the decision space is high. Consequently, convergence-diversity balancing emerges as the most critical and difficult issue for medium-scale EMOPs [30]. Moreover, uncertainty in surrogate models should be addressed only when it hinders the overall performance improvement of the algorithms [45]. This highlights that not all types of infill solutions are necessary at different optimization states. Therefore, whether to emphasize convergence-diversity balancing or uncertainty management should depend on the optimization state assessment. 

Inspired by the hierarchical structure, where different levels focus on different tasks [52], [53], [54], [55], this work proposes a two-level surrogate model management strategy. This strategy segregates convergence-diversity balancing and uncertainty management into distinct levels. The first level focuses primarily on balancing convergence and diversity by selecting estimated non-dominated solutions with good SDE values as infill solutions. In the second level, the estimated

SAMOEA-TL2M selects infill solutions using the proposed two-level model management strategy. Furthermore, ARI is employed to guide the alternating execution of each level. Therefore, the challenges of convergence-diversity balancing and uncertainty management can be effectively tackled, enabling SAMOEA-TL2M to find a set of well-converged and well-diversified non-dominated solutions within a limited number of FEs.

## _C. Two-Level Model Management Strategy_ 

In SAEAs, convergence-diversity balancing and uncertainty management are difficult tasks for the infill solutions selection, especially when solving medium-scale EMOPs. This difficulty arises because the search space becomes larger and more complex as the dimensionality increases. Therefore, a twolevel model management strategy is proposed to select _λ_ infill solutions from _P_<sup>�</sup> _G_ . The first level focuses on the solutions with good convergence and diversity. In the second level, the uncertainty of solutions is considered. Algorithm 3 gives the main process of this strategy.

For the first concern, the roles of each level should be explicitly defined. In SAEAs, the balance of convergence and diversity is the first issue when solving medium-scale EMOPs [30]. Moreover, uncertainty in surrogate models is considered when it hinders overall performance improvement of algorithms, necessitating refinement of the models [45]. Therefore, SAEAs should first focus on balancing convergence and diversity, followed by managing the uncertainty in surrogate models. To achieve this, the proposed two-level model management strategy adopts a hierarchical approach. At the first level, infill solutions are selected with an emphasis on maintaining the balance between convergence and diversity. The second level is dedicated to addressing the uncertainty in surrogate models when current surrogate models require refinement to improve reliability. 

In SAMOEA-TL2M, the ARI is proposed as a metric for the optimization state assessment. When the ARI value is no less than a predefined threshold _α_ , it implies that the current surrogate models are reliable enough to positively contribute to the overall performance of SAMOEA-TL2M. In this scenario, SAMOEA-TL2M will conduct the first level to balance the convergence and diversity. However, when the ARI value is less than _α_ , it suggests that the surrogate models require further refinement. Thus, SAMOEA-TL2M activates the second level to select uncertainty infill solutions, thereby improving the fidelity of surrogate models. 

Regarding the second concern, two levels enhance the performance of SAMOEA-TL2M in solving medium-scale EMOPs due to the following three reasons. First, the first level selects the non-dominated solutions with good SDE values as the infill solutions. The non-dominance ensures convergence, while SDE helps maintain diversity within the medium-scale decision space [51]. This ensures an effective trade-off between convergence and diversity. Second, the introduced IDW can effectively measure the uncertainty in surrogate models for each solution, particularly in the medium-scale decision space [39]. By leveraging IDW, the second level guides the selection of infill solutions that can manage surrogate model uncertainties, thereby improving model reliability. Third, by segregating convergence-diversity balancing and uncertainty management into distinct levels, SAMOEA-TL2M ensures that each challenge is tackled in a targeted manner. To be specific, the first level facilitates convergence while maintaining diversity. The second level reinforces the accuracy of surrogate models by refining their predictions when needed, thus avoiding potential pitfalls caused by unreliable approximations. Moreover, our simulation results can further verify the contribution of each level.

EMOPs.

been made to incorporate surrogate models into the framework

evolutionary algorithms (SAEAs). In SAEAs, surrogate mod-

to achieve a set of non-dominated solutions that can well

B. Radial Basis Function
The RBF model is a commonly utilized surrogate model
in SAEAs [39], [45], [49]. This is due to its high effi-
ciency and accuracy in approximating irregular surfaces, while
maintaining robust performance even when the dimensionality
of problems increases. In addition, various types of kernel
functions are employed within RBF models, such as linear,
thin plate, cubic, multiquadratic, and Gaussian. In this work,
the RBF model with the cubic kernel function is employed
as the surrogate model to approximate each objective. Further
details on the RBF model can be found in Section I of the
supplementary materials due to the page limit.
C. Shift-Based Density Estimation
SDE is an effective diversity maintenance mechanism pro-
posed in [50]. The primary idea of SDE is to shift the
positions of solutions based on their relative distance from the
PF. Therefore, unlike traditional density estimation methods
that focus solely on the distribution of solutions within the
population, SDE takes into account both the distribution and
convergence properties of the solutions.
SDE is widely used for multiobjective optimization due
to its negligible computational cost, seamless integration
without additional parameters, and simplicity. Moreover, the
effectiveness of SDE for multiobjective optimization with a
large number of decision variables has been verified in [51].
Therefore, this work employs SDE to maintain the diversity
of solutions. The details of SDE can refer to Section II of the
supplementary materials due to the page limit.
D. Motivation
When solving EMOPs, convergence-diversity balancing
and uncertainty management are two key challenges for
SAEAs [1]. A common approach to tackle these challenges is
to simultaneously consider convergence, diversity, and uncer-
tainty in one iteration in the infill solution selection [3],
[17], [23], [35], [39]. However, this method faces difficulties
when addressing medium-scale EMOPs. It is particularly chal-
lenging to approximate the true PF within a limited number
of FEs when the dimension of the decision space is high.
Consequently, convergence-diversity balancing emerges as the
most critical and difficult issue for medium-scale EMOPs [30].
Moreover, uncertainty in surrogate models should be addressed
only when it hinders the overall performance improvement of
the algorithms [45]. This highlights that not all types of infill
solutions are necessary at different optimization states. There-
fore, whether to emphasize convergence-diversity balancing or
uncertainty management should depend on the optimization
state assessment.
Inspired by the hierarchical structure, where different levels
focus on different tasks [52], [53], [54], [55], this work
proposes a two-level surrogate model management strategy.
This strategy segregates convergence-diversity balancing and
uncertainty management into distinct levels. The first level
focuses primarily on balancing convergence and diversity by
selecting estimated non-dominated solutions with good SDE
values as infill solutions. In the second level, the estimated

tions that cannot be easily satisfied in high-dimensional
problems [39], [59]. In contrast, IDW offers a computationally
efficient alternative that is seamlessly integrated with the RBF
model and is scalable to the dimensionality of the problems
considered in this work [39].
The above two levels are alternately executed based on the
ARI value. To be specific, if the ARI value is no less than α,
SAMOEA-TL2M will conduct the first level for the balance
of convergence and diversity. However, if the ARI value is
less than α, SAMOEA-TL2M will conduct the second level
to select some uncertainty infill solutions to further refine
the surrogate models. In this work, α = 0.4 according to
the sensitivity analysis in Section III of the supplementary
materials. Moreover, the selected infill solutions are exactly
evaluated and added to DS. The exactly evaluated infill
solutions will be used to update the population, as described
in Step 4 of Section III-A.
D. Update Population
After obtaining the exactly evaluated infill solutions I, the
population Pt will be updated. To be specific, the exactly
evaluated infill solutions I and the population Pt are combined
to form a new population N P. Then, the N elite solutions in
N P are conserved for the next iteration. In this work, the
selection criterion in NSGA-II + SDE proposed in [50] is
used to select N elite solutions. The selected N elite solutions
form the population Pt+1 for the next iteration. The selection
criterion in NSGA-II + SDE can refer to Section II of the
supplementary materials.
Remark 1: In the initial stage, N solutions are randomly
selected from the exactly evaluated samples in DS to form the
initial population P0. During the search process, the exactly
evaluated infill solutions I are used to update Pt. Therefore,
all solutions in the population Pt are the exactly evaluated
solutions.

For the first concern, the roles of each level should be
explicitly defined. In SAEAs, the balance of convergence
and diversity is the first issue when solving medium-scale
EMOPs [30]. Moreover, uncertainty in surrogate models is
considered when it hinders overall performance improvement
of algorithms, necessitating refinement of the models [45].
Therefore, SAEAs should first focus on balancing convergence
and diversity, followed by managing the uncertainty in surro-
gate models. To achieve this, the proposed two-level model
management strategy adopts a hierarchical approach. At the
first level, infill solutions are selected with an emphasis on
maintaining the balance between convergence and diversity.
The second level is dedicated to addressing the uncertainty
in surrogate models when current surrogate models require
refinement to improve reliability.
In SAMOEA-TL2M, the ARI is proposed as a metric for
the optimization state assessment. When the ARI value is
no less than a predefined threshold α, it implies that the
current surrogate models are reliable enough to positively
contribute to the overall performance of SAMOEA-TL2M.
In this scenario, SAMOEA-TL2M will conduct the first level
to balance the convergence and diversity. However, when the
ARI value is less than α, it suggests that the surrogate models
require further refinement. Thus, SAMOEA-TL2M activates
the second level to select uncertainty infill solutions, thereby
improving the fidelity of surrogate models.
Regarding the second concern, two levels enhance the
performance of SAMOEA-TL2M in solving medium-scale
EMOPs due to the following three reasons. First, the first
level selects the non-dominated solutions with good SDE
values as the infill solutions. The non-dominance ensures
convergence, while SDE helps maintain diversity within the
medium-scale decision space [51]. This ensures an effec-
tive trade-off between convergence and diversity. Second,
the introduced IDW can effectively measure the uncertainty
in surrogate models for each solution, particularly in the
medium-scale decision space [39]. By leveraging IDW, the
second level guides the selection of infill solutions that
can manage surrogate model uncertainties, thereby improving
model reliability. Third, by segregating convergence-diversity
balancing and uncertainty management into distinct levels,
SAMOEA-TL2M ensures that each challenge is tackled in
a targeted manner. To be specific, the first level facili-
tates convergence while maintaining diversity. The second
level reinforces the accuracy of surrogate models by refin-
ing their predictions when needed, thus avoiding potential
pitfalls caused by unreliable approximations. Moreover, our
simulation results can further verify the contribution of each
level.

V. CONCLUSION
This work proposes SAMOEA-TL2M to effectively solve
medium-scale EMOPs. The proposed SAMOEA-TL2M can
effectively balance convergence, diversity, and model uncer-

two-level model management strategy and an ARI. To be
specific, the first level focuses on selecting the non-dominated
solutions with a good balance of convergence and diver-
sity, while the second level prioritizes solutions with high
uncertainty to refine the surrogate model. The two levels are
executed alternately based on the ARI value. Extensive exper-
iments conducted on three widely used test suites and TREE
problems demonstrate that SAMOEA-TL2M outperforms
SPGP-SAEA, EDN-ARMOEA, IR-SAEA, MO-L2SMEA, and
TP-SAEA in terms of convergence and diversity. The results
